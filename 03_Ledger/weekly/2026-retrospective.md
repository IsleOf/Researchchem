# 2026 retrospective — what actually appeared

> Counts are provisional. UNODC and EUDA year-end tallies are not out, and every figure here is a lower bound because identification lags the market by 3–9 months.

## Headline counts
- **UNODC EWA:** 1,525 substances reported cumulatively by 155 countries. In the 2025/6 reporting period, 698 substances reported, of which **110 reported for the first time** by 33 countries. ([UNODC, Aug 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/c08e6fbf-3f79-4654-8fe9-409e4b24bd59))
- **EUDA:** ~1,050 substances monitored, 50 new in 2025. EUDA notes controls in Europe *and in non-EU source countries* appear to have **reduced** the number of genuinely new substances appearing each year, especially fentanyl derivatives and synthetic cannabinoids. ([EDR 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en))
- **Pattern:** fewer brand-new scaffolds, faster substitution *within* established classes. Quantity of novelty is down; churn is up.

## By class

### Opioids — the year's main story
- **Orphines (benzimidazol-2-ones)** became a full class: nine new ones since 2024, descendants of brorphine. Cychlorphine, chlorphine, spirochlorphine. Attributed to China's mid-2025 nitazene class ban.
- **EUDA opened reviews of cychlorphine and spirochlorphine in spring 2026** — the step before formal risk assessment.
- **Tetramethylfentanyl-related substances** became the **most prevalent designer opioid class in US testing in H1 2026**, after being added to a commercial testing menu in Dec 2025 — a reminder that "most prevalent" partly tracks what labs started looking for. ([Aegis H1 2026](https://www.aegislabs.com/services/novel-psychoactive-substances/2026-mid-year-nps-summary/))
- **Nitazenes:** 25 identified in Europe to date; three-quarters of EU member states reported one in the last five years, over a third reported an orphine. Found most often in **tablets (60%)** and increasingly in **vapes and falsified medicines**. ([UNODC, Apr 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/973e194e-f8d6-4b4e-8aca-166aaec4ac7c))
- See [[depressants/nitazenes]]

### Stimulants
- **α-PVP flagged by EUDA as a growing concern** — high potency, agitation, paranoia, aggression, psychosis.
- **EU controlled 2-MMC, 4-BMC and NEP on 12 Jan 2026**, which sets up the next isomer substitution.
- **N-isopropylbutylone** replaced N,N-dimethylpentylone in the US after its Aug 2025 control.
- Volume: 48.5 t of cathinones seized/imported in the EU in 2024, up from 4.5 t in 2021.
- See [[stimulants/cathinone-churn]]

### Benzodiazepines
- **Bromazolam emergency-scheduled 18 Mar 2026**; successors (ethylbromazolam) were already in the supply. Clonazolam re-emerged as Scotland's most common street benzodiazepine. Phenazolam and desalkylgidazepam rising in the US.
- See [[depressants/designer-benzodiazepines]]

### Cannabinoids
- Semi-synthetic cannabinoids in **e-liquids and vapes** were the growth form; 27 new cannabinoids in 2025, 16 semi-synthetic.

### Adulterants (not sold as drugs)
- **Medetomidine** completed its displacement of xylazine. **BTMPS**, an industrial UV stabiliser, often exceeds the fentanyl by mass with zero human toxicity literature.
- See [[depressants/veterinary-sedative-adulterants]]

## The structural finding
Three generic class bans now exist for synthetic opioids: fentanyl-class (2019), nitazene-class (2025), orphine-class (1 Oct 2026). Each was followed by substitution to a scaffold outside the written definition. The October ban is therefore the start of a cycle, not the end of one.
