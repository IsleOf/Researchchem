# Ipamorelin — risks

## What the human safety data actually covers

The only published controlled human safety dataset is the phase 2 ileus trial: **n=117,
intravenous, up to 7 days.** In it, treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 87.5% of
the ipamorelin group vs 94.8% of placebo — no excess signal versus placebo
(PMID 25331030). That is a genuinely reassuring data point for *that* exposure, and it is
the whole of the controlled human safety evidence. It says nothing about repeated
subcutaneous use over weeks to months, which is how the compound is actually used.

A completed 320-patient phase 2 (NCT01280344) **never published results**, so any safety
information it contains is unavailable. See [[ipamorelin/human-evidence]].

## On-target effects that are also risks

- **Appetite stimulation / increased food intake** ([[risk:appetite-stimulation]]).
  GHSR-1a is the ghrelin receptor; orexigenic effect is intrinsic to the mechanism, not a
  side effect. Chronic rodent dosing increased food intake and **body-weight gain**
  (Venkova 2009; chronic young-female-rat study — see [[ipamorelin/animal-evidence]]).
  This directly undercuts fat-loss marketing: the receptor's native signal is "eat".
- **Reduced insulin sensitivity / raised fasting glucose** ([[risk:insulin-resistance]]).
  The strongest evidence is class-level: in a 2-year randomized placebo-controlled trial of
  the oral ghrelin mimetic MK-677 (25 mg/d) in healthy adults aged 60–81, fasting glucose
  rose a mean 0.3 mmol/L (5 mg/dL, p = 0.015) and insulin sensitivity decreased, with
  fat-free mass increased but intra-abdominal adiposity **not** reduced
  (Nass 2008, *Ann Intern Med*, PMID 18981485). A class review of GH secretagogues
  concluded they are generally well tolerated but identified raised blood glucose from
  reduced insulin sensitivity as the main concern
  (Sigalos & Pastuszak, *Sex Med Rev* 2018;6(1):45-53, PMID 28400207).
  *Strong class evidence; not measured for ipamorelin specifically over long exposure.*
- **Fluid retention, oedema, arthralgia, carpal-tunnel-type symptoms**
  ([[risk:water-retention]]) — standard dose-limiting effects of GH-axis stimulation.
  *Inferred from class; not quantified for ipamorelin.*
- **Transient flushing, headache, dizziness, sweating** — commonly reported with
  GHS-class peptides. *Class-level, low severity.*

## IGF-1 and the theoretical neoplasia concern

[[risk:igf1-cancer-theoretical]]. Circulating IGF-1 in the upper range is associated with
modestly increased risk of prostate cancer (OR 1.49, 95% CI 1.14–1.95, 75th vs 25th
percentile) and premenopausal breast cancer (OR 1.65, 1.26–2.08) in meta-analysis of
observational studies (Renehan 2004, *Lancet* 363:1346-53, PMID 15110491).

Three qualifications that matter and are usually dropped in both directions:
1. This is **observational epidemiology of endogenous IGF-1 in untreated people**, not
   evidence that any secretagogue causes cancer.
2. The associations are described by the authors themselves as **modest and variable by
   cancer site**.
3. **No carcinogenicity study of ipamorelin exists**, and the 2018 class review explicitly
   names long-term cancer incidence and mortality as an unresolved question for this drug
   class.

A relevant counterpoint from the ipamorelin-specific data: in Johansen 1999, 15 days of
dosing in adult rats induced longitudinal bone growth **without** affecting total IGF-1 or
IGFBPs. So the magnitude and persistence of IGF-1 elevation under real ipamorelin exposure
is not well characterized. Treat as a genuine unquantified theoretical risk — neither
dismissible nor demonstrated.

## Adverse effects flagged by FDA

In FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee review material for ipamorelin
(October 2024) and related compounding safety documentation:
- FDA stated that, based on available clinical data, ipamorelin-related substances
  **may raise safety concerns**.
- Compounded ipamorelin acetate **may pose immunogenicity risk** for certain routes of
  administration due to potential for **aggregation or peptide-related impurities**
  ([[risk:immunogenicity-impurities]]). The substance contains **unnatural amino acids**,
  adding to the complexity of peptide characterization.
- FDA noted it had **not identified safety-related information** for ipamorelin acetate via
  certain other injectable routes — i.e. absence of data, not evidence of safety.
- Secondary summaries of the FDA review list hypokalaemia, insomnia and hyperglycaemia as
  reported adverse effects, and some commercial pages assert fatal serious adverse events
  in an ipamorelin trial arm with causality not established. **These specific items could
  not be verified against FDA's own documents in this review and are recorded here as
  unverified.** The verifiable FDA positions are the four bullets above.
  Primary documents: https://www.fda.gov/media/182088/download (briefing),
  https://www.fda.gov/media/185414/download (meeting record).

## Contraindications and interactions (pharmacologically expected)

- **Active or suspected malignancy** — avoid stimulating the GH/IGF-1 axis, by analogy with
  GH therapy practice.
- **Diabetes / impaired glucose tolerance** — additive to existing dysglycaemia; may alter
  the effect of glucose-lowering therapy.
- **Uncontrolled appetite, binge-eating, or weight-gain-sensitive conditions** — the
  orexigenic effect is on-target.
- **Concurrent exogenous GH or [[cjc-1295]]** — additive on GH/IGF-1; the blend is the usual
  real-world case and has no human safety data.
- **Somatostatin analogues** — oppose the mechanism.
- **Known gastroparesis/prokinetic therapy** — additive gastric motility effects.

## Hormone-axis note
Ipamorelin does not suppress the HPG axis ([[risk:hpg-suppression]] does not apply). Its
original selling point versus non-selective GHRPs was comparatively little co-release of
ACTH, cortisol and prolactin; that selectivity was established in **rat pituitary cells**
(Raun 1998) and has not been characterized across exposures in humans. Sustained GH/IGF-1
elevation from any secretagogue produces IGF-1-mediated feedback on endogenous GH
regulation; ipamorelin's ~2 h half-life makes this far more reversible than with
CJC-1295 DAC.

## Product-quality risk
Unapproved material, and FDA's documented concern for this exact substance is
immunogenicity from aggregation and peptide-related impurities, compounded by the unnatural
amino acids making characterization harder. See [[grey-market-quality]] and
[[ipamorelin/regulatory]].
