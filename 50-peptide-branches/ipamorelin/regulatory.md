# Ipamorelin — regulatory status

## Approval
**Not approved as a medicine in any region.** Ipamorelin never completed phase 3 for any
indication. Its development programme (postoperative ileus, Helsinn) stopped after phase 2
— one published negative trial and one completed-but-unpublished trial
([[ipamorelin/human-evidence]]). There is no marketing authorization in the US, EU, UK,
Canada, Australia or Japan.

## United States — compounding (503A/503B)

Ipamorelin is **not a component of any FDA-approved drug** and there is **no applicable
USP or NF drug substance monograph** for ipamorelin free base or ipamorelin acetate
(FDA PCAC briefing, October 2024). Both facts block the ordinary statutory routes to
compounding, leaving inclusion on the **503A Bulk Drug Substances List** as the only path —
and it is not on that list.

Timeline, from FDA material:
- **Ipamorelin acetate was placed in Category 2** of FDA's interim list of bulk drug
  substances nominated for compounding — substances FDA had sufficient information to
  evaluate and judged to **raise significant safety risks**. Listing dated
  **29 September 2023**. FDA's stated concerns: potential **immunogenicity** for certain
  routes of administration due to **aggregation or peptide-related impurities**, and the
  presence of **unnatural amino acids** complicating peptide characterization. FDA also
  noted it had **not identified safety-related information** for certain other injectable
  routes — absence of data rather than evidence of safety.
- **In September 2024** FDA removed ipamorelin acetate (along with CJC-1295) from
  Category 2 of the interim list. This is a **procedural change pending advisory review,
  not an authorization** — it does not mean 503A pharmacies became free to compound it.
- FDA brought ipamorelin to the **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee on
  29 October 2024**, evaluating **both ipamorelin free base and ipamorelin acetate on
  FDA's own initiative** (FDA stated it was unclear which form the nominators intended,
  and acted because of significant safety concerns about certain peptides in compounded
  drug products). FDA's assessment was that, based on available clinical data,
  ipamorelin-related substances **may raise safety concerns**, with limited human safety
  data weighing against inclusion.

**Net position:** ipamorelin is **not** on the 503A bulks list; FDA has documented
significant safety concerns and reviewed it unfavourably. Specific PCAC vote tallies
circulating on commercial peptide sites could not be verified against FDA's own meeting
record and are deliberately **not** reproduced here. PCAC votes are advisory; rulemaking
has not concluded.

## Anti-doping — WADA

**Prohibited at all times, Section S2** (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related
Substances and Mimetics). **Ipamorelin is named explicitly** in the *growth hormone
secretagogues* sub-group of S2, alongside anamorelin, macimorelin and tabimorelin — it
appears by name in official WADA Prohibited List editions (e.g. 2015, 2020); earlier
editions grouped it with ghrelin mimetics and GH-releasing peptides such as GHRP-6 and
hexarelin. The companion GHRH-analogue sub-group names [[cjc-1295]].

Both components of the commonly sold CJC-1295 + ipamorelin blend are therefore
S2-prohibited, in and out of competition. Section numbering has shifted between editions;
check the current text at https://www.wada-ama.org/en/prohibited-list.

## Other regions
No approval anywhere. In most jurisdictions this is an unlicensed medicinal product:
possession as laboratory reference material is generally lawful, supply or marketing for
human use is not. "Research use only" labelling is a supply-side device, not a legal
category that legitimises human use.
