# Ipamorelin — mechanism

## Primary target

**Growth hormone secretagogue receptor 1a (GHSR-1a, the ghrelin receptor —
[[target:ghsr]])** — a class A GPCR signalling via Gq/PLC/IP₃ and intracellular calcium.
Ipamorelin is a synthetic ghrelin mimetic: a pentapeptide,
**Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂**, containing unnatural amino acids.

Receptor location determines its two distinct pharmacologies:
- **Pituitary somatotrophs** → GH release ([[effect:gh-release]]) and downstream IGF-1
  ([[effect:igf1-elevation]]).
- **Enteric nervous system / gastric smooth muscle** → increased gastric contractility and
  accelerated gastric emptying ([[effect:gastric-prokinesis]]), via a ghrelin-receptor
  mechanism involving cholinergic excitatory neurons. This was the basis of its actual
  clinical development programme — see [[ipamorelin/human-evidence]].

It does **not** act at the GHRH receptor; that is the target of [[cjc-1295]]
([[target:ghrhr]]). The two compounds act on separate receptors converging on the same
somatotroph, which is the mechanistic argument behind the commonly sold blend.

## Selectivity — the defining claim

Raun 1998 characterized ipamorelin as "the first selective growth hormone secretagogue".
In rat pituitary cell assays its GH-releasing potency and efficacy were similar to GHRP-6,
but its **selectivity for GH release resembled that of GHRH** rather than that of the
non-selective GHRPs. The practical meaning: unlike GHRP-6 and hexarelin, it was not
expected to co-release ACTH, cortisol and prolactin to a comparable degree.

**Evidentiary caution:** this selectivity claim is widely restated online as "no effect on
cortisol, ACTH or prolactin even at 200× the ED₅₀". That specific figure could not be
verified against the primary abstract in this review and should be treated as **unverified
secondary embellishment**. The defensible statement is the one above: selectivity
comparable to GHRH in the original rat characterization. Selectivity in rat pituitary cells
is also not the same as a demonstrated absence of cortisol/prolactin effects in humans
across exposures, for which there is no adequate published human dataset.

Ghrelin-receptor agonism is also orexigenic by its nature — appetite stimulation is an
on-target effect of this receptor, not an off-target surprise. See [[ipamorelin/risks]].

## Pharmacokinetics (human)

From the only published human PK/PD study (Gobburu 1999, intravenous administration in
healthy male volunteers, dose escalation with 8 subjects per dose level):
- **Terminal half-life ~2 h** — short-acting.
- Clearance 0.078 L/h/kg; volume of distribution at steady state 0.22 L/kg.
- Dose-proportional kinetics.
- **GH release peaked at ~0.67 h** after dosing; the concentration–GH relationship was
  described with an indirect response model.
- Inter-individual variability of the pharmacodynamic parameters was **larger** than that
  of the pharmacokinetic parameters — i.e. the GH response to a given exposure varies more
  between people than the exposure itself does.

Contrast with [[cjc-1295]] DAC, whose half-life is measured in days. Ipamorelin produces a
discrete GH pulse; CJC-1295 DAC produces a sustained elevation.
