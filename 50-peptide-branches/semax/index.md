# Semax

**What it is:** A synthetic heptapeptide, Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro: the ACTH(4-7) fragment joined to a stabilising C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro. It is often called an "ACTH(4-10) analog" and has no corticotropic (adrenal-stimulating) activity. Developed at the Institute of Molecular Genetics, RAS (Moscow). Member of [[nootropic-regulatory-peptides]].

**Status:** Registered as a medicine in Russia, mainly as intranasal drops for ischemic stroke and cognitive/cerebrovascular disorders (secondary sources; the official register was not checked directly). Not approved by FDA, EMA or other major Western regulators. In 2023 FDA placed it in 503A Category 2 (significant safety risks). See [[semax/regulatory]].

**Evidence tier: 3.** The human data are small, mostly non-randomised Russian studies from one clinical group. There is a reasonably coherent rat literature (BDNF/TrkB, ischemia transcriptomics) from the same institute. No independent replication was found.

## Key points
1. **Mechanism (rat):** Semax raises hippocampal BDNF and TrkB expression ([PMID 16996037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16996037/)). After experimental ischemia it shifts immune, vascular and neurotrophin gene expression ([PMID 24661604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24661604/)).
2. **Human stroke data are weak:** a 1997 non-randomised study of 30 treated patients against 80 conventionally treated controls ([PMID 11517472](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11517472/)). A 2018 rehabilitation study of n=110 linked Semax to higher plasma BDNF ([PMID 29798983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29798983/)).
3. **No credible evidence for nootropic enhancement in healthy people.** Only small EEG/fMRI studies exist (e.g., n=24 fMRI, [PMID 30225715](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30225715/)).
