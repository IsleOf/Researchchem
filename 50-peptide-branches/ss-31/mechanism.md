# SS-31 — mechanism (L2)

Back to [[ss-31/index]].

## Target
- **Cardiolipin** (`target:cardiolipin`), the anionic phospholipid unique to the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) and required for cristae formation and for supercomplex assembly of the electron transport chain. SS-31 binds cardiolipin via electrostatic (cationic residues) and hydrophobic (aromatic Dmt/Phe) interactions (Szeto 2014, Birk 2013).
- Not a classical receptor agonist; uptake is reported to be energy- and potential-independent, with ~1000–5000-fold IMM accumulation in preclinical work (review data; preclinical).

## Pathway / proposed effects
1. Cardiolipin binding prevents cardiolipin from converting **cytochrome c into a peroxidase**, while preserving its electron-carrier role → less cardiolipin peroxidation.
2. Stabilises **cristae architecture** → improved oxidative phosphorylation / ATP output, reduced electron leak and mitochondrial ROS (`effect:mitochondrial-bioenergetics`, `effect:reduced-mito-ros`).
3. In aged mouse cardiomyocytes, normalised proton leak and reduced mitochondrial ROS (Chiao 2020).
4. Barth syndrome (TAZ gene defect) features abnormal cardiolipin remodelling (monolysocardiolipin accumulation) — providing the mechanistic rationale for the lead indication.

Caveat: much of the mechanistic literature comes from the inventor's lab and the sponsor; independent replication of the precise binding model is limited.

## Pharmacokinetics (non-dosing)
- Peptide, given parenterally (subcutaneous in chronic trials; IV infusion in acute cardiac trials).
- After a 4-h IV infusion in HFrEF, plasma levels peaked at end of infusion and were undetectable by 24 h (Daubert 2017) — short plasma half-life, with the proposed action residing in tissue mitochondria.
- Renal elimination relevant: the US label notes insufficient information for eGFR <30 mL/min or dialysis.
