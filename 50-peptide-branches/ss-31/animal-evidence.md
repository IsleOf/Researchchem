# SS-31 — animal & cell evidence (L2)

Back to [[ss-31/index]].

| Study | Species / model | Outcome | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brines-independent: Szeto lab body of work (reviewed in Szeto 2014) | Rodent ischemia–reperfusion (heart, kidney, brain), isolated mitochondria | Reduced cardiolipin peroxidation, preserved cristae, improved ATP recovery after ischemia | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3976620 |
| Birk et al. 2013 (J Am Soc Nephrol) | Rat renal ischemia; mitochondrial preparations | SS-31 binds cardiolipin, "re-energises" ischemic mitochondria | via Szeto 2014 review (not independently verified) |
| Chiao et al. 2020 (eLife) | Old mice, 8 weeks treatment | Substantially reversed age-related diastolic dysfunction; normalised proton leak, reduced mito-ROS; no additive benefit in mCAT mice (shared oxidative mechanism) | https://elifesciences.org/articles/55513 |
| Campbell et al. 2019 (Free Radic Biol Med 134:268) | Aged mice | Reported reversal of age-related redox stress and improved exercise tolerance (title-level only; not verified in detail) | cited in Chiao 2020 |

Notes:
- Animal benefits are strongest in acute ischemia and aging models; translation to acute MI (EMBRACE STEMI) failed.
- Aging-mouse data are often cited online as evidence of human "longevity" effects — this is an extrapolation (see [[ss-31/claims-vs-evidence]]).
