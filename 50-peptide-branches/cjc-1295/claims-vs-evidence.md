# CJC-1295 — claims vs evidence

| Marketing claim | What the evidence shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Raises GH and IGF-1" | Correct, and well documented for the DAC form. GH up ~2–10×, IGF-1 up ~1.5–3× after a single dose, sustained days (Teichman 2006, PMID 16352683; Ionescu 2006, PMID 17018654). | **Supported** |
| "More physiological than GH injections because it preserves natural pulses" | Pulse frequency and amplitude were unchanged while trough GH rose ~7.5× (Ionescu 2006). The mechanism is upstream of the pituitary, so somatostatin feedback is retained. But "preserves pulsatility" is a pharmacodynamic observation; no study shows it translates into a better safety or outcome profile than GH. | **Partly supported — mechanism yes, benefit unproven** |
| "Builds muscle / cuts fat / recomposition" | **No human trial of CJC-1295 has ever reported a body-composition endpoint.** The only trial that could have (NCT00267527, in visceral obesity) was terminated and published nothing. Inference from GH biology is not evidence for this compound. | **Unsupported** |
| "Improves sleep, recovery, injury healing, skin, energy" | No human study of CJC-1295 measured any of these. Zero data. | **Unsupported** |
| "Anti-ageing" | No study of any duration, in any species, on ageing, function, or mortality. | **Unsupported** |
| "Clinically tested and proven safe" | Two short pharmacodynamic trials in healthy adults reported no serious adverse reactions. Against that: the phase 2 programme was halted after a participant death with causality never established, and FDA has documented serious adverse events including increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction with limited clinical data. "Tested" yes; "proven safe" no. | **Misleading** |
| "FDA-reviewed" / "compliant compounded peptide" | FDA reviewed it and **proposed it not be added** to the 503A bulks list (PCAC, Dec 2024). Prior Category 2 placement meant *significant safety risks*; its 2024 removal from Category 2 was because nominations were withdrawn, not a clearance. | **Misleading — inverts the finding** |
| "CJC-1295 and Mod GRF (1-29) are the same thing" | They are not. The DAC linker that binds albumin is what produces the multi-day half-life; without it, the molecule is short-acting. Human data exist only for the DAC form. | **False** |
| "Half-life about a week, so infrequent dosing" (as a convenience claim) | The ~5.8–8.1 d half-life is real for the DAC form. Framed as a benefit it omits the corollary: an excessive IGF-1 excursion cannot be withdrawn for over a week. | **Technically true, materially incomplete** |
| "Stacks synergistically with ipamorelin" | Combining a GHRH-receptor agonist with a ghrelin-receptor agonist is a coherent mechanistic hypothesis (two pathways, two receptors). **No published human trial has tested the combination** for any endpoint, including whether the GH rise is additive. | **Hypothesis only** |
| "Safe for athletes / undetectable" | Explicitly named in WADA S2, prohibited in and out of competition. | **False** |

## Pattern
The evidence base for CJC-1295 establishes that it does what its mechanism predicts to a
biomarker (GH, IGF-1) and stops there. Every claim about an *outcome a person would want*
is an extrapolation from that biomarker, across a development programme that was abandoned
after a death. The gap between "raises IGF-1" and "builds muscle safely" is the whole of
the missing evidence.
