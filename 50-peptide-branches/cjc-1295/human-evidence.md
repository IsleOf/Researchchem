# CJC-1295 — human evidence

All published human data concern the **DAC (albumin-binding)** form. There is no published
human trial of "CJC-1295 without DAC" / Mod GRF (1-29) under that name.

## Teichman 2006 — sustained GH and IGF-1 elevation
- **Design:** two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled ascending-dose trials,
  28 d and 49 d duration.
- **Population:** healthy adults, ages 21–61.
- **Outcome:** single subcutaneous doses raised GH ~2–10 fold for ≥6 days and IGF-1
  ~1.5–3 fold for 9–11 days. Estimated half-life 5.8–8.1 d. Dose-dependent and sustained.
  Reported as generally well tolerated at the doses studied; no serious adverse reactions
  reported in the publication.
- **What it does not show:** no body-composition endpoint, no strength, sleep, recovery,
  injury-healing, or clinical outcome. This is a pharmacodynamic/safety study.
- Teichman SL, Ball SA, Garceau M, Castaigne JP, Frohman LA. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*
  2006;91(3):799-805. PMID 16352683. doi:10.1210/jc.2005-1536

## Ionescu & Frohman 2006 — pulsatility preserved
- **Design:** open-label pharmacodynamic study; GH profiling before and ~1 week after a
  single injection.
- **Population:** healthy men, ages 20–40 (small n, single-digit to low teens).
- **Outcome:** GH pulse frequency and pulse amplitude were unchanged; **trough GH rose
  ~7.5-fold**; mean GH and IGF-1 rose. Notably, the IGF-1 increase **did not correlate**
  with any measured parameter of GH secretion.
- **Interpretation:** supports the "preserves pulsatility" mechanistic claim. The absent
  GH–IGF-1 correlation weakens any attempt to titrate or predict IGF-1 exposure from GH data.
- Ionescu M, Frohman LA. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2006;91(12):4792-7. PMID 17018654.
  doi:10.1210/jc.2006-1702

## NCT00267527 — phase 2 in HIV-associated visceral obesity (terminated)
- **Design:** ConjuChem phase 2, CJC-1295 in HIV patients with visceral obesity.
  Registry start December 2005; listed as **terminated**, completion date September 2006.
- **Outcome:** no efficacy results published. The study was stopped in July 2006 following
  the death of a participant at a site in Argentina. Causality was never publicly
  established and the programme was not resumed. See [[cjc-1295/risks]] for the detail and
  the limits of what is verifiable.
- Registry: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00267527

## Evidence gaps (explicit)
- **No body-composition data in humans.** No published human trial of CJC-1295 reports
  lean mass, fat mass, or anthropometric outcomes.
- **No trial of the CJC-1295 + [[ipamorelin]] combination**, despite it being the commonest
  way the compound is sold.
- **No long-term safety data.** Maximum published exposure is weeks; the terminated phase 2
  is the only longer-exposure study and it reported nothing.
- No published human data on the no-DAC form.
