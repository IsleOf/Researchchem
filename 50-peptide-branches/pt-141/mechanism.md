# PT-141 — mechanism (L2)

Back to [[pt-141/index]].

## Targets
- **MC4R** (`target:mc4r`) — principal target for the pro-sexual effect; hypothalamic (incl. medial preoptic area) melanocortin signalling is thought to modulate dopaminergic desire/reward circuits (`effect:increased-sexual-desire`).
- **MC3R** (`target:mc3r`) — also agonised; contribution to sexual effects less defined.
- **MC1R** (`target:mc1r`) — on melanocytes; agonism → melanin production → **focal hyperpigmentation** (`risk:hyperpigmentation`).
- Non-selective across melanocortin receptors (MC2R excepted per general melanocortin pharmacology).

## Pathway notes
- Acts centrally, unlike PDE5 inhibitors (which act on genital blood flow). In rats it increased **proceptive/appetitive** behaviour (solicitation) without changing lordosis or general locomotion — supporting a desire-specific (not reflexive arousal) effect (Pfaus 2004).
- Melanocortin activation also produces **transient BP increase / HR decrease** and **nausea** (central emetic pathways) — class effects shared with Melanotan II.

## Pharmacokinetics (non-dosing)
- Peptide, SC injection; label reports peak concentrations about 1 h after injection and a mean terminal half-life of ~2.7 h (per Vyleesi prescribing information).
- May slow gastric emptying → can alter absorption of co-administered oral drugs (label).

## Relation to Melanotan II
- Melanotan II (MT-II) was developed at the University of Arizona as a tanning agent; Palatin licensed it, noticed sexual side effects, and developed bremelanotide, described as an MT-II metabolite lacking the C-terminal amide. MT-II itself was never approved and is sold illegally as a "tanning" injectable.
