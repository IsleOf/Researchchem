# ARA-290 — mechanism (L2)

Back to [[ara-290/index]].

## Targets
- **Innate repair receptor (IRR)** — a proposed heteromer of the EPO receptor (`target:epor`) and the β-common receptor CD131 (`target:cd131`, shared with IL-3/IL-5/GM-CSF receptors). Expressed/up-regulated in injured or inflamed tissue.
- Does **not** meaningfully activate the classic (EPOR)2 homodimer that drives erythropoiesis → non-erythropoietic (`effect:non-erythropoietic`). Brines 2008: neither the helix-B peptide nor the 11-mer stimulated erythropoiesis in vitro or in vivo.

## Pathways / effects
- Anti-inflammatory and tissue-protective signalling (`effect:tissue-protection`, `effect:anti-inflammatory`); EMA describes it as controlling inflammatory processes via the IRR.
- In rodent nerve injury, suppresses spinal microglial response and produces long-lasting reduction in allodynia (`effect:analgesic-neuropathic`).
- Proposed small-nerve-fibre regeneration (corneal and intra-epidermal GAP-43+ fibres) (`effect:nerve-fibre-regeneration`) — human evidence is surrogate-based.
- Evidence for receptor requirement: β-common receptor knockout mice lose ARA-290's anti-allodynic effect (Swartjes 2011).

Caveat: the IRR concept is largely the work of one research network (Brines/Cerami and collaborators); the exact receptor composition remains debated in the wider literature.

## Pharmacokinetics (non-dosing)
- Plasma half-life is described in secondary sources as very short (minutes; not verified against a primary PK paper here), yet effects reportedly persist after dosing stops — interpreted as triggering downstream repair programmes. Treat this as an author interpretation.
- Given IV (early sarcoidosis trial) or SC (later trials).
