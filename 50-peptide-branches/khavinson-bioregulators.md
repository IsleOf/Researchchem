# Khavinson bioregulators (Russian short-peptide "bioregulators")

Short peptides (2–4 residues) developed since the 1980s–2000s by V. Khavinson and colleagues. The work came from the St Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology, with collaborators including V. Anisimov at the Petrov Institute of Oncology. Many are synthetic analogues of tissue extracts ("cytomedins"), such as Epithalamin from the pineal gland and Thymalin from the thymus.

## Core hypothesis
- Tissue-specific short peptides enter the nucleus and bind histones or DNA promoter regions. In that way they "epigenetically" restore gene expression in ageing tissue.
- The developer group's own systematic review states that very few studies exist in this area ([PMID 34834147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34834147/)).
- The hypothesis is **not independently established**.

## Family-wide quality concerns
1. **Single-source literature:** Almost all studies come from one research network. Many are in Russian-language journals, in *Neuro Endocrinology Letters* or *Bull Exp Biol Med*, or in group-affiliated venues, with limited independent peer review.
2. **Extract vs peptide conflation:** Human outcome claims (e.g. mortality reductions) often refer to crude extracts, not the synthetic peptides sold today.
3. **No preregistered RCTs, PK or toxicology:** None were found for any member.
4. **Grey-market supply:** Product identity and sterility are unverified.
5. **WADA:** All members are likely prohibited under S0 (non-approved substances).
