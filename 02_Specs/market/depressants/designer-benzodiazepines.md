# Designer benzodiazepines (2025–26)

**Area:** [[depressants/index]] · **Compound nodes:** [[bromazolam]] · [[clonazolam]]

## Summary
Bromazolam was the defining designer benzodiazepine of 2022–25 and was finally emergency-scheduled in the US in March 2026. Two successors are now visible: **ethylbromazolam** (first seen by Toronto's drug-checking service in late 2024, now in Canada, UK and Australia) and a **re-emergence of clonazolam**, which Public Health Scotland's RADAR reports has become Scotland's most commonly detected street benzodiazepine. Throughout, the delivery format is the same: tablets pressed to look like pharmaceutical alprazolam.

## 1. Bromazolam: dominance and scheduling
- **DEA temporarily placed bromazolam in Schedule I effective 16 March 2026**, by temporary order published the same day and running to **16 March 2028** ([FR **2026-05064**](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/16/2026-05064/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-bromazolam-in-schedule-i), Docket DEA-1420). DEA's press release is dated 18 March 2026 ([DEA](https://www.dea.gov/press-releases/2026/03/18/dea-emergency-schedules-bromazolam)) — **use 16 Mar 2026 as the effective date; 18 Mar is the press-release date.** A correcting amendment fixed an invalid chemical name in the original order, effective 24 Jun 2026 and applicable from 16 Mar 2026 ([FR 2026-12653](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12653/schedules-of-controlled-substances-placement-of-bromazolam-in-schedule-i-correction)). **Verified 2026-11-09.**
- The Federal Register notice describes bromazolam as **currently one of the most commonly identified benzodiazepines in drug seizures**, encountered in **all 50 states**, with at least **259 overdose cases from April 2021 through February 2026**.
- The same notice states bromazolam is **likely under-reported** because laboratories are still obtaining reference standards — the same reference-standard lag that affects [[nitazenes]].
- **Caveat on "dominance":** seizure frequency is not market share. No source found gives quantitative market-share data, and DEA itself flags under-reporting, so prevalence estimates are soft.

## 2. What is succeeding it
- **Ethylbromazolam.** First detected by **Toronto's Drug Checking Service on 8 November 2024**; subsequently reported by drug-checking services in **Canada, the UK and Australia**. In vitro **GABA-A activity is similar to bromazolam**, so comparable harm potential is expected ([Arch Toxicol 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00204-026-04433-9); [PMID 42159737](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42159737/)).
  - Note the drug-checking-first detection pattern: harm-reduction services found this a year or more before it appeared in regulatory documents. See [[drug-checking]].
- **Ethylflualprazolam.** Found in a counterfeit blue "**B707**" tablet imprinted as 2 mg alprazolam (2026 US report; secondary source — **treat as a single reported finding, not a trend**).
- **Clobromazolam (phenazolam).** Named in the FDA/ECDD comment notice of 26 Aug 2026 ([FR 2026-17380](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17380/international-drug-scheduling-single-convention-on-narcotic-drugs-convention-on-psychotropic)); a triazolobenzodiazepine first synthesised in the 1980s, now distributed in powders, capsules and pressed tablets **misrepresented as alprazolam**. ⚠️ **Note:** clobromazolam was **not** on the 49th ECDD's review slate (19–22 Oct 2026) — of the eight substances in that notice, only cychlorphine, desalkylgidazepam, ethylbromazolam, etomethazene and spirochlorphine received critical reviews, and etomidate and medetomidine pre-reviews. So the comment notice is not by itself evidence that a review is under way for this substance.
- **Flubromazolam** became **permanently US Schedule I effective 1 April 2026** — **VERIFIED 2026-11-09** against the Federal Register: final rule published 2 Mar 2026, Docket DEA-989, placing **clonazolam, diclazepam, etizolam, flualprazolam and flubromazolam** in Schedule I ([FR **2026-04112**](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/02/2026-04112/schedules-of-controlled-substances-placement-of-clonazolam-diclazepam-etizolam-flualprazolam-and)). All five had been temporarily scheduled from 26 July 2023, extended to 26 July 2026 ([FR 2025-14037](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/25/2025-14037/schedules-of-controlled-substances-extension-of-temporary-placement-of-clonazolam-diclazepam)), then made permanent. The rule states the action "also enables the United States to meet its obligations under the **1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances**" — so these five are internationally controlled, not merely US-scheduled.

## 3. Clonazolam re-emergence (Scotland)
- **Public Health Scotland RADAR** issued alerts in **February 2026** following reports of increased harm from changes in Scotland's drug supply, flagging **clonazolam as re-emerging** and specifically high-potency ([PHS news, Feb 2026](https://publichealthscotland.scot/news/2026/february/phs-urges-caution-following-reports-of-increased-harm-from-changes-in-scotland-s-drug-supply/)).
- The **RADAR quarterly report (January 2026)** reportedly found **clonazolam is now the most common street benzodiazepine in Scotland**, and warned of overdose and death risk **if ethylbromazolam and clonazolam establish themselves** in that market ([Drink and Drugs News](https://www.drinkanddrugsnews.com/clonazolam-now-scotlands-most-common-street-benzo/)).
- Scotland's supply is also being hit by [[medetomidine]], which RADAR notes is **likely to be consumed unknowingly** and has been **most commonly detected in drugs sold as heroin and as benzodiazepines** — i.e. a benzodiazepine-shaped tablet can now carry an alpha-2 agonist. See [[depressants/veterinary-sedative-adulterants]].
- Clonazolam's re-emergence matters because it is one of the higher-potency triazolobenzodiazepines; the practical consequence is that a tablet's **appearance carries no information about potency**.

## 4. Counterfeit alprazolam tablets
- NSW Health surveillance: **only 8% of counterfeit "alprazolam" samples since 2019 actually contained alprazolam**, and **fourteen novel benzodiazepines** have been detected in counterfeit alprazolam in that jurisdiction ([NSW Health drug warnings](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/sabs/Documents/2025-si-013.PDF)).
- This is the single most useful number in this note: a pressed "Xanax" tablet is better modelled as an unknown GABA-A agonist of unknown potency than as alprazolam.
- Historical UK/EU context: counterfeit benzodiazepine tablets have carried **etizolam, flualprazolam and flubromazolam**, sometimes at doses far above the labelled product ([The Conversation, UK fake benzodiazepines](https://theconversation.com/rise-in-fake-benzodiazepines-in-the-uk-may-put-people-at-risk-of-serious-harm-143874)).

## 5. Etizolam and flualprazolam status
- Both were central to the 2019–2022 UK/Scottish "street benzo" wave; both have since been scheduled in multiple jurisdictions and have largely been displaced in reporting by bromazolam and now clonazolam/ethylbromazolam.
- **US status RESOLVED 2026-11-09:** **etizolam and flualprazolam are both permanently US Schedule I, effective 1 April 2026**, in the same final rule as flubromazolam, clonazolam and diclazepam ([FR **2026-04112**](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/02/2026-04112/schedules-of-controlled-substances-placement-of-clonazolam-diclazepam-etizolam-flualprazolam-and), Docket DEA-989). The rule covers each substance "and their salts, isomers, and salts of isomers". It is also an implementation of the **1971 Convention**, so their international control is not in doubt either.
- **Outside the US, status remains jurisdiction-specific and not verified here.** Etizolam remains a licensed medicine in some countries (e.g. Japan, India, Italy), which complicates "NPS" framing and means a US Schedule I listing says nothing about its availability as a prescription product elsewhere.

## 6. Scale of the class
- EUDA: **40 new benzodiazepines notified to the EU Early Warning System between 2007 and 2025**, of which **28 were present on the market in 2024** across 22 Member States and Norway ([EUDA EDR 2026, NPS](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- EUDA's benzodiazepine market analysis: [EU Drug Market — NPS, benzodiazepines](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/eu-drug-markets/new-psychoactive-substances/distribution-and-supply/benzodiazepines_en).

## 7. Withdrawal and seizure risk
- Designer benzodiazepines produce **physical dependence like licensed benzodiazepines**, but three features worsen it:
  1. **Unknown and variable potency per tablet** means people can be far more dependent than they believe.
  2. **Long-acting metabolites** of some analogues prolong both intoxication and the withdrawal tail.
  3. **Abrupt cessation risks a withdrawal syndrome that includes seizures and delirium, which can be fatal.** This is the standard, well-established benzodiazepine withdrawal hazard and applies fully to the designer analogues.
- Published guidance treats this as requiring **medically supervised, gradual dose reduction** rather than abrupt discontinuation. No tapering schedules are given here — that is an individualised clinical decision.
- The dependence risk is compounded when designer benzodiazepines are being consumed **unknowingly** (counterfeit tablets, or benzodiazepines present in a sample sold as something else), because there is no intentional reduction plan at all.

## 8. Why naloxone does not reverse them
- Naloxone is a **competitive antagonist at opioid receptors**. Benzodiazepines act as **positive allosteric modulators at GABA-A receptors** — a completely different receptor and mechanism. There is no pharmacological route by which naloxone affects them.
- EUDA states this plainly: overdoses from new synthetic opioids can be reversed with naloxone, but **benzodiazepine-driven overdose requires a different antidote that is generally only administered in medical settings and may require hospitalisation** ([EUDA EDR 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- The practical consequence in a mixed exposure (the common case): **naloxone can reverse the opioid component and leave substantial sedation and respiratory compromise behind.** A partial response to naloxone should not be read as "not an opioid overdose" or as "the emergency is over". See [[depressants/risks]] and [[polydrug-risk]].
- Flumazenil, the benzodiazepine antagonist, is **not** a field or bystander intervention and carries its own seizure risk in dependent or mixed-overdose patients; its use is a narrow in-hospital decision.

## Successor detections, H1 2026 (added 2026-11-09)
Single-lab/service shares — not national prevalence.
- **Phenazolam** (= clobromazolam): bromazolam + phenazolam together >70% of designer-benzo detections in H1 2026; bromazolam's share falling, phenazolam's holding. **Still not specifically scheduled federally in the US as of 2026-11-09 — checked 2026-11-09**: a search of the Federal Register for "clobromazolam" returns exactly one document, the FDA/ECDD comment notice (FR 2026-17380), and no DEA scheduling action, temporary or permanent. (This speaks only to a *specific listing*; it does not rule out treatment as a controlled substance analogue under 21 U.S.C. 802(32), which this repo has not assessed.)
- **Ethylbromazolam:** ~6% (Q1) → ~8% (Q2) 2026; now the most prevalent designer benzodiazepine in UK WEDINOS data. In-vitro GABA-A activity similar to bromazolam → similar expected effect and harm ([PMID 42159737](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42159737/)).
- **Desalkylgidazepam** (incl. metabolite): <2% (Q1) → >4% (Q2) 2026. Toronto drug checking detected it more often than ethylbromazolam Nov 2024–Sep 2025.
- **Flubromazolam, etizolam, flualprazolam, clonazolam, diclazepam:** all permanently US Schedule I from 1 Apr 2026 (FR 2026-04112) — verified.
