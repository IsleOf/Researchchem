# Depressants, sedatives and opioid NPS — market and health overview

**Category:** [[depressants]] · **Back:** [[MOC]]

## What this area covers
The highest-mortality NPS group: ultra-potent synthetic opioids (nitazenes and the new orphines), designer benzodiazepines, non-opioid veterinary sedatives used as adulterants, concentrated kratom derivatives, and the miscellaneous retail and prescription depressants that stack on top of all of it.

## What changed in 2025–26 — the short version
1. **A new ultra-potent opioid class arrived.** The **orphines** (benzimidazol-2-ones: cychlorphine, chlorphine, spirochlorphine — relatives of brorphine) appear to be replacing nitazenes in US and European markets, widely attributed to China's mid-2025 class-wide nitazene controls. The EU notified **three nitazenes and three orphines** in 2025; orphines have been detected across **22 US states** by late 2026.
2. **Medetomidine finished displacing xylazine** in the Northeast corridor — **90% of Philadelphia Q1 2026 fentanyl-primary samples** — and brought with it a **distinct, severe withdrawal syndrome** refractory to opioid withdrawal treatment, with **77.5% ICU admission** in a 209-patient series.
3. **Bromazolam was finally emergency-scheduled** in the US (18 March 2026) just as **ethylbromazolam** emerged and **clonazolam re-established itself as Scotland's most common street benzodiazepine**.
4. **7-OH scheduling went into a loop.** FDA recommended Schedule I in July 2025; DEA issued, withdrew and reissued a notice of intent through 2026. As of 2026-11-08 **no order for 7-OH itself is in effect**.
5. **The detection gap became the dominant structural risk.** Nitazenes, orphines, medetomidine, most novel benzodiazepines and BTMPS are all absent from routine clinical toxicology, so prevalence and mortality numbers across this entire area are lower bounds of unknown tightness.

## The one-line version
**Naloxone still works on the opioids, which are getting more potent and more varied; it does nothing about the alpha-2 agonists, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids and industrial additives that now sit alongside them — and almost none of those appear on a routine toxicology screen.**

## Scope limits
This area covers monitoring, forensic, toxicology and regulatory literature only. No synthesis routes, precursors, vendors or sourcing. No dosing protocols — clinical naloxone and withdrawal management is described only at the level of published general guidance.
