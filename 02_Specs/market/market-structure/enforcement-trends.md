# Enforcement and interdiction trends

Up: [[market-structure/index]]

**One-line:** Published seizure data show a market that is **diversifying in substance count while concentrating in volume**, and that has moved from bulk freight toward small-parcel flows for NPS and peptides — which makes seizure counts a measure of inspection effort as much as of supply.

## Reading seizure statistics at all
Before any number below is used:
- **Counts vs quantities diverge.** In the EU in 2024, **five substances accounted for ~90% of the NPS quantity** seized by law enforcement, four of them cathinones — while ~400 distinct NPS appeared in seizures ([European Drug Report 2026 – NPS](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- **Parcel seizures scale with screening capacity.** A rise can mean more inspection, a single targeting operation, or more supply. Monitoring bodies state this caveat; secondary reporting usually drops it.
- **Category migration breaks series.** Scheduled cathinones left the NPS count for the amphetamine-type stimulant count (UNODC), producing an artefactual decline.
- **Potency inverts the meaning of mass.** For nitazenes, a small mass figure can represent a very large number of doses, so kilogram trends and dose-equivalent trends can point different ways.

## EU: synthetic opioids
From the EU Early Warning System, as reported in the European Drug Report 2026:
- **2024: 35.5 kg across 1,063 seizures**, up from **22 kg in 2023**.
- Most common substances in those seizures: **metonitazene, carfentanil, tramadol, protonitazene**.
- Geographic concentration: **Germany, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania** accounted for most seizures.
- **Seven new synthetic opioids** notified to the EWS in 2025; **50 NPS** detected in Europe for the first time in 2025 (total monitored 1,050).

## EU: NPS overall
- Europol/EMCDDA reported **more than 30.7 tonnes of NPS seized in Europe in 2022**, dominated by a few very large cathinone and ketamine seizures ([EUDA, 2024 analysis](https://www.euda.europa.eu/news/2024/new-analysis-reveals-highly-dynamic-and-resilient-market-new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- The same analysis records a **partial production shift from China to India**.
- **No Europol EU Drug Markets Report for 2025 or 2026 was located.** The most recent full joint market analysis found is *EU Drug Markets Analysis 2024* ([PDF](https://www.europol.europa.eu/cms/sites/default/files/documents/EU%20Drug%20Markets%20Analysis%202024.pdf)). Current EU market intelligence is carried by the EUDA's annual report rather than a standalone Europol market report — **treat "Europol 2025/2026 threat assessment" claims as unverified.**
- Parcel flows: Europol-sourced information in the EUDA opioids chapter indicates fentanyl moving into the EU in parcels. The chapter's quantitative series covers **2018–2021**, showing a **drop of just over 60% in fentanyl-derivative seizures** over that window ([EUDA heroin and other opioids — trafficking and supply](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/eu-drug-markets/heroin-and-other-opioids/trafficking-and-supply_en)). **No parcel-specific EU figures for 2025–26 were found.**

## EU: counterfeit medicines
- EU EWS reports of **counterfeit medicines containing nitazenes are growing** (EDR 2026). A secondary report of the same document gives counterfeit nitazene tablets rising from **380 (2022) to >50,000 (2024)** — **unverified against the EUDA text**.

## US: DEA threat assessment
From the [2025 National Drug Threat Assessment](https://www.dea.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/2025NationalDrugThreatAssessment.pdf) and DEA reporting:
- **Top five nitazenes encountered in 2024:** metonitazene, protonitazene, N-pyrrolidino etonitazene, N-desethyl isotonitazene, isotonitazene.
- Nitazenes appear in counterfeit tablets (e.g. presented as M-30 oxycodone) and in polydrug mixtures, typically **without the consumer's knowledge**.
- **22 unique nitazenes identified since 2020, 21 of them Schedule I** ([DEA public safety advisory, 12 May 2026](https://www.dea.gov/press-releases/2026/05/12/public-safety-advisory)).
- DEA's own explanation of the pattern is scheduling-driven substitution: new variants emerge as pressure builds on existing ones. See [[market-structure/scheduling-churn]].
- **Proportionality:** a DEA state/territory report (Sept 2025) states the current magnitude of the nitazene threat is **relatively small compared with fentanyl**, which dominates the US opioid market.
- **Published critique:** *Filter* argues the 2025 NDTA's nitazene section overstates nitazenes as a free-standing market presence rather than an adulterant in fentanyl, and notes the absence of cited support ([Filter](https://filtermag.org/dea-2025-national-drug-threat-assessment/amp/)). Worth recording as a contested reading of an official threat assessment.
- Benzodiazepine side: a Nov 2025 DEA bulletin reports **>1,000 bromazolam exhibits in tablet form**, 96% of bromazolam counterfeit tablets rectangular/oval with alprazolam-associated markings ([bulletin](https://www.getsmartaboutdrugs.gov/sites/default/files/2025-11/Bromazolam-Uncontrolled-Benzodiazepine-Frequently-Bought-Foreign-Websites-Use-Counterfeit-Pills.pdf)).

## Peptides: the newest interdiction category

### US (CBP)
- The best-documented action is at the **Port of Cincinnati**: CBP reported interdicting **roughly 5,000 peptide shipments concealed in more than 300 China-origin master cartons**, announced March 2026 ([Clark & Esposito analysis](https://www.clarkespositolaw.com/post/cbp-seizes-5-000-peptide-shipments-navigating-cbp-and-fda-lawyer-issues-as-an-importer); [news report](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/5k-shipments-unapproved-weight-loss-181443625.html)).
- Products included **retatrutide, semaglutide and tirzepatide**.
- **Date conflict:** sources give the interdiction window as either **Dec 2025 – 25 Mar 2026** or **Dec 2024 – Mar 2025**. The 2025–26 window matches the March 2026 announcement. Flag as **unresolved**.
- Structurally notable: consolidation into master cartons at the freight layer, then break-bulk distribution — a hybrid of bulk import and parcel distribution. This is the single clearest published indication that the research-peptide trade now moves at commercial freight volumes.

### Ireland (HPRA)
- **Over 750,000 units of illegal medicines detained in 2025.**
- A reported **~30-fold rise in detained GLP-1 products** (March 2026). **Secondary source; verify against HPRA's own enforcement statistics.**

### UK (MHRA / Border Force)
- **No official MHRA or Border Force peptide seizure statistics were located.** Claims of "significant increases" in UK border seizures of unlicensed peptide products trace to commercial peptide-sector sites and are **unverified**.
- Enforcement actions reported secondhand: an MHRA operation near **Northampton** recovering ~**12,000 doses** of unlicensed weight-loss medicines, dated **May 2026** by one source and **October 2025** by another — **date unresolved, source quality poor**.
- **Methodological warning:** this topic has an unusually high density of commercial peptide-seller and law-firm marketing content presenting itself as enforcement analysis. Several such pages frame seizure risk comparatively in ways that function as sourcing advice. Use only regulator-published statistics.

## What the enforcement data supports, and what it does not

**Supported:**
- Synthetic-opioid seizures in the EU rose substantially 2023→2024 in both mass and count.
- Substance diversity keeps rising while seized volume concentrates in a handful of substances.
- Research peptides have become a distinct, freight-scale interdiction category since late 2025.
- Counterfeit solid-dose products are a growing vector for both nitazenes and designer benzodiazepines.

**Not supported / not measurable from published data:**
- Any estimate of total grey-market peptide volume, or of the fraction intercepted.
- Any parcel-interdiction volume series for NPS or peptides for 2025–26.
- Any published causal link between GLP-1 compounding restrictions and the growth in peptide imports — plausible, **unverified**.
- Any claim that enforcement has reduced availability of any NPS class. Published monitoring shows substitution, not reduction.
