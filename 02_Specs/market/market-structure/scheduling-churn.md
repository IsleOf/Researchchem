# Scheduling churn and analogue substitution

Up: [[market-structure/index]]

**One-line:** Monitoring bodies consistently document the same pattern — a control is imposed on a named substance or a defined chemical class, detections of the controlled substances fall, and structurally adjacent substances outside the legal definition appear in seizures and post-mortem casework within months.

## Why this is a market-structure fact, not a chemistry fact
The relevant published observation is about *definitions*. Generic (class-wide) controls work by specifying core structures and permitted substitutions. Anything outside that written scope is legal by default until the next instrument. Forensic and monitoring literature therefore treats the boundary of the legal definition as the predictor of what appears next — not toxicity, not demand.

## China's class-wide controls

### 2019 fentanyl-related substances (effective 1 May 2019)
- UNODC's legislation database records this as a generic control covering substances structurally related to fentanyl by one or more listed modifications ([UNODC generic legislation, China](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Country/DetailsLegalSystem?code=GL&country=CN)).
- US government reporting (CRS, citing a March 2025 State Department report) states that almost no fentanyl or fentanyl analogues have since been detected entering the US directly from China, with US-bound production concentrated in Mexico ([CRS China Primer](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF10890.web.html)). **Caveat:** this is a US government claim, not an independent measurement.
- The documented displacement was into **non-scheduled precursor chemicals** rather than into finished analogues ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-fentanyl-pipeline-and-chinas-role-in-the-us-opioid-crisis/); [PMC8871795](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8871795/)). China scheduled all INCB-listed fentanyl precursors in June 2025.
- **Contested:** the common claim that the 2019 control *caused* the nitazene wave is **not established** in the monitoring literature. Searches found no UNODC or EUDA substitution study making that causal link. Treat "2019 ban → nitazenes" as **unverified**.

### 2021 synthetic cannabinoids as a class (announced 11 May 2021, effective 1 July 2021)
- Announced by the China National Narcotics Control Commission alongside 18 individual substances; described as the first whole-class control of synthetic cannabinoids anywhere ([UNODC announcement, May 2021](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/ff032a29-2e14-4dab-b7d8-ab86d355c809)).
- The generic definition enumerates specific core structures, so compounds built on cores outside the list were never in scope.
- A 2025 review in the forensic literature — "Outsmarting generic legislation: 4 years into the cat-and-mouse game of the synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist market since the Chinese ban in 2021" ([PMID 40983778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40983778/)) — catalogues four years of definition-evading product classes appearing after the ban. *(Bibliographic record seen in search results only; PubMed was not reachable from this session to confirm the abstract — journal and author list **unverified**.)*
- Worked example documented by labs: ADB-FUBIATA, outside the 2021 scope, moved from 14th most prevalent synthetic cannabinoid in 2021 to 3rd in H1 2022 in one US clinical-toxicology series ([Aegis clinical update](https://www.aegislabs.com/clinical-update/synthetic-cannabinoids-2/)).
- Forensic pharmacology has also documented a distribution pattern in which weakly-CB1-active semi-finished substances outside the ban are supplied and converted downstream; the Ghent/CFSRE work measured the distributed substances as weakly active and the end products as strongly active ([CFSRE, July 2025](https://www.cfsre.org/resources/publications/do-it-yourself-synthetic-cannabinoid-receptor-agonist-precursors-as-a-ban-evading-strategy-comparison-of-the-pharmacological-characteristics-of-precursors-and-their-final-products)). The monitoring implication is that a legal-status screen on the shipped material does not predict the pharmacology of what is consumed.
- China extended generic control to benzimidazole-type synthetic cannabinoids effective 1 July 2025 (UNODC legislation database).

### 2025 nitazene class control
- China placed nitazene-class substances under domestic control via a generic definition anchored on etonitazene, recorded by UNODC with a 6 June 2025 date and reported nationally as a July 2025 ban.
- **The clearest documented substitution event in this whole file:** the EUDA attributes the post-2025 rise of **orphines** — a distinct opioid chemotype outside the nitazene definition — to that ban. Nine new orphines have been identified since 2024, and two (cychlorphine, spirochlorphine) are under formal EU risk assessment ([European Drug Report 2026 – NPS](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- Orphine analogues were identified for the first time in UK drug-checking submissions in the 2025/26 WEDINOS year ([WEDINOS annual report 2025–26](https://phw.nhs.wales/app/uploads/sites/2/2026/08/WEDINOS-annual-report-2025-to-2026.pdf)).

## EU-wide controls
- EU control decisions run through EUDA risk assessment, then a Commission delegated directive. In 2026 the EU brought three cathinones — 2-MMC, 4-BMC and NEP — under control as "drugs" in EU law ([EUDA news 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/news/2026/eu-takes-action-control-three-harmful-new-drugs_en)).
- This is substance-by-substance, not class-wide, and the lag from first detection to control is measured in years, so the EU instrument is structurally slower than the market.
- Observed downstream effect of cathinone scheduling (UNODC): seizures reported under the *cathinone NPS* heading fell sharply in most regions largely because scheduled cathinones are now counted as amphetamine-type stimulants. **This is a reporting-category artefact, not a fall in supply** — an important trap when reading seizure series across a scheduling boundary.

## US temporary / emergency scheduling
The CSA's temporary (emergency) scheduling power under 21 U.S.C. 811(h) lets DEA place a substance in Schedule I on an imminent-hazard finding, for an initial period extendable while permanent action proceeds. Recent nitazene and benzodiazepine actions:

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| 29 Jul 2024 | Temporary Schedule I: N-desethyl isotonitazene, N-piperidinyl etonitazene (two-year term to 29 Jul 2026) |
| 26 Jun 2025 | Notice of intent to temporarily schedule seven further benzimidazole-opioids ([FR 2025-11462](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-06-26/pdf/2025-11462.pdf)) |
| 15 Aug 2025 | Temporary emergency Schedule I order, two nitazenes, on imminent-hazard grounds ([DEA Diversion](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/drug_chem_info/nitazenes/nitazenes.html)) |
| 12 Jan 2026 | **Permanent** Schedule I: N-pyrrolidino metonitazene, N-pyrrolidino protonitazene ([FR 2026-00362](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-12/pdf/2026-00362.pdf)) |
| 16 Mar 2026 | Bromazolam placed in Schedule I by emergency temporary order, to 16 Mar 2028 ([FR 2026-05064](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/16/2026-05064/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-bromazolam-in-schedule-i)) — *verified; DEA's press release of 18 Mar is not the effective date* |
| 27 Aug 2026 | Further temporary Schedule I placement (benzimidazole-type), referencing an HHS letter of 1 Apr 2026 ([FR 2026-17531](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-27/pdf/2026-17531.pdf)) |

- DEA's own framing is explicitly substitution-based: new variants emerge as scheduling and enforcement pressure builds on existing ones. DEA counts **22 unique nitazenes since 2020, 21 of them Schedule I** ([DEA public safety advisory, 12 May 2026](https://www.dea.gov/press-releases/2026/05/12/public-safety-advisory)).
- Legislative response: H.R. 5032 (Nitazene Control Act), introduced 22 Aug 2025, would schedule the benzimidazole-opioid class permanently ([text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5032/text)). No evidence of enactment found as of this note's date.
- GAO has published the standing policy critique of class-wide scheduling — breadth versus research burden and over-inclusion ([GAO-21-499](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-21-499.pdf)).
- International control is the slowest layer: CND placed three NPS under international control in March 2026.

## What this means for risk
- A substance's legal status carries **no information about its potency or toxicity**. Within the post-ban cohorts, potency has repeatedly moved in both directions.
- Every substitution step resets the evidence base to zero: no human PK, no reference standards, no immunoassay, no post-mortem interpretation thresholds. See [[market-structure/monitoring-systems]].
- The substitution cycle is now faster than the scheduling cycle in both the EU and US, and faster than the analytical-standard supply chain. That gap — not the substances themselves — is the structural risk.
