# What independent and forensic testing actually finds

Up: [[market-structure/index]]

**One-line:** Across every independent dataset — national drug-checking, forensic tablet analysis, falsified-medicine seizures and community peptide testing — the dominant failure mode is **not** contamination with something exotic. It is that the label does not describe the contents: wrong substance, wrong amount, or nothing.

## National drug-checking: mismatch rates

### WEDINOS (Public Health Wales), April 2025 – March 2026
- **36.6% of community samples with a stated purchase intent did not contain the expected substance**, up from ~35% the previous year ([annual report PDF](https://phw.nhs.wales/app/uploads/sites/2/2026/08/WEDINOS-annual-report-2025-to-2026.pdf); [DDN summary](https://www.drinkanddrugsnews.com/more-than-a-third-of-wedinos-samples-do-not-contain-the-expected-substance/)).
- 8,209 samples analysed (8,032 in 2024/25).
- Samples submitted as **diazepam**: a benzodiazepine-type substance was identified in 1,131 samples = **64%** of diazepam-intent submissions — i.e. around a third contained no benzodiazepine at all.
- **43% of samples submitted as THC/CBD/cannabinoid vapes contained synthetic cannabinoids.**
- **Orphine analogues identified for the first time** in this reporting year — the drug-checking layer picking up the post-nitazene-ban substitution described in [[market-structure/scheduling-churn]].
- **Denominator caveat:** 36.6% is for community samples *with a known purchase intent*, not all samples. The report's scoring methodology should be read before comparing years or services.

### Interpretation
A ~35–37% mismatch rate, stable across two years in the most transparent national dataset available, is the single most useful summary statistic in this area. It is a property of the distribution system, not of any one substance.

## Forensic tablet analysis: counterfeit pressed pills

### Bromazolam tablets (UK, published 2026)
- 47 bromazolam tablets from UK drug-checking services and police: **55% mimicked the appearance of licensed alprazolam or diazepam** ([Gardner et al., *Drug Testing and Analysis* 2026, doi:10.1002/dta.70045](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dta.70045); [preprint](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-26w75)).
- Median content **0.49 mg**, range **0.09–5.4 mg** — a ~60-fold spread across the sample set.
- **Within a single batch of 10 tablets: 1.66–2.41 mg.** Even same-batch tablets are not interchangeable.
- Linked to post-mortem casework via the National Programme on Substance Use Mortality (NPSUM).

### US counterfeit tablet data
- A November 2025 DEA bulletin reports **more than a thousand bromazolam exhibits in tablet form**, with **96% of bromazolam-containing counterfeit tablets rectangular or oval** and bearing markings associated with Xanax/generic alprazolam ([DEA bulletin DCT-BUL-016-26](https://www.getsmartaboutdrugs.gov/sites/default/files/2025-11/Bromazolam-Uncontrolled-Benzodiazepine-Frequently-Bought-Foreign-Websites-Use-Counterfeit-Pills.pdf)).
- Nitazenes have been found in tablets presented as M-30 oxycodone and in polydrug mixtures, generally without the consumer's knowledge ([DEA 2025 NDTA](https://www.dea.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/2025NationalDrugThreatAssessment.pdf); [DEA nitazene threat report, Nov 2025](https://www.dea.gov/sites/default/files/2025-11/STR-002-25%20Nitazenes%20an%20Emerging%20Threat%20of%20a%20Diverse%20Group%20of%20Synthetic%20Opioids.pdf)).

### EU counterfeit-medicine trend
- Nitazene-containing counterfeit tablets reported to the EU EWS rose from **380 tablets in 2022 to more than 50,000 in 2024** ([secondary report of the 2026 EDR](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/counterfeit-opioid-crisis-europe-2024-1802066) — figure **not yet confirmed against the EUDA report text; treat as unverified**). The directional finding of growing counterfeit-medicine nitazene reports *is* in the EUDA 2026 report.

### Australian service-level alerts
- Point-of-care services publish individual confirmations, e.g. bromazolam in samples sold as diazepam, and bromazolam plus clobromazolam in a sample sold as "Xanax" ([TheKnow alerts](https://theknow.org.au/alerts_warnings/bromazolam-found-in-2-counterfeit-diazepam-samples/)). These are case reports, not rates.

## Falsified peptide medicines: the forensic baseline

### Janvier et al. 2018, *Talanta* (Belgium)
The reference study for falsified peptide quality. Samples were the most frequently encountered falsified peptide drugs seized by Belgian customs and regulatory agencies **2009–2017**, traced to three suspected illegal internet pharmacies ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0039914018306283)).
- **Purity 5–75%** for cysteine-containing peptides.
- Against in-house pharmacopoeial-style QC criteria, **only 38.0% of the peptides met the required purity**.
- **Class 1 elemental impurities — arsenic and lead — were present.**
- Analysed by HPLC-DAD, UHPLC-TOF-MS and/or UPLC-MS/MS, covering API content, peptide-related impurities, small-molecule contaminants, elemental impurities and residual solvents.
- **Unverified secondary claims** circulating from this paper and **not** confirmed in the abstract: "26% contained arsenic or lead above safety thresholds"; "30% contained incorrect amino acid sequences." Do not cite these.
- **Limits:** one country, one decade-old seizure window, selected for suspicion. Not a market-wide rate.

## Grey-market "research grade" peptides: the 2026 dataset

**"Evaluation of Research Grade Peptides Marketed Directly to Consumers Reveals Extensive Variability in Purity and Measured Abundance"** — Preprints.org, posted 24 April 2026, DOI [10.20944/preprints202604.1748.v1](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202604.1748). **Not peer-reviewed.**

- Re-analysis of a large public community-testing dataset of consumer-facing "research grade" peptides. Secondary accounts give the scope as roughly **6,000–6,500 independent lab reports across ~200 sellers and ~14 peptide classes, Dec 2024 – Apr 2026**; one commentary cites a much larger record count (~64,787). **Exact N is inconsistent between write-ups — read the preprint before quoting a sample size.**
- Five most-represented classes: **BPC-157, GHK-Cu, retatrutide, tesamorelin, tirzepatide** (retatrutide N≈507, tirzepatide N≈335 in the per-class analysis).
- **Failure rates are standard-dependent:** **41.6% failed** under the more liberal "Compounding Standards Model"; **71.1% failed** under the stricter model. Most failures were **dose accuracy**, not identity.
- **~15% of the endotoxin-tested subset had measurable endotoxin.** The endotoxin-tested subset is small.
- **Purity did not predict endotoxin burden (R² < 0.01, P = 0.75).** This is the single most important finding in the file: a certificate showing high chromatographic purity carries **no information about pyrogen load**.
- Roughly **1 in 42 vials contained none of the labelled peptide** (secondary account of the same dataset).
- The authors note grey-market pricing is far below compounded or approved alternatives and suggest this drives demand.
- **Authorship unverified.** Secondary sources variously attribute it to Mendias (and "Awan", and "Krysia et al."). ResearchGate lists Christopher L. Mendias. Do not assert an author list.
- **Severe methodological caveats:** the underlying reports are **vendor- or buyer-submitted to a commercial testing platform**, i.e. self-selected and not randomly sampled; there is no chain of custody; sellers with good results have an incentive to submit and publish, which if anything biases the failure rate *downward* for the sellers represented and makes the dataset silent on those who never test at all.

### Platform-level analyses of the same data
- One public analysis reports, for **tirzepatide**: purity <95% in 20/7,073 (0.28%), but **>10% over label in 1,715/4,018 (42.68%)**. For **retatrutide**: 0.58% below 95% purity, **52.86% more than 10% over label**. It also reports failure concentration — 0.53%–2.43% of sellers accounted for half of the wrong-identity, low-purity and severe-underfill results.
- **Treat as unverified commercial analysis.** It is published by a testing-adjacent commercial site, not peer-reviewed, and its inclusion criteria and deduplication are its own. The *direction* is nonetheless consistent with the preprint: identity failures are uncommon, **dose accuracy failures are the norm**, and overfill is more common than underfill.

## Licensed compounders: sterility and endotoxin are not solved by licensure
2026 produced a cluster of US recalls showing that endotoxin failure occurs in **licensed, inspected 503A pharmacies**, not only in the grey market:

- **Victory Medical Center Pharmacy** (Austin, TX), Aug 2026 — three lots of compounded glutathione recalled for elevated endotoxin; product distributed in TX, FL, NY ([Bloomberg](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/texas-pharmacy-recalls-popular-peptide-due-to-bacterial-toxins)).
- **Optimal Balance Pharmacy** (Houston, TX), 19 Aug 2026 — one lot, recalled to patient level; reported adverse events included **fever, chills, severe headache, nausea, vomiting and allergy-like symptoms** ([FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/optimal-balance-pharmacy-issues-voluntary-nationwide-recall-certain-lots-compounded-glutathione-200)).
- FDA noted both pharmacies obtained glutathione **labelled as dietary-supplement grade from the same manufacturer** — an upstream raw-material failure, not two independent pharmacy errors.
- **Greenwich Rx**, nationwide recall posted by FDA 1 Oct 2026 — compounded glutathione distributed to patients nationwide 26 Jun – 21 Sep 2026 ([FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/greenwich-rx-issues-voluntary-nationwide-recall-compounded-glutathione-due-elevated-endotoxin-levels)). A secondary source reports an FDA inspection report issued in Sept 2026 (unverified).
- **Apothecary Pharma** (NC), 24 Sep 2026 — nationwide recall of compounded **tirzepatide** injection because sterility could not be assured.
- FDA's stated hazard for endotoxin exposure: inflammatory reactions, anaphylactic shock and death.

### Why this matters for interpretation
Endotoxin is a **pyrogen**, not a microorganism. Terminal sterility and low endotoxin are independent attributes: a sterile-filtered product can carry a clinically significant pyrogen load from contaminated raw material. The 2026 preprint's R²<0.01 purity-vs-endotoxin result and the 2026 glutathione recalls point at the same thing from opposite ends of the regulatory spectrum.
