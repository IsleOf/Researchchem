# Open questions, gaps and contested claims

Up: [[health-risks/index]]

## A. Claims I could not verify — do not reuse without a primary source

These circulate widely. Each was searched for and **not confirmed against a primary source** in this research.

1. **"100% endotoxin positivity in grey-market semaglutide, JMIR Dermatology 2024."** Repeated on several consumer sites. The study could not be located or confirmed. Treat as unverified.
2. **"73% of 1,200+ samples failed" (attributed to a named peptide lab).** Appears only on a vendor-adjacent site. Unverified.
3. **"40–75% of grey-market peptides fail quality criteria."** A misquotation of the Apr 2026 preprint, which reports **41.6–71.1%**. Use the preprint's figure, and label it preprint-grade.
4. **MK-677 hip-fracture trial (Adunsky et al. 2011, *Arch Gerontol Geriatr* 53(2):183–9, doi [10.1016/j.archger.2010.10.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.archger.2010.10.004), [PubMed 21067829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21067829/)).** **✅ Verified (2026-11-09), via a peer-reviewed secondary source.** A narrative review in *Sexual Medicine Reviews* (Sigalos & Pastuszak, 2017;6(1):45–53, doi [10.1016/j.sxmr.2017.02.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sxmr.2017.02.004), full text at [PMC5632578](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5632578/)) reports the trial as **123 elderly patients over 24 weeks**, the **only** randomised double-blind placebo-controlled GH-secretagogue trial stopped early over CHF, with **4 patients on MK-677 (6.5%) and 1 on placebo (1.7%) developing CHF**. The wiki figures were therefore numerically right. **Two caveats the wiki relays drop:**
   - the review attributes the excess "in part to **higher baseline blood pressures** in the ibutamoren group" — i.e. the arms were not balanced on a key cardiac covariate;
   - the **same 4 (6.5%) vs 1 (1.7%) split is also reported for elevated blood glucose** in this trial, so the two endpoints are easy to conflate when the figure travels without its label.
   A **contrasting** trial (Bach et al., *JAGS* 2004;52(4):516–23, n=161, 6 months, same indication) found serious AEs **similarly distributed** between arms. Adunsky remains the strongest controlled human CHF signal in the class, but it is one 123-patient trial with a covariate imbalance and one larger non-replication. *Abstract not read directly: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov article pages return a bot interstitial in this environment.*
5. **"Bradycardia refractory to naloxone in 92% of cases" (Chicago medetomidine cluster).** From a secondary summary; **not found in the MMWR text**. The MMWR states most confirmed cases had no or partial naloxone response; a poison-centre summary gives 11 of 12.
6. **"91% ICU admission, 24% intubation" (Philadelphia medetomidine withdrawal series) — CLOSED 2026-11-09.** **Both figures are in the MMWR text:** "Among the 165 patients, **150 (91%) required intensive care unit (ICU) care, including 39 (24%) who received endotracheal intubation**." The report is MMWR 74(15):266–268 (doi 10.15585/mmwr.mm7415a2, 1 May 2025). **The erratum is resolved: it is MMWR 74(17):296, 15 May 2025** (doi 10.15585/mmwr.mm7417a3) — the "74(15)" in some sources is the issue being *corrected*, not the issue the erratum appeared in. It corrects **reference 1 on page 268 only** and **changes no figure**. ([erratum](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7417a3.htm))
7. **"Pittsburgh reported ten cases."** Wrong — the MMWR reports **23**.
8. **Adrafinil "elevates liver enzymes and requires periodic liver function monitoring."** Found **only** on supplement and nootropic sites. No case report, cohort or LiverTox adrafinil entry located. The prodrug mechanism is plausible; the clinical magnitude is unmeasured.
9. **"Melanoma in situ diagnosed four weeks after beginning MT-II from a regulated compounding pharmacy" (2026 summary).** Secondary only; unverified.
10. **FDA peptide impurity thresholds (~0.1% identification focus, ANDA pathway unsuitable above ~0.5%).** From FDA slide decks and trade press; **not verified against the final guidance text**. A 2018 BIO comment letter disputed the justification for those thresholds.
11. **Exact date of the TGA peptide safety advisory.** Secondary sources place it mid-2026; not confirmed on tga.gov.au.
12. **FDA warning-letter recipient names and product lists (24 Aug 2026).** From law-firm alerts; not verified against FDA's warning-letter database.
13. **PCAC BPC-157 vote.** **Confirmed 8–6 with one abstention in favour (23 July 2026)** by multiple summaries; the outlier "failed 6–7" claim is not supported.
14. **NECC final case and death totals.** Sources give 233/15 (mid-Oct 2012), 48 deaths (Mar 2013), and retrospective totals as high as 798 affected / 64 deaths, with one version saying >100 dead. **Irreconcilable from what was retrieved** — needs CDC's final outbreak report.
15. **Mendelian-randomisation evidence on IGF-1 and cancer causality.** **✅ Retrieved 2026-11-09 — and it does not say what the repo said it said.** Larsson & Burgess, *Cancer Medicine* 2020;9(18):6836–6842 ([PMC7520358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7520358/)), using 416 IGF-1 SNPs, found causal support for **colorectal cancer only** (OR per SD 1.11 in UK Biobank, 1.22 in BioBank Japan); **prostate was inconclusive** (1.03, CI 0.97–1.09 in the PRACTICAL consortium), **breast was null** in UK Biobank, and there was **no significant association with 14 other cancers**. The earlier description of MR work as "generally supportive" of a causal direction was **too generous and has been corrected** in [[health-risks/cancer-and-long-term]]. Separately, the **19-site pooled cohort** that note said did not exist **does** (Knuppel et al., [PMC7642199](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7642199/), n=412,645): total-cancer HR 1.03 per 5 nmol/L, but **site directions diverge**, with liver strongly **inverse** (0.32). Neither study addresses *pharmacologically* raised IGF-1 — that remains the real gap.
16. **Hunter criteria exact items.** Dunkley et al. 2003 (*QJM*) and Werneke et al. 2016 (*BMC Neurology*) were not retrieved in full; the criteria are deliberately not reproduced.

**Access limitation to note:** `cdc.gov` and `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` did not resolve from this session's network, so MMWR and PMC sources were characterised from search results and secondary summaries rather than read in full. Volume/issue/page/doi details were cross-checked across multiple independent sources, but **direct full-text verification of the MMWR reports and the PMC articles has not been done**. That applies to every MMWR figure in this area.

## B. Genuinely unknown — nobody has the answer

### Quality and contamination
- **What is actually in grey-market vials beyond identity and purity?** No peer-reviewed dataset quantifying **heavy metals / elemental impurities**, **residual solvents**, **TFA counter-ion load**, or **sub-visible particulates** in grey-market peptide vials was located. This is the largest single empirical gap in this area and is directly measurable.
- **Aggregate content.** Aggregation is the mechanistic driver of immunogenicity and is promoted by exactly the handling grey-market material receives. **No one has measured aggregate content in grey-market vials.**
- **Does immunogenicity from grey-market peptides ever cross-neutralise the endogenous hormone?** Mechanistically possible and would mean harm persisting after stopping. **No published case located.** Would require anti-drug-antibody assays in exposed people.
- Is the Apr 2026 preprint's ~15% endotoxin figure robust? It rests on self-submitted third-party certificates — a sample of unknown representativeness. **Needs peer review and an independent sampling frame.**

### Cardiometabolic
- **Does grey-market GH-secretagogue use produce clinically meaningful glucose dysregulation at real-world exposures?** Nass 2008 gives +0.3 mmol/L over two years at a known dose of a characterised product. Nobody knows what unmonitored use of uncharacterised material does.
- **Additive chronotropic load:** GLP-1 agonist + stimulant co-use. Both literatures exist; the combination is addressed by neither. Explicitly inferred in [[health-risks/cardiometabolic]].
- **No meta-analysis directly compares semaglutide with tirzepatide on heart rate.** The cross-study comparison in the notes mixes populations and designs and must not be read as a ranking.
- **Cardiac effects of the investigational multi-agonists** ([[retatrutide]], [[survodutide]], [[cagrilintide]], [[mazdutide]]) outside sponsor trials — unknown, and unknown entirely for grey-market copies.
- Incidence of cathinone cardiotoxicity. The poison-centre data give 222 cases; **there is no denominator**, so no incidence or dose-response estimate is possible.

### Endocrine
- **Does continuous (long-acting) stimulation of pulsatile receptor systems cause progressive desensitisation in humans?** This is the central unanswered pharmacological question of the GH-secretagogue market. The DAC form of CJC-1295 has a ~6–8 day half-life applied to a system built for ~hourly pulses; **no human study answers whether that sustains stimulation or downregulates the receptor.** The GnRH-agonist precedent shows continuous agonism can be a net suppressant.
- **Why did IGF-1 rise without correlating with any GH parameter** in the human CJC-1295 study (Ionescu 2006)? Unexplained, and it undercuts the assumed mechanism.
- **What is the true natural recovery rate of the HPG axis after non-medical androgen exposure?** The 2023 scoping review (recovery over months) and the 2021 systematic review (**4 of 38 with known outcomes fully reversible**) point in opposite directions, both observational with opposite selection biases. **No prospective controlled data exist.** The 2025 Irish Endocrine Society meta-analysis exists only as an abstract — its pooled result is the single most useful outstanding datum here.
- **Female HPG effects and ovulatory function** in this population: essentially unstudied.
- **Fertility outcomes after grey-market GH-secretagogue or Khavinson-peptide exposure:** no data at all.
- **Thyroid-hormone contamination of grey-market "metabolic" products:** no systematic data. Given the documented undeclared-semaglutide case, the question is live.

### Organ-specific
- **Cathinone-specific rhabdomyolysis incidence.** No case series located; currently a class inference from the amphetamine/MDMA/cocaine literature.
- **Etizolam and designer-benzodiazepine withdrawal seizures.** No etizolam case reports and no withdrawal-specific seizure series located. Class-inferred.
- **What actually caused the bromazolam cluster's hyperthermia, seizures and myocardial injury?** The MMWR authors list anoxic injury, withdrawal features, or an unmeasured intoxicant as alternatives and state no signal attributable to bromazolam intoxication existed. **Still unresolved.**
- **Endocarditis incidence among this injecting population:** no estimate located.
- **Is the NAION signal a class effect of incretin agonists or semaglutide-specific? — ANSWERED as a regulatory matter, 2026-11-09.** FDA now treats it as a **class** question: it requires the warning on **all** GLP-1 RAs and states that "NAION cases have been reported for all currently marketed GLP-1 RA products". That is a labelling decision on a **potential** risk, not a causal finding — FDA found no mechanism in animal data, its trial-level rate (6.4 vs 3.8 per 100,000 PY) sits inside the background population range, and its own Sentinel cohort found no significant excess against SGLT-2 inhibitors. The EU PRAC conclusion (Jun 2025) still covers semaglutide only, and Novo Nordisk still disputes causality. **So: regulated as a class effect, not demonstrated as one.**
- **Melanotan II and melanoma:** causality unestablished and confounded by concurrent UV tanning-bed use in most cases. A Dutch dermatology review finds no conclusive evidence of a link to dysplastic naevi while the concern remains. **This needs a cohort with UV-exposure adjustment, which does not exist.**

### Cancer and long-term
- **Does pharmacologically raising IGF-1 increase cancer risk?** The epidemiology measures *endogenous* variation within the normal range in untreated people. The extrapolation to pharmacological elevation is unvalidated, and no cohort of grey-market secretagogue users exists.
- **Do angiogenic peptides promote existing subclinical tumours?** Mechanistically coherent (and the anti-VEGF drug class proves the pathway matters in human cancer), but reviewed sources state no evidence of BPC-157 causing human cancer or initiating animal tumours. **No carcinogenicity study of any of these peptides exists.** Note that a negative initiation study would not answer the promotion question.
- **Epitalon in vivo direction.** Telomere lengthening via ALT in breast-cancer lines (2025, independent) vs fewer tumours in some rodent data (Anisimov 2003). Two opposing non-human findings, no resolution.
- **The structural question:** a harm with 10-year latency affecting 1 in 2,000 users would be **undetectable** — no denominator, no exposure registry, no outcome ascertainment. So "no reported cancers" carries **zero** evidential weight. Is there any feasible study design that could detect such a signal in an unregistered population? (Candidate: linking anonymised drug-checking submissions to health records. Nobody has attempted it.)

## C. Contested claims where credible sources disagree

| Claim | Position A | Position B |
|---|---|---|
| **Semaglutide causes NAION** | **EMA PRAC (Jun 2025):** very rare side effect, up to 1 in 10,000, ~2-fold risk in T2D; label changed; stop if confirmed | **Novo Nordisk:** totality of evidence does not suggest a reasonable possibility of causal relationship; benefit-risk remains favourable |
| **HPG axis recovery after androgen exposure** | **2023 scoping review:** near-complete testosterone recovery over months; gonadotropins 3–6 months | **2021 systematic review:** only 4 of 38 cases with known outcomes fully reversible; full recovery difficult in most |
| **Hunter criteria for serotonin syndrome** | Widely described as the most sensitive criteria and institutional standard | **2020 J Integr Neurosci review:** cannot be regarded as a gold standard — missed more cases than two other criteria sets |
| **Naloxone adequacy for novel potent opioids** | Amaducci 2023: significantly more boluses needed (1.33 vs 0.36); Australian guidance says higher/repeated doses may be required | A 2025 conference slide deck states standard dosing is sufficient and higher doses increase withdrawal; a Best Bets appraisal found *similar* cumulative dose (4.40 mg vs 6.41 mg). **Unresolved; the practical guidance — repeat as needed, prioritise ventilation — is the same either way.** |
| **MK-677 and glucose handling** | Nass 2008 and Chapman 1996: fasting glucose rises, insulin sensitivity falls | One ageing-research wiki reports fasting glucose and insulin unchanged with worsened OGTT. Conflicts with the trials; treat the wiki as unreliable. |
| **PCAC BPC-157 vote direction** | Majority of coverage (incl. major outlets): recommended, 8–6 with one abstention; confirmed in search | One outlier source: failed 6–7 (not supported) |
| **Epitalon oncogenic direction** | Al-Dulaimi 2025: telomere lengthening via ALT in breast-cancer lines | Anisimov 2003: fewer tumours in some rodent models |

## D. What to watch

1. **Peer review of the Apr 2026 purity/endotoxin preprint**, and whether the endotoxin figure survives an independent sampling frame.
2. **FDA rulemaking on the seven PCAC peptides.** The Jul 2026 recommendations are non-binding and notice-and-comment rulemaking had not started. FDA staff had recommended against. Watch for the Federal Register notice — and watch for the recommendations being reported as safety clearances, which they are not.
3. **Whether further FDA warning letters follow the 24 Aug 2026 batch**, and whether the "research use only" disclaimer defence is tested in litigation. FDA's reasoning — that dosing instructions and dose calculators establish intended human use — is the operative legal lever.
4. ~~The CDC erratum to MMWR 74(15):266–268~~ — **closed 2026-11-09** (74(17):296; reference-only correction). Still open: any follow-up series on medetomidine withdrawal as the adulterant spreads geographically (NYC advisory 2026 suggests it is).
5. ~~Whether NAION monitoring extends to other incretin agonists, or stays semaglutide-specific.~~ **Closed 2026-11-09:** FDA extended it to the entire GLP-1 RA class. Still open: whether the EU follows, and whether any regulator moves from "potential risk" to a causal finding.
6. **Whether any jurisdiction establishes surveillance for peptide-associated adverse events.** The TGA's spontaneous reports (SIRS, hypersensitivity, liver injury) are currently the only regulator-level dataset in existence. If one country builds a case definition and a reporting pathway, this area stops being evidence-free.
7. ~~Full-text verification of the Adunsky 2011 MK-677 per-arm CHF counts~~ — **closed 2026-11-09**: 4 (6.5%) vs 1 (1.7%) of 123 over 24 weeks, confirmed via [PMC5632578](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5632578/). Residual question: the trial's **baseline blood-pressure imbalance** between arms, which the review flags as a partial explanation and which only the Adunsky full text can quantify.
8. **Analytical work on heavy metals, residual solvents and aggregates** in grey-market vials. Directly measurable, cheap relative to its value, and nobody has published it.

**Update 2026-11-09 — VERIFIED against FDA.** FDA Drug Safety Communication of **9 November 2026** requires application holders for **all** GLP-1 RA medications to add NAION information to the **Warnings and Precautions** section of labeling — the whole class (exenatide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, lixisenatide, semaglutide, tirzepatide, orforglipron and the insulin combinations), not just Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro/Zepbound ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential)). Full wording, product list and the underlying numbers are in [[health-risks/organ-specific]]. This closes the regulatory arc described above — and reinforces the point that detecting a rare harm required an approved product, prescription databases, a regulator with compulsion powers and a label to change. None of that machinery exists for grey-market supply.
