# Injection and contamination harms

Up: [[health-risks/index]]

The single most consistent finding across the grey-market literature is that **the biggest documented acute harms come from the product and the route, not from the molecule's pharmacology**. A compound can be pharmacologically benign and still put someone in an emergency department because the vial was never made, tested or filled to any sterile-injectable standard.

See also: [[grey-market-quality]] · [[drug-checking]]

## 1. Pyrogenic (endotoxin) reactions

**Mechanism.** Bacterial endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide, LPS) from Gram-negative organisms is a cell-wall fragment. It is **not** killed by sterilising filtration or by autoclaving in any reliable way — the bacteria can be dead and the endotoxin still active. Injected LPS binds TLR4 and triggers IL-1/IL-6/TNF release, producing the classic picture of fever, rigors/shaking chills, myalgia, headache, tachycardia and hypotension beginning roughly 30 minutes to 6 hours after injection. Licensed parenteral products are therefore released against a bacterial endotoxin test (BET/LAL) limit; "research use only" material is released against nothing.

**Published findings in grey-market peptides.** An April 2026 preprint analysing a large public dataset of third-party certificates on grey-market research peptides reported that **41.6–71.1% of samples failed basic quality criteria** and that **measurable endotoxin was present in ~15% of samples**, with **no correlation between a high purity score and a low endotoxin level** — i.e. a "99% pure" certificate says nothing about whether the vial is pyrogen-free.
- Source: [Preprint, Apr 2026](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202604.1748)
- **Status: preprint, not peer-reviewed.** The exact failure-rate range should be treated as provisional, and secondary sites quoting "40–75%" are misquoting it.
- **Unverified:** a widely-repeated claim that a 2024 *JMIR Dermatology* study found "100% endotoxin positivity" in grey-market semaglutide could not be confirmed from a primary source and should not be cited.

**Regulatory corroboration.** Australia's TGA safety advisory on unapproved peptide products lists **severe allergic reactions, systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) and generalised hypersensitivity** among reports received, across products including BPC-157, GHK-Cu, TB-500, retatrutide and CJC-1295, many carrying "research use only" disclaimers. ([TGA advisory](https://www.tga.gov.au/node/575382); secondary summary: [Medical Republic](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/emerging-side-effects-of-unapproved-peptides/126627)). A SIRS presentation after injecting an unapproved peptide is clinically very hard to separate from an endotoxin reaction or from early sepsis — which is the point.

## 2. Non-sterile compounding outbreaks — documented case examples

These are the reference events for what happens when injectables are prepared outside a validated sterile process. They involve *licensed* facilities, which makes them a floor, not a ceiling, for grey-market risk.

| Date | Event | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 2012 – 2013 | **New England Compounding Center (NECC), Framingham MA** — fungal contamination of compounded preservative-free methylprednisolone acetate for epidural injection | The largest fungal meningitis outbreak documented in the US. *Exserohilum rostratum* was the predominant organism. Case and death counts rose through the outbreak: CDC reported 233 cases/15 deaths by mid-Oct 2012 and 48 deaths by 10 Mar 2013; retrospective totals cited are substantially higher (one summary gives 798 affected / 64 deaths). **The exact final totals differ between sources and should be checked against CDC's final report before being quoted.** ([CDC HAN 00327 archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130216175848/http://www.bt.cdc.gov/HAN/han00327.asp) · [CDC analysis, PMC4451895](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4451895) · [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Compounding_Center_meningitis_outbreak)) |
| 26 Jul 2024 | **FDA alert: compounded semaglutide dosing errors** | FDA warned of adverse events, some requiring hospitalisation, from overdoses of compounded semaglutide. In most reports patients drew **5–20× the intended dose**. Causes: multidose vials and syringes rather than fixed-dose pens, non-standard concentrations varying between compounders, mg↔unit↔mL conversion errors by prescribers, and patients unfamiliar with drawing a dose. Reported events: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, syncope, headache, migraine, dehydration, acute pancreatitis, gallstones. FDA noted that semaglutide's ~1-week half-life means a **prolonged** period of observation may be needed. ([FDA alert, via Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20240909/fda-warns-of-adverse-events-due-to-overdosing-of-compounded-semaglutide) · [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/fda-warns-clinicians-of-adverse-events-due-to-overdosing-of-compounded-semaglutide)) |
| 24 Aug 2026 | **FDA warning letters to online peptide sellers** | CDER's Office of Unapproved Drugs and Labeling Compliance issued letters to several sellers (ipamorelin, tesamorelin, GLP-1s among the products). FDA held that **"for research use only / not for human consumption" disclaimers did not protect the sellers**, because dosing information, injection instructions and dose calculators on the same websites established intended human use. Bacteriostatic water sold alongside was itself treated as an unapproved drug because it was intended for preparing an injection. ([Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/fda-balances-peptide-policy-with-enforcement-against-unlawful-peptide-sellers) · [McDermott client alert](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=50381208)) |

Direct relevance: the NECC event shows that sterility failure in an injectable can kill people who had no pharmacological exposure risk at all. The 2024 FDA alert shows that **non-standard concentration is itself a hazard** — the mechanism of harm was arithmetic, not toxicology.

## 3. Abscess, cellulitis and soft-tissue infection

Repeated non-sterile injection is the dominant route to bacterial soft-tissue infection. A North Carolina syringe-service-programme survey found **2 in 3 participants reported a lifetime skin/soft-tissue infection (SSTI) and 4 in 10 were hospitalised for their most recent one** — useful as an order-of-magnitude baseline, though the population is not the same as peptide users.

Where an alpha-2 agonist adulterant is involved, the wound picture changes. Xylazine-associated wounds are described as beginning with **soft-tissue necrosis** rather than as primarily infectious cellulitis/abscess, with progressive necrosis and delayed healing; the proposed mechanism is local vasoconstriction reducing cutaneous perfusion. A 2026 scoping review in *Advances in Skin & Wound Care* synthesises presentation and management and concludes multidisciplinary care is required ([PMID 42007753](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42007753/)). The earliest signal was Rodríguez et al. 2009, which found a higher prevalence of skin ulcers in people injecting xylazine-containing material. Philadelphia's clinical recommendations are the most developed public guidance ([PDF](https://hip.phila.gov/document/4132/Recommendations_for_Caring_for_People_with_Xylazine-Associated_Wounds_1.8.pdf)).

Endocarditis, osteomyelitis and epidural abscess are the recognised deep sequelae of injection-associated bacteraemia. **Unverified in this research:** no incidence estimate for endocarditis specific to this population was located.

## 4. Particulate and solvent contamination

- **Particulates.** Reconstituting lyophilised powder with non-pharmacopoeial water, drawing through a coring stopper, or filling from an unfiltered line introduces visible and sub-visible particulates. Licensed injectables are controlled to USP <787>/<788> sub-visible particle limits. Grey-market vials have no such release testing. Consequences described in the parenteral literature are injection-site granuloma, phlebitis and, for particulates reaching the circulation, embolic/granulomatous pulmonary disease.
- **Residual solvents.** Peptide synthesis and purification use solvents (e.g. acetonitrile, DMF, TFA counter-ion, piperidine) controlled under ICH Q3C for licensed products. Grey-market certificates of analysis rarely report residual solvent or counter-ion content at all; TFA load in particular is a known analytical and tolerability variable in research-grade peptide material. **Mark as mechanistic/regulatory inference — no case series quantifying harm from residual solvents in grey-market peptide vials was located.**
- **Bacteriostatic water and preservatives.** Benzyl alcohol, the usual bacteriostatic agent, is a recognised neonatal toxicity hazard (see existing node `risk:benzyl-alcohol-neonates`) and is not appropriate for all routes. FDA's Aug 2026 letters treated bacteriostatic water sold for this purpose as an unapproved drug.

## 5. Heavy metals and residual process impurities

Licensed drug products are controlled for elemental impurities under ICH Q3D (catalyst and equipment-derived metals) and for process-related impurities and genotoxic impurities under ICH Q3A/M7. None of this applies to "research use only" material, and routine grey-market certificates of analysis typically report **identity and HPLC purity only** — i.e. the two tests that would *not* detect heavy metals, residual solvents, elemental impurities or endotoxin.

**Mark clearly:** this is a documented *gap in testing*, not a documented *finding of contamination*. No peer-reviewed dataset quantifying heavy-metal content in grey-market peptide vials was located in this research. Claims circulating on vendor-adjacent sites (e.g. a "73% of 1,200+ samples" figure) are **unverified and should not be reused**.

The one directly documented identity failure found: the TGA reported a serious reaction to a product **sold as retatrutide** in which laboratory testing found **undeclared semaglutide and no retatrutide at all** ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/node/575382)). That is `risk:product-mislabelling` in its most consequential form — the user had no way to know which pharmacology they had taken.

## 6. Immunogenicity from peptide impurities and aggregates

This is the risk most specific to peptides and the least appreciated.

**The regulatory position.** FDA's May 2021 final guidance on ANDAs for certain highly purified synthetic peptides (synthetic glucagon, liraglutide, nesiritide, teriparatide, teduglutide referencing rDNA-origin listed drugs) states that **peptide-related impurities and aggregates may pose immunogenicity risks**, and requires comparative characterisation of the generic's impurity profile against the reference product to set acceptance limits. FDA reviewers describe in silico and in vitro tools for assessing product-related impurities and note that highly sensitive assays are needed to demonstrate absence of innate-immune-response-modulating impurities. ([FDA presentation materials](https://fda.gov/media/166572/download) · [summary](https://www.bioxconomy.com/modalities/cmc-regulatory-challenges-during-peptide-development))
- Reported impurity thresholds (identification/assessment focus above ~0.1%; ANDA pathway possibly unsuitable above ~0.5%) appear in FDA slide decks and trade press. **Mark as unverified against the final guidance text.** A 2018 BIO comment letter argued the draft did not adequately justify those thresholds ([letter](https://dev.bio.org/sites/default/files/legacy/bioorg/docs/BIO%20Letter_%20ANDA%20for%20synthetic%20peptides_2-2-18.pdf)).

**Why it matters here.** Three mechanisms:
1. **Deletion/insertion and D-amino-acid sequence variants** from imperfect solid-phase synthesis present novel epitopes the immune system has never seen.
2. **Aggregates.** Peptides aggregate via hydrophobic interaction or misfolding; aggregated protein/peptide is a classic driver of anti-drug antibody formation. Aggregation is promoted by exactly the conditions grey-market material experiences: unvalidated lyophilisation, unrefrigerated shipping, freeze-thaw cycling, agitation, and reconstitution in the wrong diluent at the wrong concentration. FDA's position is that a generic must not increase aggregation tendency or alter aggregate nature.
3. **Innate-immune-modulating impurities** (the endotoxin issue above acts as an adjuvant, raising the chance that any immune response that does form is a durable one).

**Clinical consequences described in the peptide/biologic literature:** injection-site reactions, generalised hypersensitivity, and — for peptides analogous to endogenous hormones — the possibility of **cross-reactive antibodies neutralising the endogenous hormone**, which is a mechanism for harm persisting after the product is stopped. **Mark: no published case of cross-neutralisation from a grey-market peptide was located.** The existing nodes `risk:immunogenicity` and `risk:immunogenicity-impurities` cover this.

## What this adds up to

- Purity certificates answer the wrong question. Identity + HPLC purity does not address sterility, endotoxin, elemental impurities, residual solvent, or aggregate content.
- The quantified finding is the 2026 preprint's ~15% endotoxin positivity and 41.6–71.1% overall quality failure, with purity and endotoxin uncorrelated — **preprint-grade evidence**.
- The historical worst case (NECC) came from a licensed facility, which bounds how much comfort licensure alone provides.
- Non-standard concentrations are an independent hazard: the 2024 FDA alert's 5–20× overdoses were caused by the presentation, not the drug.
