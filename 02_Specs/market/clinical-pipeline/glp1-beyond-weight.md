# GLP-1 evidence beyond weight loss (L1)

Part of [[clinical-pipeline/index]]. Related: [[clinical-pipeline/next-gen-obesity]], [[clinical-pipeline/risks]], [[clinical-pipeline/open-questions]].

The commercial and grey-market story treats "GLP-1" as a general-purpose metabolic and behavioural drug. The trial evidence is far more uneven than that. This note separates **proven on a hard clinical endpoint**, **approved on a surrogate**, **positive but single-trial**, **exploratory**, and **negative**.

Doses are not recorded (repo rule); where trials used different doses for different indications that is noted qualitatively only.

---

## Proven on hard clinical endpoints

### Cardiovascular — SELECT (semaglutide)
Adults with overweight/obesity **and** established cardiovascular disease, **without** diabetes. Primary composite (CV death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke): **6.5% vs 8.0%**, **HR 0.80**. This is the strongest single result in the field — a weight-management agent reducing MACE in a non-diabetic population. N Engl J Med 2023, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2307563.
**Limits:** secondary prevention only. It does **not** establish CV benefit in people without existing CV disease, which is most grey-market users.

### Kidney — FLOW (semaglutide)
Type 2 diabetes **with** chronic kidney disease. Primary kidney composite (kidney failure, sustained ≥50% eGFR decline, kidney or CV death) reduced **24%**; kidney-specific components **HR 0.79**; CV death **HR 0.71**; eGFR slope improved by ~**1.16 mL/min/1.73 m²/year**. N Engl J Med 2024, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2403347.
Follow-ups: benefit consistent across CKD severity (PMC11931213); CV subgroup analyses PMID 42233552; prespecified **pooled kidney analysis across SELECT, FLOW and SOUL** (~30,800 participants) — primary kidney composite **HR 0.84**, narrower secondary composite **HR 0.80**, PMID 42567173. Kidney outcomes in the non-diabetic SELECT population: PMC11271413.

### Obstructive sleep apnoea — SURMOUNT-OSA (tirzepatide)
**FDA-approved December 2024** for moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity — the first drug ever approved for OSA. Two randomised placebo-controlled trials, **469 adults** without type 2 diabetes, **52 weeks**; weight reduction **−17.7%** (non-PAP-using trial) and **−19.6%** (PAP-using trial). FDA approval letter: supplement 217806Orig1s013.
**Caveat that matters:** the apnoea benefit tracks weight loss closely. Whether any of it is weight-independent is **not established** — reviews explicitly leave this open.
Newer: retatrutide's OSA basket reported AHI reductions of **−22.9 (low), −34.3 (mid) and −31.7 (high)** events/hour vs **−9.9** placebo at 80 weeks (hybrid-treatment estimand). ⚠️ **Corrected 2026-11-09**: these values appear in the **TRIUMPH-1 primary paper's own abstract** (doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2604169), and the low-dose figure was previously omitted here. A separate substudy report (PMID 42904878) was not retrievable, so do not cite it as an independent result — see [[clinical-pipeline/next-gen-obesity]].

## Approved on a histological surrogate (benefit not yet confirmed on outcomes)

### MASH — ESSENCE (semaglutide)
**FDA accelerated approval 15 August 2025** (Wegovy) for non-cirrhotic MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3). Interim 72-week results: **MASH resolution without worsening fibrosis 62.9% vs 34.3%** placebo; **≥1-stage fibrosis improvement without worsening MASH 36.8% vs 22.4%**. Mean weight change −10.5% vs −2.0%. Favourable hepatic safety; no discontinuations for liver-enzyme elevation. N Engl J Med 2025, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2413258.
**Status:** accelerated approval on **histology**, not on decompensation, transplant or death. Final approval awaits long-term outcome data. **Cirrhosis is explicitly outside the indication.** AASLD updated practice guidance Nov 2025 (PMID 41201884). Patient-reported outcome analysis PMID 42580587.

### Liver fat — SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD (survodutide)
Phase 3 in obesity with MASLD; co-primary endpoints met on both estimands; up to **84.2%** of efficacy-estimand participants achieved ≥30% relative liver-fat reduction (Nature Medicine; PMC13472939).
**This is liver fat on imaging, in MASLD — not MASH histology.** It does not support a MASH indication on its own.

### MASH histology — survodutide phase 2
48 weeks: MASH improvement without worsening fibrosis in **47%, 62% and 43%** of the three dose arms vs **22%** placebo. Note the **non-monotonic dose response** — the mid arm outperformed the top arm, which is a real interpretive problem.
Phase 3 **LIVERAGE** (F2–F3, NCT06632444) and **LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis** (n≈1590 compensated MASH cirrhosis, NCT06632457, primary completion ~2029) are **enrolling; no results**.

## Exploratory: addiction

This is where the gap between public enthusiasm and evidence is widest. There is **no completed phase 3 for any substance use disorder**.

### Alcohol use disorder
- **Oral semaglutide RCT**, n=50, moderate-to-severe AUD, treatment-seeking, **8 weeks**. **Mixed/negative on its primary measures**: did **not** significantly reduce laboratory-measured craving or drinks per day. Did reduce **heavy drinking days, drinks per drinking day, naturalistic craving and cannabis-use days**. PMID 42522065, PMC13567346.
- **Once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide RCT**, n=48, **non-treatment-seeking** adults. Reduced alcohol consumed during a post-treatment laboratory self-administration task. PMID 39937469.
- Both are **phase 2, n<60, short, surrogate-heavy**. "Semaglutide treats alcoholism" is not supported.
- **CRAVE** (NCT07218354) plans to randomise **622** US veterans with moderate-to-severe AUD; no results. Also registered: NCT05892432, NCT05520775.

### Nicotine / tobacco
- Strongest human signal is for **exenatide**, not semaglutide: in a small pilot, exenatide + nicotine patch roughly doubled self-reported abstinence at 6 weeks vs placebo + patch. **Pilot-scale.**
- **Dulaglutide did not improve abstinence** (metabolic benefits only).
- One RCT reported reduced daily cigarette consumption with semaglutide; most semaglutide smoking-cessation evidence is observational. Trials ongoing. Review: PMID 41697556. Meta-analysis across substance use disorders: PMC13409016.
- Figures like "40–50% craving reduction" circulating in popular coverage have **no identifiable trial basis** — treat as unsupported.

### Opioid use disorder
- A trial is **registered** (NCT04199728) but I found **no published outcomes**. The 2026 meta-analytic literature covers alcohol and tobacco only.
- **There is currently no human efficacy evidence for GLP-1 agonists in opioid use disorder.**

## Negative: cognition / neurodegeneration

### EVOKE and EVOKE+ (oral semaglutide, early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease)
Two phase 3 randomised placebo-controlled trials. **Both failed.** No statistically significant slowing of progression on the **CDR-SB** primary endpoint at **week 104** in either trial; prespecified pooled analyses showed **no delay in progression to dementia** in MCI due to Alzheimer's through **week 156**. Biomarkers moved in the expected direction (reductions up to ~10% in some measures) **without clinical benefit**. Safety consistent with other semaglutide indications; no new signals. The 1-year extensions were discontinued.
Topline Nov 2025; CTAD Dec 2025; published **Lancet 2026**, PIIS0140-6736(26)00459-9. Plain-language summary PMC13390525.

**Why this matters for this knowledge base:** EVOKE was the flagship "GLP-1 as neuroprotective / anti-ageing" hypothesis test, with ~25,000 patient-years behind it, and it was comprehensively negative. Any grey-market or wellness claim that GLP-1 agonists protect the brain now has to contend with the largest and most direct test of that claim having failed. Candidate explanations under discussion include limited CNS penetration — these are hypotheses, not findings.
