# Risks that matter in the clinical pipeline (L2)

Part of [[clinical-pipeline/index]]. See also [[clinical-pipeline/muscle-preservation]], [[clinical-pipeline/glp1-beyond-weight]].

Trial-derived and regulator-derived risk only. No dosing or management protocols — clinical decisions belong with a prescriber. Rates below are per trial arm and duration as published.

---

## Gastrointestinal adverse events — the dominant, dose-limiting problem
`risk:gi-adverse-events`

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation. Mostly mild-to-moderate, mostly during dose escalation, mostly transient — but they are what drives discontinuation across the entire incretin class, and the rate scales with mechanism and potency.

**Discontinuation due to adverse events (mostly GI), by trial:**

| Trial | Agent | Duration | Active arms | Placebo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GLORY-1 | mazdutide | 48 wk | **1.5% / 0.5%** | 1.0% |
| ATTAIN-1 | orforglipron | 72 wk | **5.3–10.3%** | 2.7% |
| ATTAIN-2 | orforglipron | 72 wk | **6.1–9.9%** | 4.1% |
| TRIUMPH-3 | retatrutide | — | **9.8% / 13.5%** | 4.8% |
| TRIUMPH-4 | retatrutide | 68 wk | **12.2% / 18.2%** | 4.0% |
| ZUPREME-1 | petrelintide | 42 wk | dropouts **8.4%** | dropouts 13.6% |

Two things stand out. **Retatrutide's triple agonism carries the highest discontinuation burden measured so far** — up to ~18% in TRIUMPH-4 — which is the price of ~25–29% weight loss and is rarely mentioned alongside the efficacy headline. And **petrelintide (amylin monotherapy) is the only agent where dropouts were lower on drug than on placebo**, with vomiting less frequent than placebo; if that holds in phase 3 it is a genuinely different tolerability class, but it is phase 2 only.

A pooled meta-analysis of orforglipron gives AE discontinuation **7.8% vs 2.7%** placebo (RR 2.99, 95% CI 2.07–4.33), rising with dose. An indirect comparison suggested oral semaglutide had lower discontinuation than orforglipron — indirect, so weak.

## Lean-mass loss
`risk:lean-mass-loss`

Approximately **25%** of weight lost is lean mass (narrative range ~20–40%; much lower in some populations). Meta-analytic DXA lean mass **MD −0.78 kg (95% CI −1.39 to −0.16)**. **Evidence certainty graded low to very low**; data cannot exclude clinically relevant muscle loss in susceptible groups — older adults, pre-existing sarcopenia. DXA lean mass is not muscle, and lean-mass loss is not sarcopenia (which requires weakness/impaired function). **No trial has shown an incretin increases clinically defined sarcopenia, and no muscle-sparing agent has shown a strength or function benefit.** Full treatment in [[clinical-pipeline/muscle-preservation]].

## Gallbladder disease
`risk:gallbladder-disease`

Rapid weight loss plus reduced gallbladder motility. The clearest trial-arm figure in the 2025–26 data: **MariTide phase 2, gallbladder events in 1–6% of active obesity-cohort arms vs 0% placebo**, highest in the top-dose arm. A class-level quantitative synthesis was not retrieved in this research — **the magnitude of class risk is not characterised here** and should not be inferred from the MariTide figure alone.

## Pancreatitis
`risk:pancreatitis`

**ATTAIN-1 reported five cases of mild pancreatitis** in the orforglipron arms. This is a known class label warning. The incidence is low and the causal attribution per case is not established from these data; a class-level quantitative estimate was **not retrieved** in this research. Marked **incompletely characterised**.

## Increased heart rate
`risk:increased-heart-rate`

**ATTAIN-1 reported a modest increase in heart rate** with orforglipron, consistent with the class. Clinical significance over years of exposure is unestablished. Note that SELECT showed net MACE *benefit* in secondary prevention despite this, so the heart-rate effect has not translated into harm in the populations studied — but those populations were monitored, screened and had established CV disease.

## NAION — semaglutide-specific ocular signal
`risk:naion`

Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy, a cause of sudden, usually permanent, painless vision loss in one eye.

- **EMA PRAC began a review January 2025** (triggered by Icelandic reports) and **concluded June 2025**: NAION is a side effect of semaglutide medicines (Ozempic, Rybelsus, Wegovy) with frequency **"very rare" — up to 1 in 10,000 people**. Product information updated. PRAC advised that **if NAION is confirmed, semaglutide should be stopped**.
- Evidentiary basis: large epidemiological studies in adults with type 2 diabetes in which semaglutide users had roughly **double** the risk versus non-users.
- **WHO issued a safety alert 27 June 2025** and its advisory committee concluded the semaglutide Risk Management Plan should be revised to include NAION as a potential risk.
- **Genuinely contested.** The manufacturer stated the data do not suggest a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship. The **American Academy of Ophthalmology and NANOS issued a joint consensus statement not supporting the EMA recommendation at this time**, noting absolute NAION risk remains low even among semaglutide users.
- **No equivalent FDA label action was confirmed** in this research — marked **unverified** rather than absent.
- Harm-reduction relevance: the absolute risk is small, but the outcome is irreversible monocular vision loss, and it is **semaglutide-specific so far** — not demonstrated as a class effect. Grey-market semaglutide users have no prescriber monitoring and no label to read.

## Psychiatric signals
`risk:psychiatric-signal`

**Suicidal ideation / self-harm with GLP-1 agonists — currently not supported as causal:**
- **EMA PRAC concluded April 2024** that available evidence **does not support a causal association** between GLP-1 receptor agonists (dulaglutide, exenatide, liraglutide, lixisenatide, semaglutide) and suicidal or self-injurious thoughts and actions. EMA's own database study found no association.
- **JAMA Psychiatry meta-analysis (144 studies)**: no imbalance in the very low incidence of suicide and self-harm between GLP-1 RA and placebo arms of randomised trials.
- Observational meta-analysis (4 studies): **RR 0.568, 95% CI 0.077–4.205** — no significant difference, but the confidence interval is so wide the estimate is near-uninformative. PMC11823376.
- An umbrella review concludes the class is a safe option in diabetes/obesity.
- **Assessment: no causal signal established; precision is poor for rare events.** Not "proven safe", but the burden of evidence currently does not support the alarm.

**Psychedelic and dissociative agents — different risk structure entirely:**
- COMP005 (psilocybin): treatment-emergent AEs through week 26 in **84.8%** of the active arm vs **59.8%** placebo.
- MM120/DT120: most AEs mild-to-moderate and **confined to the dosing day**.
- The 2024 MDMA **Complete Response Letter** cited **safety concerns and inadequate adverse-event documentation** — a regulator-level finding that psychedelic-assisted-therapy trials under-captured harms. That critique applies with far more force to unsupervised use.
- **Esketamine is Schedule III and available only under a REMS** requiring in-clinic administration and post-dose monitoring. HPPD case reports exist in the lysergamide literature (e.g. PMC13539430).
- **Every positive psychedelic phase 3 delivered the drug with medical screening, a monitored session and psychological support.** The trials provide no evidence about use without those, and the risk profile outside them is not characterised by this evidence base.

## Hepatic — SARM-specific
`risk:liver-enzyme-elevation`

**Enobosarm (oral SARM) QUALITY trial: more active-arm participants had elevated liver enzymes**, reported by the sponsor as subsequently declining during the trial. Consistent with the known hepatic liability of oral SARMs and the main safety differentiator between enobosarm and the antibody-based muscle-sparing agents.

## Risks specific to unregulated supply
`risk:product-quality`

None of the above addresses the risks that come from the product rather than the molecule: wrong identity, wrong content, no sterility or endotoxin control, no cold chain, and no label. Every efficacy and safety figure in this area was generated with a characterised, regulator-inspected product, protocol screening (exclusions for e.g. medullary thyroid carcinoma / MEN2 history in the OSA trials), and clinical monitoring. **Transferring a trial efficacy number to an unregulated vial transfers none of that.** Agents in this area with **no completed phase 3 anywhere** — retatrutide, petrelintide, MariTide, amycretin, survodutide, every muscle-sparing agent — have no established long-term safety profile at all.

## Unknowns
- Long-term (multi-year) safety of triple agonism; TRIUMPH durations are 68–80 weeks.
- Whether muscle-sparing co-therapy preserves **function**, not just mass.
- Whether the amylin tolerability advantage survives phase 3 and larger n.
- Whether NAION is semaglutide-specific or a class effect.
- Pancreatitis and gallbladder incidence at a class level, quantitatively.
- Effects of discontinuation: weight regain is predominantly fat, and lean mass does not automatically return (the trevogrumab rebound data are the only direct evidence, phase 2).
