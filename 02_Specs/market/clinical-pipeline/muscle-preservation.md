# Lean-mass preservation during incretin weight loss (L1)

Part of [[clinical-pipeline/index]]. Related: [[clinical-pipeline/next-gen-obesity]], [[clinical-pipeline/risks]], [[clinical-pipeline/open-questions]].

## The problem, stated accurately

Incretin-induced weight loss is not pure fat loss. The commonly cited figure is that roughly **25% of weight lost is lean mass**, with narrative reviews giving a range of about **20–40%**; one MASLD-trial review found a much smaller share (~4%), so the spread across populations and methods is wide. A meta-analysis reports greater DXA lean-mass reduction with GLP-1 RA treatment, **MD −0.78 kg (95% CI −1.39 to −0.16)**. The "25–40%" framing often quoted as consensus is **not** a consensus figure.

Three qualifications that are routinely dropped:

1. **DXA "lean mass" is not muscle.** It includes water, connective tissue and organ mass. Reviews are explicit that DXA lean-mass decline should not be read as muscle loss without functional or imaging context.
2. **Lean-mass loss is not sarcopenia.** Sarcopenia requires demonstrated weakness or impaired function as well. No trial of an incretin has shown an increase in clinically defined sarcopenia.
3. **Certainty is low.** A 2026 systematic review in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism graded the evidence **low to very low certainty**, and concluded the data are *insufficient to exclude* clinically relevant muscle loss in susceptible populations — i.e. neither reassurance nor alarm is supported.

Populations flagged as higher-risk: **older adults** and those with **pre-existing sarcopenia** (scoping review PMC13272609). Some weight loss of lean mass is physiologically expected and partly appropriate — a smaller body needs less structural tissue. The open question is the margin beyond that.

Key reviews/analyses: PMID 42608323 (body-composition meta-analysis), PMID 42794486 (mechanisms, semaglutide vs tirzepatide), PMID 41022269 (sarcopenia narrative review), PMC13303403 (nutrition/exercise/monitoring strategies), PMC13566191, PMC12957034, PMC13090617 (MASLD), PMC12673431 (SEMALEAN — reported preserved lean mass and metabolic efficiency with semaglutide; single study).

**Non-drug comparator:** the only interventions with long-standing evidence for attenuating lean-mass loss during caloric deficit are adequate protein intake and resistance training. None of the agents below has been shown superior to, or to add on top of, an optimised exercise/protein programme in a head-to-head trial.

## The agents

All are **phase 2, all investigational, none approved for this indication anywhere.** Every readout below has **lean mass as the primary endpoint** — a surrogate. **No trial has yet shown improved strength, physical function, falls, fractures, or any clinical outcome.** That is the central caveat for the whole class.

### Bimagrumab — anti-activin type II receptor antibody (Lilly)
**BELIEVE**, phase 2b, adults with obesity without diabetes, combination with semaglutide, 48/72 weeks (reports differ by timepoint).
- Total weight loss: **−22.1%** (bimagrumab + semaglutide) vs **−15.7%** (semaglutide alone) vs **−10.8%** (bimagrumab alone).
- Fat-mass loss: **−45.7%** in the high-dose combination vs **−27.8%** semaglutide alone.
- **Lean mass: −2.9% combination vs −7.4% semaglutide alone**; bimagrumab alone *gained* **+2.5%**. At week 48: −2.6% vs −7.9%.
- AEs: mild-to-moderate **acne** and **muscle spasms** characteristic of this mechanism.
- Presented ADA June 2025; published in Nature Medicine (exact citation **not confirmed here**). NCT05616013.
- **Honest reading:** lean mass still *declined* in the combination arms. This is "less lean-mass loss", not prevention.

### Apitegromab — anti-pro/latent myostatin antibody (Scholar Rock)
**EMBRAZE**, phase 2, randomised double-blind placebo-controlled, with tirzepatide, **24 weeks**, ~51 per arm.
- **+1.9 kg lean mass retained vs tirzepatide alone = 54.9% preservation, p=0.001** (primary endpoint met).
- Total weight loss similar between arms: **−12.3%** (apitegromab) vs **−13.4%** (tirzepatide alone) — i.e. the lean-mass sparing did **not** come at the cost of weight loss, but also did not increase it.
- Serious AEs balanced (~2% per arm); AE-related discontinuations balanced; none attributed to apitegromab.
- Published in Nature Medicine; **PMC13375528**. Sponsor-funded with author disclosures.

### Trevogrumab — anti-myostatin antibody, ± garetosmab (anti-activin A) (Regeneron)
**COURAGE**, phase 2, with semaglutide. NCT06299098.
- Lower-dose portion, **52 weeks**: lean mass preserved at both 26 and 52 weeks (**primary endpoint met**) — **42.5%** preservation in the higher of the two low-dose arms and **20.5%** in the lower, relative to placebo + semaglutide. Lower-dose trevogrumab **did not add to weight loss**.
- A prespecified **MRI substudy** reported prevention of **70%** of semaglutide-associated muscle loss — this is the only imaging-based (rather than DXA) readout in the class, which strengthens it.
- Higher-dose arms at **26 weeks**: reduced lean-mass loss during treatment **and increased lean mass during rebound weight regain after semaglutide discontinuation** — the most interesting finding in this area, since post-discontinuation regain is predominantly fat.
- Presented EASD September 2026. Sponsor's own framing is that the signal **warrants further studies to confirm**. `[sponsor]`

### Enobosarm — oral selective androgen receptor modulator (SARM) (Veru)
**QUALITY**, phase 2b, **n=168**, older adults with obesity, added to semaglutide, **16 weeks**.
- **71% relative reduction in lean-mass loss** vs semaglutide + placebo (primary endpoint met); the company also quotes ~99% preservation for the lower of the two doses `[sponsor]`.
- The **higher dose added nothing** over the lower; the lower is the proposed phase 3 dose.
- Greater fat loss, but **similar total body-weight loss** to semaglutide alone.
- Blinded extension after stopping semaglutide: weight regain cut ~**46%** and fat regain prevented `[sponsor]`.
- Fewer GI AEs reported with the combination `[sponsor]`.
- **Safety signal:** more enobosarm-treated participants had **elevated liver enzymes**, which the company reports subsequently declined during the trial. This is the one class-specific red flag in this group and is consistent with the known hepatic profile of oral SARMs.
- Not peer-reviewed; figures are company-reported. A follow-on trial (**PLATEAU**, primary endpoint percent body-weight change at 68 weeks) is described in 2026 updates; a confirmed registrational phase 3 start was **not** found. Review: *Enobosarm in Muscle Wasting*, JCSM Communications 2026, doi 10.1002/rco2.70027.

**Note on enobosarm specifically:** SARMs are already one of the most heavily trafficked grey-market categories, and enobosarm is among the most-studied SARMs. A positive lean-mass readout alongside a GLP-1 is therefore the single most likely driver of grey-market SARM + GLP-1 stacking. Recorded as a market signal only.

## Cross-cutting assessment

| Agent | Mechanism | Partner | Duration | Lean-mass result | Functional outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bimagrumab | ActRII antibody | semaglutide | 48–72 wk | −2.9% vs −7.4% | none reported |
| Apitegromab | myostatin antibody | tirzepatide | 24 wk | +1.9 kg, 54.9% | none reported |
| Trevogrumab | myostatin antibody | semaglutide | 26 / 52 wk | 42.5% (DXA), 70% (MRI) | none reported |
| Enobosarm | oral SARM | semaglutide | 16 wk | 71% relative | none reported |

Consistency across four different mechanisms is genuinely encouraging for the biology. But: all phase 2; all surrogate endpoints; durations of 16–72 weeks against a lifelong therapy; none has shown a strength, function or fracture benefit; and myostatin/activin blockade has a **history of failure in sarcopenia and cachexia on functional endpoints** despite reliably increasing muscle mass. Mass is the easy part.
