# Open questions, contested claims and retrieval gaps

## Contested claims

**1. Are cathinone detections rising or falling in the US?**
NDEWS sentinel toxicology laboratory directors reported **decreases** in synthetic cathinone detections in the [July 2026 Sentinel Site Report](https://ndews.org/wordpress/files/2026/08/SSD-July-2026-08272026-pdf.pdf), and the April 2025 report noted cathinone detections sharply down since 2023. Commercial toxicology laboratories simultaneously report cathinones as **still the dominant synthetic stimulant class**, with α-PiHpP the most common single analyte in H1 2026. Both can be true — different referral populations, different test panels, different denominators. **Unresolved: no reconciled national estimate exists.** Treat any single-source US prevalence claim as population-specific.

**2. Does chronic cathinone use cause cardiomyopathy?**
**Not supported by current clinical evidence.** The scoping review base contains **one** cardiomyopathy case and two myocarditis cases in 40 published cases ([PMID 38411851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411851/)); the mechanistic support is in vitro (H9c2 cells, methylone and 3,4-DMMC, [PMC12656588](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12656588/)). **Resolved 2026-11-09, and it reinforces rather than overturns this reading.** The reduced-EF figures are real and the source is now pinned: Shukimbayeva A, Prilutskaya M & Mansurova J, **"Cardiac manifestations of post-acute withdrawal syndrome from a history of synthetic cathinone and opioid use"**, *Medical Journal of Malaysia*, **Nov 2024**, n=294 (183 cathinone, 111 opioid), ECG + transthoracic echo ([PubMed 39614787](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39614787/)). But the figures **run the other way from how they circulate**: reduced EF in **31.1% of cathinone users vs 44.1% of opioid users** — the *opioid* arm was worse. Same for LV hypertrophy (10.9% vs 17.1%); diastolic dysfunction equal at 23.5%. The QT difference is directionally as relayed but **clinically trivial (338.2 vs 334.8 ms, both normal)**. The cohort was in **post-acute withdrawal**, not acute intoxication, and there is no drug-naive control arm. **So this study does not support cathinone-specific cardiomyopathy, and anyone citing "31.1%" as a cathinone harm signal has dropped the comparator.** *Percentages taken from the indexed abstract; full text not retrieved.*

**3. Is there a distinct "synthetic cathinone-induced psychosis"?**
The 32-study meta-analysis establishes **association with psychotic symptoms** but its authors explicitly **could not conclude a distinct induced-psychosis entity**, for want of data on symptom duration ([PMID 38446172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38446172/)). A smaller 2025 review of 29 cases found features largely shared with amphetamine and cocaine psychosis. **Current best reading: cathinone psychosis is probably not phenotypically distinct from classical stimulant psychosis.**

**4. Three percentages in wide circulation that we could not source.** From a secondary clinical summary: synthetic stimulants implicated in **~25% of ED visits involving drug-related psychosis**; mephedrone accounting for **~20%** of stimulant-related psychotic cases; **25% of cathinone-psychosis patients transitioning to a schizophrenia-spectrum disorder**. **All three unverified — primary studies not located.** The transition figure is the most consequential and the most likely to be repeated into apparent fact.

**5. Is hydrafinil a modafinil analogue?**
**Contested, and probably not.** It is a fluorenol, structurally unrelated to modafinil's scaffold; the "analogue" framing appears to be marketing lineage. Its circulating pharmacology — ~39% greater wakefulness than modafinil in animals, DAT IC50 ≈ 9 µM (~59% weaker than modafinil), no CYP2C19 affinity — traces to **secondary relays of unlocated preclinical work attributed to Cephalon**. **Whole pharmacology picture marked unverified.** Note also the inference error it invites: weaker DAT engagement with greater behavioural effect implies an *uncharacterised* mechanism, not a safer one.

**6. Is hydrafinil on the WADA 2026 list?**
Flmodafinil and fladrafinil were confirmed added to **S6.A**. One third-party reproduction of the 2026 list also showed **hydrafinil** (and benfluorex) under S6.A; **not confirmed against WADA's official text.**

**7. Did telehealth stimulant prescribing cause a diversion problem, or was Done Global a specific criminal case?**
DOJ obtained convictions on specific alleged conduct — subscription model, advertising-driven intake, pay structures and clinical workflows alleged to reward prescribing volume. **No study was found quantifying inappropriate-prescribing or diversion rates across telehealth stimulant prescribing as a whole.** Neither the "pill mill" nor the "pure access" framing is evidentially supported as a general claim. Meanwhile DEA/HHS extended the flexibilities a fourth time, to 31 December 2026, with the special-registration rule unfinalised — the regulatory position is genuinely unsettled, not merely unclear.

## Evidence gaps that matter clinically

**8. No reliable human pharmacokinetics for any currently dominant cathinone.** No measured human plasma half-life was found for α-PiHP, α-PHP, 3-MMC, 2-MMC, N-isopropylbutylone or α-PiHpP. The "short half-life drives redosing" mechanism — which this knowledge base relies on to explain binge harm — rests on **user-reported effect duration, clinical observation and animal data**, not human measurement. The closest relevant data is mouse PK comparing α-PiHP and α-PVP ([NeuroToxicology 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0161813X25000567)). **This is the largest single gap in the area.**

**9. No per-exposure toxicity comparison between serotonergic and DAT-selective cathinones.** The inference that pyrovalerones are more dangerous per exposure while 3-MMC-type compounds generate more total presentations is **reasoned from pattern, not measured.** No head-to-head study was found.

**10. No peer-reviewed forensic characterisation of counterfeit *methylphenidate* tablets**, and no 2025–26 peer-reviewed quantitative analysis of counterfeit stimulant tablet content and dose variability. Current evidence is DEA press material, a 2025 State Department report, a 2023 Mexican pharmacy drug-checking study and local harm-reduction snapshots. The phenomenon is well attested; the **analytical literature is not.**

**11. No human efficacy or safety study for flmodafinil, fladrafinil or modafiendz.** The best human data for the first two is a **6-person single-20-mg-dose doping-control study** (DOI [10.1002/dta.70100](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/dta.70100)); for hydrafinil, a **3-person 50 mg pilot** (DOI [10.1002/dta.3137](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dta.3137)). Potency and side-effect comparisons against modafinil circulating in grey-market discussion have **no human comparative basis whatsoever.**

**12. Does precursor-side control work?** The EUDA assessed 3'-chloropropiophenone on the premise that scheduling it may reduce 3-CMC availability. **No post-implementation evaluation was found.** Given that every substance-level control in this area produced substitution rather than suppression, whether precursor control behaves differently is an important and genuinely open empirical question.

## Retrieval limits in this note set

**13. euda.europa.eu, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and cfsre.org were not reachable from this session** (network restriction, not absence). All EUDA, PMC and CFSRE figures here therefore come from **search-result summaries of those pages**, not from the documents themselves. The highest-value verification step is reading the EDR 2026 **synthetic stimulants** and **NPS** chapters directly, including the per-category tables for aminoindanes, piperazines and phenethylamines that could not be extracted.

**14. UNODC's detailed EWA trend tables are access-restricted.** The **four new cathinones notified to the EU EWS in 2025** figure is the firmest 2025 stimulant notification number obtained. The **per-class 2025–26 non-cathinone stimulant breakdown is not verified** — the aminoindane (9), aminorex-derivative (6) and piperazine (BZP + >20 analogues) counts come from UNODC's **2024** technical update and therefore predate the period of interest. **"No new signal found" in those classes is a retrieval limit, not a finding.**

**15. UNODC figures internally inconsistent across pages:** ">1 500 substances monitored" and "1 444 unique NPS recorded" appear in different 2026 UNODC materials. Unreconciled.

**16. One attribution to confirm:** the 2026 rat drug-discrimination and locomotor findings for **N-cyclohexyl butylone** (cocaine/methamphetamine-like, locomotor ED50 2.80 mg/kg) and **N-cyclohexyl methylone** (not cocaine-like, ED50 33.46 mg/kg, possible convulsant risk) appear to belong to [Front Pharmacol 10.3389/fphar.2025.1728824](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1728824/full), but **the DOI-to-content mapping was not confirmed.**

**17. Figures from secondary sources needing primary verification:** FDA shortage listing status as of October 2026 (19 of 73 listings unavailable; Adderall XR status disputed between sources) → check the FDA Drug Shortages database. DEA quota increases October 2025 and 2026 (~25% dextroamphetamine) → check the Federal Register. Done Global sentencing details (He 6 years + $1M; Brody 2 years + $1M) → check DOJ announcements. The ~19%/year growth in Dutch NPS poisoning incidence → check the primary *Addiction* paper. The 14-of-32-studies meta-analysis subset in Daswani et al. → check full text.

## What to watch next

1. **Which compound replaces 2-MMC** in the EU after the January 2026 control, and how fast Dutch drug-checking data register it. This is the best-instrumented substitution event in the world and the next data point in a five-step series.
2. **Whether α-PiHpP gets scheduled in the US**, and what succeeds it on the pyrrolidinophenone ladder.
3. **Whether the N-isopropylbutylone wave peaks on the NFLS 2–3-year cycle** — which would predict a successor appearing in Florida forensic data around 2026–27.
4. **Whether the 2026 cathinone tonnage exceeds 48.5 t**, and whether the India→Netherlands bulk import route persists after precursor controls.
5. **Whether a non-cathinone stimulant class breaks out.** Nothing suggests it in 2025–26, but attention is concentrated on opioids, so the detection threshold for a stimulant signal is currently high.
6. **Whether any human pharmacology appears for flmodafinil or fladrafinil** now that WADA listing has created an institutional reason to study them.
7. **Whether the FDA shortage is formally resolved**, and whether informal-supply substitution falls when it is.
