# Emerging stimulant NPS watchlist, 2025–26

**One-line summary:** New stimulant notifications in 2025–26 are overwhelmingly **cathinones** — and specifically *small structural steps* off already-controlled compounds; the non-cathinone stimulant classes (aminoindanes, piperazines, aminorex-type oxazolines, phenethylamines) remain monitored but show **no new class-level breakout this year**, and the genuinely new regulatory and surveillance energy has gone to synthetic opioids, not stimulants.

## Tier 2 — characterised, limited detections, plausibly next

- **N-cyclohexyl methylone** and **N-cyclohexyl butylone** — N-cycloalkyl cathinones. N-cyclohexyl methylone was **first identified in the US in 2022 in seized material in Florida**; N-cyclohexyl butylone was structurally elucidated earlier ([Forensic Sci Int 2024 NMR/chiral separation of N-cyclohexylmethylone](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S037907382400433X)). A 2026 rat drug-discrimination and locomotor study reported **N-cyclohexyl butylone as strongly cocaine- and methamphetamine-like (locomotor ED50 2.80 mg/kg)** while **N-cyclohexyl methylone was not cocaine-like and only weakly methamphetamine-like (ED50 33.46 mg/kg)**, with the authors suggesting the latter **may be *more* dangerous because of convulsant effects** despite weaker stimulant properties. *Attribution unverified:* this appears to be [Front Pharmacol 10.3389/fphar.2025.1728824](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1728824/full), but the DOI-to-content mapping was not confirmed. Both were described as **not yet legally controlled** in the retrieved material — verify current scheduling.
- **MDPHP and MDPiHP** — methylenedioxy pyrrolidinophenones, appearing in the same 2026 forensic case series as α-PHP/α-PiHP ([Forensic Sci Int 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0379073826001040)).
- **Further α-PiHP analogues** — a 2026 *Archives of Toxicology* integrated in silico/in vitro/in vivo structure–activity study of α-PiHP analogues (DOI [10.1007/s00204-026-04483-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00204-026-04483-z)) effectively maps the space that supply is likely to move into next.

## Non-cathinone stimulant classes: monitored, not breaking out

Per UNODC's [technical update on the challenge of NPS (2024)](https://www.unodc.org/documents/scientific/The_Challenge_of_NPS_A_technical_update_2024.pdf) — the most recent class-level enumeration retrieved:

| Class | Cumulative reported to UNODC | 2025–26 signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Aminoindanes** (e.g. MDAI, 5-IAI) | **9** substances | No new 2025–26 notification found |
| **Aminorex-type oxazolines** | **6** derivatives | No new 2025–26 notification found |
| **Piperazines** (BZP and analogues) | BZP plus **>20** analogues | No new 2025–26 notification found; historically cyclical |
| **Phenethylamines** | large group, counted separately | Notable as a **harm** class rather than a novelty class: third-largest contributor to Dutch NPS poisonings 2012–2025 |

**Important caveat:** this enumeration is from 2024 and therefore **predates the 2025–26 notifications**. The EDR 2026 NPS chapter contains per-category tables including aminoindanes and piperazines; the figures could not be extracted (euda.europa.eu was not reachable from this session and the published chapter should be read directly). **Do not read "no signal found" as "no new substances exist" in these classes** — read it as a retrieval limit. Flagged in [[stimulants/open-questions]].

One non-cathinone stimulant does appear in recent UNODC advisory text: **N,N-dimethylamphetamine**, mentioned among other NPS co-detected alongside synthetic opioids in a 2026 advisory. Single mention, incidental to an opioid report, not a stimulant-focused finding.

## Novel stimulant classes flagged this year

**No genuinely new stimulant chemical class was identified as flagged in 2025–26.** The 2026 international control actions went elsewhere: at the **69th session of the Commission on Narcotic Drugs**, two nitazenes were added to Schedule I of the 1961 Convention (effective **29 May 2026**) and MDMB-FUBINACA to Schedule II of the 1971 Convention (effective **25 November 2026**). UNODC's recent advisories are dominated by synthetic opioids — **cychlorphine** and related compounds, often mixed with fentanyl, heroin or cocaine.

This matters for a stimulant watchlist in two ways. First, **analytical and regulatory attention is concentrated on opioids**, which means a stimulant signal has to be larger before it is noticed. Second, **stimulant supply is increasingly contaminated by opioids** — the cocaine/opioid co-detection pattern means stimulant users carry opioid overdose risk whether or not they intend any opioid exposure. See [[stimulants/risks]].

## The structural rule for anticipating the next notification

Given the 2021–2026 record, the highest-probability next entries are not novel classes but:

1. **One more positional isomer** of the most recently controlled ring-substituted cathinone (2-MMC controlled → watch remaining methcathinone and halomethcathinone positions).
2. **One more carbon or branch** on the pyrrolidinophenone alkyl chain (α-PHP → α-PiHP → α-PiHpP → …), plus ring-methylated variants of each.
3. **One more N-alkyl or N-cycloalkyl substitution** on the methylenedioxy "-ylone" scaffold (pentylone → dimethylpentylone → N-isopropylbutylone → N-cyclohexyl variants → …).

Each arrives with no human pharmacology, is sold under the previous compound's street name, and is typically detected first by a single forensic laboratory months before any monitoring body reports it. **The surveillance lag, not the chemistry, is the main risk amplifier.**
