# Cardiac and psychiatric harms of stimulant NPS

**One-line summary:** The clinical literature on synthetic cathinones is dominated by **cardiac arrest, hyperthermia with rhabdomyolysis, and transient psychosis**; the best single explanation for why cathinone harm exceeds equivalent-intent use of classical stimulants is the **pharmacological combination of high DAT potency with a short duration of action**, which drives compulsive redosing and cumulative sympathomimetic load.

## 1. Cardiac and myocardial harm

The anchoring synthesis is **Cardiotoxicity After Synthetic Cathinone Use: Two Cases, a Case Series and Scoping Review** ([PMID 38411851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411851/), *Cardiovascular Toxicology* 2024, DOI [10.1007/s12012-024-09832-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12012-024-09832-x)). It has three layers:

**Scoping review — 30 articles, 40 cases:**

| Complication | n |
|---|---|
| Cardiac arrest | 28 |
| Ventricular tachycardia | 4 |
| Supraventricular tachycardia | 1 |
| STEMI | 2 |
| NSTEMI | 2 |
| Cardiomyopathy | 1 |
| Myocarditis | 2 |

**Poison-centre case series:** **222 mono-intoxications with cardiotoxicity** reported to the **Dutch Poisons Information Centre, 2012–2022**, of which **63% involved 3-MMC**. The commonly reported features were tachycardia, hypertension, palpitations and chest pain. The authors' own caveat matters: these were largely **self-reported exposures without toxicological confirmation**, so substance attribution is uncertain.

**Index case:** a 28-year-old man with life-threatening arrhythmias. Across the assembled cases, **14/42 (33%) had α-PVP as the only psychoactive substance identified** — one of the few places the literature gets close to isolating cathinone effect from polydrug confounding.

**How to read this.** The dominance of *cardiac arrest* (28/40) over *myocardial infarction* (4/40) and *cardiomyopathy* (1/40) tells you the acute mechanism is predominantly **catecholaminergic — arrhythmia, extreme tachycardia, hypertensive load, and hyperthermia-driven collapse** — rather than coronary thrombosis. Cardiomyopathy and myocarditis are real but rare in the published record. **The claim that cathinones commonly cause a chronic dilated cardiomyopathy is not supported by this evidence base**; the single cardiomyopathy case and two myocarditis cases cannot carry it.

Supporting and adjacent 2025–26 work:

- *Clinical Toxicology* 2026 review, **acute cardiovascular complications of sympathomimetic recreational drug use**, DOI [10.1080/15563650.2026.2701369](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15563650.2026.2701369) — the current general framework for this presentation class.
- **Mechanistic (cell) work, November 2025**: oxidative stress in the cardiotoxicity of **methylone and 3,4-DMMC** in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts ([PMC12656588](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12656588/)). This is in vitro; it proposes a mechanism, it does not establish clinical cardiomyopathy.
- **⚠️→✅ Citation identified and the direction corrected (2026-11-09).** The study is Shukimbayeva A, Prilutskaya M, Mansurova J, **"Cardiac manifestations of post-acute withdrawal syndrome from a history of synthetic cathinone and opioid use"**, *Medical Journal of Malaysia*, **November 2024** ([PubMed 39614787](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39614787/)) — a case-control study of **n=294** (183 cathinone, 111 opioid) with ECG and transthoracic echocardiography. The percentages are real but **point the opposite way to how they are usually relayed**: reduced ejection fraction was found in **31.1% of the cathinone group vs 44.1% of the opioid group** — i.e. **the opioid comparator fared worse**, so this study is *not* evidence of cathinone-specific systolic dysfunction. Left ventricular hypertrophy was likewise **lower** in the cathinone group (10.9% vs 17.1%); diastolic dysfunction was equal (23.5% in both). The QT difference, though reported as longer in the cathinone group, is **338.2 ± 28.5 ms vs 334.8 ± 33.5 ms** — about 3 ms, with both means in the normal range. Note also the population: patients in **post-acute withdrawal**, not acute intoxication. *Figures from the indexed abstract; full text not retrieved (PubMed article pages return a bot interstitial in this environment).*

## 2. Hyperthermia

Hyperthermia is the presentation most likely to kill quickly and is strongly associated with sustained redosing, physical exertion and hot environments.

The best-documented recent case is **α-PiHP-related hyperthermia in a young adult** (*Cureus*, 2 October 2024, DOI [10.7759/cureus.70708](https://www.cureus.com/articles/298195-management-of-suspected--pyrrolidinoisohexanophenone--pihp-related-hyperthermia-in-a-young-adult-a-case-report), PMC11530085): a **32-year-old man** who ingested a very large quantity of [[alpha-pihp]] in a suicide attempt, developing **hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis and acute kidney injury**. Clinicians could not cleanly separate serotonin syndrome from a sympathomimetic toxidrome; **cyproheptadine was unavailable and dantrolene was given, with a prompt fall in core temperature**.

Three caveats the authors themselves raise, and which this note repeats rather than softens: **α-PiHP was never confirmed on toxicology** (urine amphetamine screening only), the case is a suicide attempt rather than recreational exposure, and **the dantrolene suggestion rests on this single case report**. It is not a treatment protocol and should not be read as one. (No dosing information appears here by design.)

The triad to recognise is **hyperthermia → rhabdomyolysis → acute kidney injury**, with agitated delirium often preceding it. Reported causes of death across the cathinone literature include acute kidney failure, cardiovascular complications, multiple organ failure and serotonin syndrome.

## 3. Stimulant-induced psychosis

The best evidence is **Daswani, Choles, Kim & Barr, "A systematic review and meta-analysis of synthetic cathinone use and psychosis", *Psychopharmacology* 2024;241(5):875–896** ([PMID 38446172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38446172/), DOI [10.1007/s00213-024-06569-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-024-06569-x)):

- **32 studies** included in the systematic review (**14 in the meta-analysis** — figure taken from a preview, treat as approximate).
- Finding: **synthetic cathinone consumption is associated with risk of developing psychotic symptoms**, indicated by the prevalence of hallucinations and/or delusions.
- Crucial limitation, stated by the authors: because of **missing data on classifying factors, particularly symptom duration**, they **could not conclude that a distinct "synthetic cathinone-induced psychosis" exists**. The association is real; the causal/diagnostic entity is not established.

A smaller 2025 review of **15 studies covering 29 cases** of psychosis after synthetic cathinone use concluded that these presentations **share most features with amphetamine- and cocaine-induced psychosis** ([article](http://article.sapub.org/10.5923.j.ajmms.20251504.75.html)) — low-tier journal, tiny case base, but directionally consistent: there is no evidence of a cathinone-specific psychosis phenotype.

**Figures to treat with suspicion.** A widely repeated secondary summary states that synthetic stimulants are implicated in **~25% of ED visits involving drug-related psychosis**, that mephedrone accounts for **~20%** of stimulant-related psychotic cases, and that **25% of patients transition to a schizophrenia-spectrum disorder** ([Psychiatric Times](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/assessing-addressing-synthetic-cannabinoids-cathinones-neuropsychiatric-toxicological-emergencies)). **The primary studies behind these percentages were not located. Mark all three as unverified.** The 25% transition figure in particular is the kind of number that gets repeated into apparent fact.

Related systematic work on neurotoxicity: [PMC10093970](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10093970/) (*Int J Mol Sci* 2023) and [PMC11395625](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11395625/) (NPS acute and chronic psychiatric effects, 2024).

## 4. Binge pharmacology: why cathinones drive compulsive redosing

This is the mechanistic core, and it explains the clinical pattern better than any single toxicity.

**DAT/SERT selectivity governs abuse liability.** From the updated structure–activity review of synthetic cathinones (*ACS Pharmacol Transl Sci* 2024, [PMC11406692](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11406692/)) and the second-generation pyrovalerone cathinone pharmacology work ([PMC8348686](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8348686/)):

| Profile | Examples | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **DAT ≫ SERT** (pyrovalerone/pyrrolidinophenone type) | MDPV, α-PVP, [[alpha-php]], [[alpha-pihp]], α-PiHpP | Potent DAT + NET uptake inhibitors, negligible SERT; cocaine/amphetamine-like; **highest abuse liability and the compulsive redosing signature** |
| **Mixed / non-selective** | mephedrone (4-MMC), [[3-mmc]], methylone | Non-selective uptake inhibition **plus serotonin release**, MDMA-like entactogenic component alongside stimulation |
| **Larger substituents → SERT-favouring** | — | SERT selectivity rises with substituent size, and the SAR review notes this may come with **increased toxicity** rather than reduced risk |

Two important qualifications so this is not overstated:
- A **high DAT/SERT ratio is associated with greater abuse liability**, and the ratio predicts self-administration of cocaine analogues better than DAT affinity alone — but a **rhesus monkey study found reinforcing *strength* was not related to DAT or SERT selectivity** ([record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348438276_Reinforcing_effects_of_synthetic_cathinones_in_rhesus_monkeys_Dose-response_and_behavioral_economic_analyses)). The relationship is not linear, and "high DAT/SERT = maximally addictive" is an oversimplification.
- Reviews note that compounds blocking dopamine uptake with little serotonin effect **may carry abuse potential exceeding that of established stimulants of abuse**.

**Short duration is the other half.** For [[alpha-pihp]] specifically, mouse pharmacology and pharmacokinetics compared with α-PVP (*NeuroToxicology* 2025, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0161813X25000567)) found α-PiHP **somewhat less potent than α-PVP for locomotor stimulation, consistent with lower peak blood and brain concentrations** — i.e. within this family, potency tracks exposure. No reliable human plasma half-life was found for α-PiHP, α-PHP, 3-MMC or the newer -ylones. **This is a genuine evidence gap**, and it means the widely stated "short half-life" explanation rests largely on **user-reported effect duration and clinical observation, not measured human kinetics.** Marked unverified.

**What the combination produces clinically.** Reviews describe users of 4-MEC redosing repeatedly, with **difficulty stopping while more of the drug remains available as effects wear off**. The behavioural loop — strong dopaminergic reinforcement, rapid offset, dysphoric comedown, immediate availability — generates sessions of many hours to days. The resulting cumulative sympathomimetic exposure, sleep deprivation and dehydration are plausibly the proximate cause of most hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, agitated delirium and arrhythmia presentations, rather than any single large exposure. **Risk here is a function of session duration, not just substance identity.**

## 5. Population-level signal

Dutch Poisons Information Centre, 14-year retrospective, *Addiction* 2026, DOI [10.1111/add.70511](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/add.70511):

- All NPS poisonings: **32 (2012) → 829 (2025)**, total **n = 4 289**, with a strong rise from 2020 onward.
- **Cathinone poisonings: 6 (2012) → 326 (2025)**, total **1 573**.
- **Cathinones, benzodiazepines and phenethylamines together = 83% of cases.**
- **3-MMC alone accounted for 965 poisonings (22% of all NPS poisonings)** — the single largest specific substance; bromazolam next.
- *Unverified secondary:* an annual incidence increase of ~19%/year 2012–2025.

Earlier context: 3-MMC poisonings in the Netherlands rose from **1 in 2013 to 70 in the first half of 2021** (*Annals of Emergency Medicine* 2022, [fulltext](https://www.annemergmed.com/article/S0196-0644(22)00271-2/fulltext)).

**Serotonergic vs dopaminergic, and relative toxicity.** The DPIC data put the mixed/serotonergic compound **3-MMC** at the top of the poisoning count — because it is the most used, not because it is the most toxic per exposure. The DAT-selective pyrovalerones (α-PVP, α-PHP, α-PiHP) dominate the **fatality and cardiac-arrest** literature. The honest summary: **serotonin-releasing cathinones generate volume of presentations plus hyperthermia/serotonin-toxicity risk; DAT-selective pyrovalerones generate severity — agitated delirium, arrhythmia, death — at lower population exposure.** No head-to-head study establishing relative per-exposure toxicity was found.
