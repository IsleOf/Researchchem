# Cathinone churn: who dominates, who replaces whom

**One-line summary:** Synthetic cathinones are now the bulk-volume synthetic stimulant of the European market (48.5 tonnes seized/imported in 2024), and the defining dynamic on both sides of the Atlantic is *positional-isomer and N-alkyl substitution*: each control decision is followed within months by a structurally adjacent analogue sold under the old name.

## Europe: volumes and who is on top

Per the EUDA [European Drug Report 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/synthetic-stimulants_en) (published 9 June 2026):

| Metric (EU) | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Cathinone seizures + imports | 37 t | **48.5 t** (+11.5 t) |
| Cathinone production sites dismantled | 54 | **63** |
| Cathinone precursor chemicals seized | 2.1 t | **2.6 t** |
| Amphetamine seized | 10.2 t | 11.4 t (34 000 seizures) |
| Methamphetamine seized | 1.8 t | 6.1 t (14 100 seizures) |

The longer trend line from the [EDR 2025](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2025/new-psychoactive-substances_en) is 4.5 t (2021) → 27 t (2022) → 37 t (2023) → 48.5 t (2024), with preliminary data indicating continued large quantities in the first half of 2025. Most volume is a small number of **bulk imports from India routed through the Netherlands**, with EU-based processing concentrated in **Poland** (53 sites in 2023, 29 in 2022, 63 in 2024). In the 2024 European Web Survey on Drugs, 9% of respondents reported last-12-month synthetic cathinone use — a self-selected online sample, not a population prevalence estimate.

Cathinones are the **second-largest NPS group** monitored by the EU Early Warning System after synthetic cannabinoids: of >1 000 NPS notified 1997–2025, **181 are synthetic cathinones**, four of them notified for the first time in 2025 ([EDR 2026 NPS chapter](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)). The EUDA frames them as "affordable alternatives" standing in for MDMA, amphetamine and cocaine.

## The Dutch case: the cleanest measured substitution cascade

Hutten et al., *Addiction* 2026 ([PMID 42452938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452938/), DOI [10.1111/add.70538](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/add.70538)) used 13 years of DIMS drug-checking data (33 Dutch services, 2012–2024):

- [[3-mmc]] first appeared in 2012, immediately after 4-MMC (mephedrone) was scheduled — one sample that year.
- Samples **sold as** 3-MMC rose to **1 853 in 2024**.
- But **only ~10% of those samples actually contained 3-MMC in 2024, down from 85% in 2021** — the year the Netherlands criminalised possession (28 October 2021).
- The replacements named are **2-MMC, 3-CMC and 4-MMC**, plus more potent compounds.

So the ban did not remove the market; it swapped the molecule while the street name persisted. A separate systematic narrative review of 2-MMC/3-MMC in the Netherlands ([Int J Drug Policy 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0955395925002671)) makes the harm-reduction point directly: substances sold as 3-MMC are in fact 2-MMC, which misleads users and corrupts their own self-reports of what a given "dose" does. Dutch treatment demand for 3-MMC rose from 33 people (2021) to 330 (2023) *despite* the ban.

3-CMC's rise after MMC control is documented analytically in a chloromethcathinone isomer-differentiation paper subtitled "the new kid on the block after methylmethcathinone (MMC) control in the Netherlands" ([Forensic Chemistry 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468170924000511)). The EUDA's own precursor assessment identifies **3'-chloropropiophenone** as the chemical feeding 3-CMC supply and notes ≥12 EU 3-CMC production/processing sites dismantled ([EUDA precursor report](https://www.euda.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2026-02/euda-par-2024-3-chloropropiophenone.pdf)) — cited here as a control/monitoring fact only; no precursor or route information belongs in this knowledge base.

## The 2026 EU control decision — and the next isomer

In January 2026 the EU brought **three cathinones** under union-wide control: **2-MMC, 4-BMC (4-bromomethcathinone) and NEP (N-ethylnorpentedrone)**. The delegated directive entered into force **12 January 2026**, with Member States required to transpose by **12 July 2026**; formal risk assessments by the EUDA Scientific Committee were conducted in **May 2025** ([EUDA news](https://www.euda.europa.eu/news/2026/eu-takes-action-control-three-harmful-new-drugs_en); [EUDA initial report on 2-MMC, June 2025](https://www.euda.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2025-06/euda_initial-report-2-mmc_advanced-release_0.pdf)).

This is the predictable next turn of the wheel: 4-MMC controlled → 3-MMC → 3-MMC controlled (NL 2021, UN 2023) → 2-MMC and 3-CMC → 2-MMC controlled (EU 2026) → **unknown**. Watch 4-MMC re-entry, other ring-substituted methcathinones, and N-alkyl homologues of pentedrone. See [[stimulants/emerging-watchlist]].

## Pyrovalerones: α-PHP, α-PiHP and their isomer ladder

[[alpha-php]] and [[alpha-pihp]] are **positional isomers of each other** (hexanophenone vs isohexanophenone chain). Both are potent DAT/NET reuptake inhibitors with negligible SERT activity — pharmacologically cocaine-like rather than MDMA-like (review: *Life* 2024, DOI [10.3390/life14040429](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11051472/), PMC11051472).

- Scheduling: α-PHP → UN Schedule II in 2020; **α-PiHP → UN Schedule II in March 2023**.
- A forensic case series on α-PHP, α-PiHP, MDPHP and MDPiHP ([Forensic Sci Int 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0379073826001040)) reports **no cases positive for α-PHP** while α-PiHP postmortem blood ranged **0.3–840 ng/mL** — i.e. in that casework α-PiHP has effectively displaced α-PHP, and the two are only distinguishable with isomer-resolving LC-MS/MS. An earlier routine-casework comparison is in *J Anal Toxicol* 2024;48(5):299 ([link](https://academic.oup.com/jat/article-abstract/48/5/299/7630422)).
- **Ring-methylated α-PiHP isomers** are now in casework: Green et al. (2025) report 2-, 3-, and 4-Me-α-PiHP detected **37 times** in Swedish forensic toxicology including **5 autopsy cases**, with in vitro monoamine reuptake inhibition data ([record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395838120_In_vitro_monoamine_reuptake_inhibition_and_forensic_case_series_in_Sweden_of_the_synthetic_cathinones_2-_3-_and_4-Me-alpha-PiHP)). An α-PiHP-analogue structure–activity study appeared in *Archives of Toxicology* 2026 (DOI [10.1007/s00204-026-04483-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00204-026-04483-z)).
- **α-PiHpP (iso-PV8)** — one carbon further along — was first identified in **China in late 2024**, reported to UNODC, and first seen by CFSRE in **January 2025**; it was **not scheduled in the US** at the time of the CFSRE monograph (June 2025), which recorded a single toxicology case ([CFSRE monograph](https://www.cfsre.org/nps-discovery/monographs/alpha-pihpp)). NDEWS first flagged it in **Broward and Palm Beach Counties, Florida**, mostly in seized drugs ([NDEWS Sentinel Site Report, April 2025](https://ndews.org/wordpress/files/2025/06/2025.04_NDEWS-SSD-Full-Report.pdf)). By the first half of **2026**, one commercial toxicology laboratory reported α-PiHpP as the **single most common synthetic stimulant analyte** in its data ([Aegis 2026 mid-year NPS summary](https://www.aegislabs.com/services/novel-psychoactive-substances/2026-mid-year-nps-summary/)) — single-laboratory clinical data, not seizure data.

## United States: the "-ylone" wave and N-isopropylbutylone

A 2026 analysis of DEA **National Forensic Laboratory System** seized-drug data 2010–2024 ([PMID 42581582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581582/), *J Anal Toxicol*) groups cathinones into **"-ylones", "pyrrolidinophenones" and "-drones"** and describes successive waves in which each dominant compound is replaced **every two to three years**; dipentylone reports declined 2023→2024 as **N-isopropylbutylone** reports rose 2022→2024.

**N-isopropylbutylone** is the clearest 2025–26 US replacement event:

- First identified **August 2024**; >40 forensic samples, mostly **Florida**, by the time of CFSRE's **November 2025 public alert** ([PDF](https://www.cfsre.org/images/content/reports/public_alerts/Public_Alert_N-Isopropyl_Butylone_112825.pdf)). CFSRE explicitly ties its rise to the **decline of N,N-dimethylpentylone, which DEA controlled in August 2025**.
- Virginia DFS first detected it **March 2024** and had **170 controlled-substance cases through July 2025**, with **3 confirmed postmortem blood cases** (*J Anal Toxicol*, DOI [10.1093/jat/bkag037](https://academic.oup.com/jat/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/jat/bkag037/8700943)).
- Commercial clinical toxicology put it at **45% of synthetic stimulant detections in 2025**, the single most prevalent, with **eutylone just under 9%** and falling from ~10% in Q1 ([Aegis 2025 NPS summary](https://www.aegislabs.com/services/novel-psychoactive-substances/2025-nps-summary/)). Treat these as one laboratory's referral population, not national prevalence.

**Eutylone** is therefore a receding rather than rising compound in US data. Its peak harm signal is historical: CDC reported **343 eutylone-involved overdose deaths in 2020**, 259 of them in Florida and Maryland ([MMWR notes from the field](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9400538/)).

## The pattern, stated plainly

1. A compound becomes popular and visible.
2. It is scheduled nationally, then internationally.
3. Supply moves to the nearest legal neighbour — a **positional isomer** (4-MMC→3-MMC→2-MMC; α-PHP→α-PiHP→Me-α-PiHP→α-PiHpP) or an **N-alkyl variant** (pentylone→dimethylpentylone→N-isopropylbutylone).
4. The **street name does not change**, so users, clinicians and self-report research are all operating on stale identity information.
5. Each new analogue arrives with **no human pharmacology**, and often with higher potency per unit mass.

Conflicting signals to hold at once: **NDEWS sentinel toxicology lab directors reported *decreases* in synthetic cathinone detections** in the [July 2026 Sentinel Site Report](https://ndews.org/wordpress/files/2026/08/SSD-July-2026-08272026-pdf.pdf), while commercial labs report cathinones still dominating the synthetic stimulant class. Both can be true — different populations, different panels. Flagged in [[stimulants/open-questions]].

A naming-convention problem compounds all of this: the EUDA published a dedicated framework and practical guidance for naming cathinones ([Pulver et al., *Drug Test Anal* 2024, EUDA PDF](https://www.euda.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2025-02/drug-testing-and-analysis-2024-pulver-emcdda-framework-and-practical-guidance-for-naming-cathinones.pdf)) precisely because inconsistent naming of isomers makes surveillance data non-comparable.
