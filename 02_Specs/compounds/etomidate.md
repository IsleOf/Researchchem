# Etomidate (and analogues)

**Category:** [[depressants]]
**Legal / approval status:** Licensed **anaesthetic induction agent** in many countries, including the US, where it is **not CSA-controlled**. Not listed in any international drug convention as of Jan 2026 — but **under WHO/ECDD review** following the Aug 2026 Federal Register notice. Nationally controlled in Singapore (Class C, Sep 2025), Hong Kong (etomidate, metomidate, propoxate, isopropoxate controlled before the vaping problem emerged), New Zealand (Temporary Class Drug Order, briefing Jun 2026); **UK: ACMD recommended control under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 in its report of 21 Oct 2025 (not Feb 2026 — see below); the Home Office accepted on 19 Jan 2026 but has not yet legislated.**

## Why this one breaks the pattern
Every other story in this repo is clandestine chemistry racing regulators. Etomidate is the opposite: **an existing licensed medicine in a novel format**. No analogue design, no clandestine synthesis, no precursor chain. Early-warning infrastructure is built to detect *novel structures*; it is poorly pointed at *familiar medicines in new delivery formats*. If this route generalises, that is a structural blind spot. See [[market-structure/monitoring-systems]].

## Mechanism and the distinctive hazard
GABA-A positive modulator → sedation/anaesthesia, with **respiratory depression** as the proximate fatal mechanism. Not reversed by naloxone.

The hazard that sets it apart from other sedatives: etomidate **inhibits 11β-hydroxylase and suppresses adrenal cortisol synthesis**. This is well established in anaesthesia (a single induction dose measurably suppresses cortisol), which is why it is avoided in critically ill patients. Repeated recreational exposure therefore carries an **adrenal suppression / adrenal crisis** risk that has no parallel in the benzodiazepine or opioid sedatives — and which would not be suspected in an undifferentiated collapse. ⚠️ The clinical significance of repeated low-dose vaped exposure is **not characterised**; this is mechanism-based concern, not demonstrated epidemiology.

## Formats observed
- **Vapes / e-liquids** — sold as **"space oil"** or **"K-pods"**, the dominant format in East and South-East Asia. Concentrations unknown to the user.
- **Tablets and crystalline material** — UK drug checking, **sometimes sold as oxycodone**.
- **Co-detected with potent opioids** — UK drug checking found etomidate in at least **nine samples**, often alongside **protonitazene** and **diazepam**. That combination is the [[polydrug-risk]] pattern with a sedative that no strip tests for.

**Deaths — CORRECTED 2026-11-09.** The ACMD report states: "There have been published reports of **seven deaths related to etomidate or related compounds in Asia and two deaths in the United States**" (Chen 2024a, Detweiler 2014, Yum 2021, Qian 2025, Wong 2025; Molina 2008). So the published total is **nine, not seven** — the repo's "seven" dropped the two US deaths. Counts still vary by source and region; check primary government notices.

## UK regulatory status (verified 2026-11-09)

**The "Feb 2026" date in earlier versions of this note was wrong.** The sequence on GOV.UK is:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **21 Oct 2025** | ACMD report *A review of the evidence on the use and harms of etomidate and related compounds* **published** |
| **19/20 Jan 2026** | **Government response** published — letter from **Sarah Jones MP, Minister of State for Policing and Crime**, dated 19 Jan 2026 |
| **6 Feb 2026** | Report **re-issued with corrected chemical structures** — this is the only "Feb 2026" date, and it is a correction, not the review |

**Recommendation 1, verbatim:** "Whilst there is currently limited evidence of detection and/or use of etomidate and related compounds in the UK, due to the potential risk that the availability and use of these etomidate and related compounds will increase in the UK, the ACMD advises that **control of etomidate and related compounds via the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 is required**. The harms are broadly equivalent to those of other sedatives such as benzodiazepines, zopiclone or pregabalin, so **listing in Class C** is recommended. As etomidate is licensed for use in medical practice, it should be listed in **Schedule 4 (Part 1) of the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001**. As there are a range of simple variants of etomidate which could be or have already been encountered, **a small generic control, similar to that used in Hong Kong legislation, should be drafted and consulted on** to cover these compounds. Since those compounds, apart from etomidate, have no legitimate medical or other use, those covered by the generic control should be listed in **Schedule 1** of the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001. They should also be designated as controlled drugs to which **section 7(4)** of the 1971 Act applies."

**Government response, verbatim:** "I accept this recommendation and **will implement it when parliamentary time allows**. Before commencing the parliamentary process I will consult with stakeholders on adopting the definition used in Hong Kong's legislation."

**Status as of 2026-11-09: accepted but not in force.** No Misuse of Drugs Act amendment controlling etomidate has been confirmed as made. ⚠️ A **draft** *Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 (Amendment) Order 2026* adding etomidate to Class C appears on legislation.gov.uk with blank making dates, and the promised stakeholder consultation on the Hong Kong-style generic definition was **not located** — both remain **unverified**, and whether the Order has been laid or approved could not be established in this session.

The other three recommendations — e-liquid testing capability, international data sharing (UNODC EWA, a UK–EUDA working arrangement agreed at the May 2025 UK–EU Summit, US NDEWS), and public/professional information (FRANK, TOXBASE page in production) — were all **accepted**, some with funding caveats.

**WHO:** etomidate received a **preliminary (pre-)review at the 49th ECDD, 19–22 Oct 2026** ([WHO](https://www.who.int/groups/ecdd/forty-ninth-ecdd-documents)); the outcome is **not yet published**. See [[concepts/ecdd-watchlist]].

## Analogues
**Metomidate, propoxate, isopropoxate, butomidate**, plus halogenated variants and a cyclopropyl derivative (listed in the ACMD review of 21 Oct 2025). UNODC maintains an **"etomidate analogues" substance group**. The analogue space is the substitution route if etomidate itself is controlled — expect it to follow the pattern in [[market-structure/scheduling-churn]].

## Key risks
- Respiratory depression; **not naloxone-reversible**
- **Adrenal suppression** — unique to this agent among street sedatives; would not be suspected clinically
- Unknown concentration in vape pods
- Co-formulation with nitazenes and benzodiazepines
- **Absent from routine toxicology and all strip tests**
- Mis-sold as oxycodone → reaches people not seeking a sedative
- Vape format lowers the barrier to use and reaches younger users
