# Ipamorelin — claims vs evidence

| Marketing claim | What the evidence shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Releases growth hormone" | Correct. Demonstrated in rat pituitary cells and in vivo (Raun 1998, PMID 9849822) and in humans, with GH peaking ~0.67 h after IV dosing (Gobburu 1999, PMID 10496658). | **Supported** |
| "Selective — doesn't spike cortisol or prolactin like other GHRPs" | The original rat characterization found GH-release selectivity resembling GHRH rather than the non-selective GHRPs. That is real and is the compound's defining property. But it is a **rat pituitary-cell** finding; there is no adequate human dataset characterizing cortisol/prolactin across exposures. The widely repeated "no effect even at 200× ED₅₀" figure could not be verified against the primary source. | **Partly supported — rodent basis solid, human claim overstated** |
| "Builds lean muscle / reduces body fat / recomposition" | **No human trial of ipamorelin has measured body composition.** Not negative — nonexistent. The nearest class evidence, 12 months of MK-677, increased fat-free mass but did **not** reduce intra-abdominal adiposity and reduced insulin sensitivity (Nass 2008, PMID 18981485). Chronic ipamorelin in rats increased **body weight and food intake**. | **Unsupported — and the on-target orexigenic effect cuts against the fat-loss half** |
| "Improves sleep, recovery, healing, joints, skin, energy" | Never measured in any human study of ipamorelin. | **Unsupported** |
| "Anti-ageing" | No study in ageing, frailty or sarcopenia in any species. | **Unsupported** |
| "Clinically proven / backed by clinical trials" | One published RCT exists (n=117, postoperative ileus) and it **missed its endpoint**: time to first tolerated meal 25.3 h vs 32.6 h placebo, p = 0.15, no significant efficacy differences (PMID 25331030). A second, larger phase 2 (n=320 actual, NCT01280344) completed in May 2014 and **never reported results** — confirmed on the registry record itself (`hasResults: false`, no results-posted date) on 2026-11-09. | **Misleading — the trials exist and they are negative or unreported** |
| "Well tolerated in clinical trials" | Defensible but narrow: TEAEs were 87.5% on ipamorelin vs 94.8% on placebo over ≤7 days of IV dosing in 117 surgical patients. That is the entire controlled human safety dataset, and it does not describe repeated subcutaneous use over weeks or months. | **Supported for the exposure studied; over-extended otherwise** |
| "Builds bone density" | Real in rats: BMC increased by DXA over 12 weeks of continuous infusion, with tibial areal BMD up but total and vertebral areal BMD **unchanged** (Svensson 2000, PMID 10828840); longitudinal bone growth in Johansen 1999 (PMID 10373343). **Never tested in humans.** | **Animal only — site-specific even there** |
| "Natural, so safer than GH" | Acting through an endogenous receptor is not the same as being safe. Reduced insulin sensitivity is a class effect; FDA documented immunogenicity concerns specific to compounded ipamorelin acetate from aggregation and peptide-related impurities. | **Misleading** |
| "FDA-approved / FDA-reviewed and cleared for compounding" | Not approved anywhere. Was in **Category 2** (significant safety risks); the Sept 2024 removal from Category 2 was procedural pending review, not clearance; FDA reviewed it at PCAC in Oct 2024 on its own initiative and found it may raise safety concerns. No USP/NF monograph. Not on the 503A bulks list. | **False** |
| "Stacks synergistically with CJC-1295" | Pairing GHSR-1a agonism with GHRH-receptor agonism is a coherent two-pathway hypothesis. **No published human study has tested the combination** on any endpoint, including whether the GH response is even additive. | **Hypothesis only** |
| "Won't show up on a drug test" | Named explicitly in WADA S2, prohibited in and out of competition. | **False** |

## Pattern
Ipamorelin's distinctive problem is the inverse of the usual one. It is *not* an untested
compound — it went through real, well-designed, placebo-controlled human trials. Those
trials tested gastric motility, and the published one failed. The market then repurposed
the compound for muscle, fat loss and anti-ageing, uses for which **not one human study
exists**, while citing the clinical development programme as proof that it is
"clinically studied". The trials being real is used to lend credibility to claims the
trials never examined.
