# Ipamorelin — animal and in vitro evidence

The animal literature is the strongest part of the ipamorelin evidence base. Note the
pattern: robust, reproducible effects on GH release, bone and gastric motility in rodents,
which then **failed to translate** in the one human trial that tested a clinical endpoint
([[ipamorelin/human-evidence]]).

## Raun 1998 — original characterization (rat, in vitro + in vivo)
- **Model:** rat pituitary cell assay and in vivo rat GH release.
- **Outcome:** high GH-releasing potency; potency and efficacy similar to GHRP-6 in
  pituitary cells; GH-release **selectivity resembling that of GHRH**, which is the basis
  of the "first selective GH secretagogue" designation.
- *Eur J Endocrinol* 1998;139(5):552-61. PMID 9849822. doi:10.1530/eje.0.1390552

## Johansen 1999 — longitudinal bone growth (adult female rat)
- **Model:** adult female rats, three-times-daily dosing for 15 days (0, 18, 90, 450 µg/day).
  Bone growth measured as the distance between fluorescent tetracycline bands in the
  proximal tibial metaphysis.
- **Outcome:** longitudinal bone growth induced. Notably, treatment **did not** affect total
  IGF-1, IGF binding proteins, or serum markers of bone formation and resorption.
- **Interpretation:** a local growth effect without a measurable systemic IGF-1 signature in
  this model — relevant because it decouples the "it raises IGF-1" narrative from an
  observed tissue effect.
- *Growth Horm IGF Res* 1999;9(2). PMID 10373343

## Svensson 2000 — bone mineral content (adult female rat)
- **Model:** 13-week-old female Sprague-Dawley rats; ipamorelin (0.5 mg/kg/day), GHRP-6,
  GH, or vehicle delivered by **osmotic minipump for 12 weeks**.
- **Outcome:** ipamorelin and GHRP-6 **increased bone mineral content** by DXA. Tibial areal
  BMD increased; total and vertebral areal BMD were **unchanged**.
- **Interpretation:** real but site-specific skeletal effect over continuous 12-week
  exposure. Frequently miscited as "Johansen 1999" — the BMC finding belongs to this paper.
- *J Endocrinol* 2000. PMID 10828840

## Jørgensen / glucocorticoid-countering study (adult rat)
- **Model:** adult rats with glucocorticoid-induced suppression of bone formation.
- **Outcome:** ipamorelin counteracted the glucocorticoid-induced decrease in bone formation.
- *Growth Horm IGF Res* / related, 2001. PMID 11735244

## Venkova 2009 — postoperative ileus (rat)
- **Model:** rodent postoperative ileus model; intravenous dosing.
- **Outcome:** a single dose (1 mg/kg; GHRP-6 20 µg/kg as comparator) **decreased time to
  first bowel movement** but had **no effect** on cumulative fecal output, food intake or
  body-weight gain at 48 h. **Repeated** dosing (0.1 or 1 mg/kg) significantly increased
  cumulative fecal pellet output, food intake and body-weight gain.
- **Authors' conclusion:** postsurgical IV infusions "may ameliorate the symptoms in
  patients with POI" — the hypothesis that the human phase 2 trial then failed to confirm.
- Venkova K, Mann W, Nelson R, Greenwood-Van Meerveld B. *J Pharmacol Exp Ther* 2009.

## Greenwood-Van Meerveld 2012 — gastric emptying mechanism (rat)
- **Model:** rodent postoperative ileus; gastric emptying and gastric smooth muscle.
- **Outcome:** ipamorelin accelerated gastric emptying by stimulating gastric contractility
  through a **ghrelin-receptor-mediated mechanism involving cholinergic excitatory neurons**.
- *J Exp Pharmacol* 2012;4. PMID 27186127

## Chronic dosing in young female rats
- Chronic in vivo ipamorelin treatment stimulated **body-weight gain** and GH release in
  vitro in young female rats. Relevant to the appetite/weight dimension of ghrelin-receptor
  agonism ([[ipamorelin/risks]]) — note the endpoint is weight gain, not fat loss.

## Translation gap (explicit)
Rodent data support: GH release, increased bone mineral content with continuous exposure,
increased food intake and weight gain, and gastric prokinesis. The human trial of the
gastric-prokinesis endpoint was **negative** (p = 0.15). The bone and body-composition
endpoints have **never been tested in humans at all**. Rodent efficacy here is a reason to
hypothesize, not a basis for human claims.
