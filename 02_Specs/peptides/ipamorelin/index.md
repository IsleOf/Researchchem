# Ipamorelin

**Family:** [[gh-secretagogues]]
**Class:** Ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a) agonist — pentapeptide growth hormone secretagogue
**Approval status:** Not approved in any region. Development for postoperative ileus
abandoned after phase 2.
**Evidence tier:** 2 — one published phase 2 RCT (n=117) with a **negative** primary result,
one early human PK/PD study, plus a substantial rodent literature.

## Summary

Ipamorelin (Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂) is a pentapeptide developed at Novo Nordisk and
described in 1998 as the first *selective* GH secretagogue: in the original
characterization its GH-release selectivity resembled that of GHRH, in contrast to the
non-selective GHRPs ([[ipamorelin/mechanism]]).

Its clinical development was **not** for body composition or anti-ageing. It was developed
as a prokinetic for **postoperative ileus**, on the gastric-motility side of ghrelin
receptor pharmacology. That programme produced the single most informative human result
available: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial that **missed its
endpoint** (median time to first tolerated meal 25.3 h vs 32.6 h placebo, p = 0.15;
no significant efficacy differences). A larger phase 2 (NCT01280344, n=320) completed in
2014 and **never published results**. Development stopped there.
See [[ipamorelin/human-evidence]].

**There is no human body-composition, muscle, fat, strength, recovery or anti-ageing trial
of ipamorelin.** Not a negative one — none at all. The marketing use case has no human
evidence of any kind behind it.
