# CJC-1295 — risks

## The 2006 trial halt and participant death — what is actually established

This is the most consequential safety fact about CJC-1295, and it is also widely
misdescribed in both directions. Accurate version:

**Established:**
- ConjuChem's phase 2 study of CJC-1295 in HIV-associated visceral obesity
  (NCT00267527) was **stopped by the company on 17 July 2006** after the death of a
  study participant. The registry lists the study as terminated.
- The participant was at a study site in **Argentina**.
- Contemporaneous reporting described the death as occurring some hours after an
  injection, and stated that the cause of death and its relationship to the study drug
  were **under investigation** at the time.
- The CJC-1295 programme was **not resumed**. No efficacy or safety results from this
  trial were ever published.

**Not established:**
- **Causality was never publicly established.** No published adjudication attributes the
  death to CJC-1295.
- The specific cause of death is not confirmed by any primary source located. A
  secondary 2025 source states myocardial infarction while itself noting causality was
  debated; treat that as **unverified**.

**How to read it:** a single death in a small phase 2 trial, with unestablished causality,
is not proof of a lethal drug effect — but a sponsor-initiated halt and permanent programme
termination is a strong negative signal, and it means the compound's safety profile beyond
a few weeks of exposure has never been characterized by anyone. This is the opposite of
"well studied".

- Trial record: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00267527
- Contemporaneous report: aidsmap, July 2006,
  https://www.aidsmap.com/news/jul-2006/lipodystrophy-study-halted-after-patient-death

## Class risks of sustained GH / IGF-1 elevation

These are inferred from GH-axis pharmacology and from better-studied GH secretagogues
(see [[ipamorelin/risks]] and the MK-677 data below), not measured for CJC-1295 specifically.
Marked accordingly.

- **Fluid retention, peripheral oedema, carpal-tunnel-type symptoms, arthralgia**
  ([[risk:water-retention]]) — the classic dose-limiting effects of GH-axis stimulation in
  adults. *Inferred from class; not quantified for CJC-1295.*
- **Reduced insulin sensitivity / raised fasting glucose** ([[risk:insulin-resistance]]) —
  GH is counter-regulatory to insulin. In the best long-term randomized data for any GH
  secretagogue, MK-677 25 mg daily in healthy older adults raised fasting glucose by a mean
  0.3 mmol/L (5 mg/dL, p=0.015) and reduced insulin sensitivity over 12 months
  (Nass 2008, *Ann Intern Med*, PMID 18981485). A 2018 review of GH secretagogues as a class
  concluded they are generally well tolerated but flagged raised blood glucose from reduced
  insulin sensitivity as the principal concern (Sigalos & Pastuszak, *Sex Med Rev*
  2018;6(1):45-53, PMID 28400207). *Class evidence, strong direction, not CJC-1295-specific.*
- **IGF-1 elevation and theoretical neoplasia concern** ([[risk:igf1-cancer-theoretical]]) —
  higher circulating IGF-1 is associated with modestly increased risk of prostate cancer
  (OR 1.49, 95% CI 1.14–1.95 comparing 75th vs 25th percentile) and premenopausal breast
  cancer (OR 1.65, 1.26–2.08) in meta-analysis of observational studies
  (Renehan 2004, *Lancet* 363:1346-53, PMID 15110491). **This is an epidemiological
  association in untreated populations, not evidence that CJC-1295 causes cancer.** No
  carcinogenicity study of CJC-1295 exists. The 2018 class review explicitly lists
  long-term cancer incidence and mortality as an open question. Treat as a
  genuine-but-unquantified theoretical risk, not a demonstrated harm.
- **Pituitary trophic effect** — Alba 2006 found increased pituitary GH mRNA with
  immunohistochemistry consistent with somatotroph proliferation in mice
  ([[cjc-1295/animal-evidence]]). Long-term consequence in humans unknown.
- **Cardiovascular adverse events identified by FDA** — in its compounding safety-risk
  material FDA identified serious adverse events associated with CJC-1295 including
  increased heart rate and a systemic vasodilatory reaction, and stated that available
  clinical data are limited ([[cjc-1295/regulatory]]).

## Contraindications and interactions (pharmacologically expected)

- **Active or suspected malignancy** — stimulating the GH/IGF-1 axis is generally avoided;
  this is standard practice with GH therapy and applies by analogy.
- **Diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance** — the insulin-sensitivity effect is additive to
  existing dysglycaemia and may alter the effect of glucose-lowering therapy.
- **Concurrent exogenous GH** — redundant and additive on IGF-1.
- **Somatostatin analogues** (octreotide, lanreotide) — directly oppose the mechanism.
- **Glucocorticoids** — blunt GH responsiveness.
- **Hypopituitarism with somatotroph loss** — a GHRH-receptor agonist requires functioning
  somatotrophs; it cannot substitute for GH where the pituitary is absent or destroyed.

## Hormone-axis note
CJC-1295 acts above the pituitary and does not suppress the HPG axis
(it is not a [[risk:hpg-suppression]] risk). Sustained GH/IGF-1 elevation does, however,
produce IGF-1-mediated negative feedback on endogenous GHRH/GH regulation; the duration
of the DAC form means this feedback cannot be reversed quickly after exposure.

## Product-quality risk
Material sold under this name is unapproved and the DAC/no-DAC labelling is unreliable
(see [[cjc-1295/mechanism]]). FDA's own concern for peptides of this class centres on
immunogenicity from aggregation and peptide-related impurities. A buyer cannot know from
the label whether they have a days-long-acting albumin conjugate or a minutes-acting
fragment — a large difference in exposure. See [[grey-market-quality]].
