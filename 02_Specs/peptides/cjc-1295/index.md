# CJC-1295

**Family:** [[gh-secretagogues]]
**Class:** GHRH (GRF) receptor agonist — growth-hormone-releasing hormone analogue
**Approval status:** Not approved in any region. Development discontinued after a 2006 phase 2 halt.
**Evidence tier:** 2 — two small published human pharmacodynamic trials (n ≈ 11–60s), no outcome trials.

## Two different molecules sold under one name

This is the single most important fact about CJC-1295 and the main source of confusion in the
grey market:

| | "CJC-1295 with DAC" (true CJC-1295) | "CJC-1295 no-DAC" / Mod GRF (1-29) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Tetrasubstituted hGRF(1-29) **plus** a C-terminal Nε-maleimidopropionamide-lysine "Drug Affinity Complex" that binds covalently to Cys34 of circulating albumin | Tetrasubstituted hGRF(1-29) only (D-Ala², Gln⁸, Ala¹⁵, Leu²⁷), no DAC linker |
| Duration | Estimated half-life ~5.8–8.1 days in humans (Teichman 2006); GH elevated ≥6 days, IGF-1 for 9–11 days | Short-acting; minutes-scale, like other GRF(1-29) analogues |
| Human data | Yes — Teichman 2006, Ionescu 2006 | None under this name |

The two are routinely conflated, including in secondary literature. Any claim sourced from
Teichman 2006 or Ionescu 2006 is a claim about the **DAC** form only. See [[cjc-1295/mechanism]].

## Summary

CJC-1295 is a long-acting GHRH analogue developed by ConjuChem. It raises GH and IGF-1
in healthy adults in a sustained, dose-dependent way while preserving GH pulsatility
([[cjc-1295/human-evidence]]). Development stopped in 2006: the phase 2 trial in HIV-associated
visceral obesity (NCT00267527) was terminated after the death of a participant, with causality
never publicly established ([[cjc-1295/risks]]). No human trial has reported body-composition,
functional, or clinical-outcome benefit. Everything sold today is unapproved material.
