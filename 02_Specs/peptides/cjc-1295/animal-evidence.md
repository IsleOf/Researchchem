# CJC-1295 — animal and in vitro evidence

## Jetté 2005 — identification and PK (rat, in vitro)
- **Species/model:** in vitro DPP-IV stability and GH-secretion assays; subcutaneous
  administration to normal male Sprague-Dawley rats; Western blot of rat serum.
- **Outcome:** all three hGRF(1-29)-albumin bioconjugates were stable to DPP-IV and bioactive.
  CJC-1295 gave a ~4-fold increase in GH AUC over 2 h versus unmodified hGRF(1-29), was
  detectable in plasma beyond 72 h, and appeared on the albumin band from ~15 min and
  remained in circulation beyond 24 h. Acute GH secretion followed subcutaneous dosing.
- **Role:** this is the paper that defines CJC-1295 as a molecule. It is about the
  DAC-conjugated form.
- Jetté L, Léger R, Thibaudeau K, Benquet C, Robitaille M, Pellerin I, Paradis V,
  van Wyk P, Pham K, Bridon DP. *Endocrinology* 2005;146(7):3052-8. PMID 15817669.
  doi:10.1210/en.2004-1286

## Alba 2006 — growth normalization in GHRH-knockout mice
- **Species/model:** GHRH-knockout (GHRHKO) mice, treated from 1 week of age for 5 weeks,
  three groups at 24 h, 48 h or 72 h dosing intervals; placebo and heterozygous littermates
  as controls.
- **Outcome:** **daily** administration produced normal body weight and length. The same
  amount given every 48 h or 72 h was less effective — those animals grew more than placebo
  but growth was not fully normalized. Total pituitary RNA and GH mRNA increased, with
  immunohistochemistry consistent with somatotroph proliferation.
- **Interpretation:** strong proof of on-target GHRH-receptor pharmacology in a
  GHRH-deficient model. It is a rescue-of-deficiency result in growing knockout mice —
  it says nothing about effects in GHRH-sufficient adult humans, and is not evidence for
  recomposition or performance claims.
- Alba M, Fintini D, Sagazio A, Lawrence B, Castaigne JP, Frohman LA, Salvatori R.
  *Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab* 2006. PMID 16822960

## Note on somatotroph proliferation
The pituitary GH-mRNA and somatotroph-proliferation finding in Alba 2006 is the clearest
animal signal relevant to long-term safety: sustained GHRH-receptor agonism is a trophic
stimulus to the pituitary, not only a secretory one. No long-term animal carcinogenicity or
pituitary-histology study of CJC-1295 appears in the published literature.
