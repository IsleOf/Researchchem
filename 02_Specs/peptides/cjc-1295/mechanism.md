# CJC-1295 — mechanism

## Primary target

**GHRH receptor (GHRHR, [[target:ghrhr]])** — a class B1 GPCR on anterior pituitary
somatotrophs. CJC-1295 is an analogue of the biologically active fragment of human
growth-hormone-releasing factor, hGRF(1-29). Agonism raises somatotroph cAMP via Gs,
driving GH synthesis and release ([[effect:gh-release]]). Hepatic GH signalling then raises
IGF-1 ([[effect:igf1-elevation]]).

Because the stimulus is upstream of the pituitary, GH output remains subject to
somatostatin-mediated negative feedback and to the hypothalamic pulse generator. This is the
mechanistic basis for the observation that pulsatility is preserved
(Ionescu 2006 — see [[cjc-1295/human-evidence]]), and is the standard argument that GHRH
analogues are more "physiological" than exogenous GH. It is a mechanistic argument, not an
outcome finding.

No meaningful activity at the ghrelin receptor GHSR-1a; that is the target of
[[ipamorelin]] ([[target:ghsr]]).

## Structure and the DAC distinction

Native hGRF(1-29) is cleaved rapidly by dipeptidyl peptidase-IV (DPP-IV), which attacks the
Ala²–Asp³ bond of native GRF (Kubiak 1993, J Med Chem, PMID 8464043). CJC-1295 addresses
this in two layers:

1. **Four amino-acid substitutions** — D-Ala², Gln⁸, Ala¹⁵, Leu²⁷ — giving
   "tetrasubstituted GRF(1-29)", which resists DPP-IV cleavage and plasma proteolysis.
   This molecule alone is what the market calls **"CJC-1295 without DAC"** or
   **Mod GRF (1-29)**. It is short-acting.
2. **The Drug Affinity Complex (DAC)** — a C-terminal Nε-3-maleimidopropionamide-lysine.
   The maleimide reacts with the free thiol of Cys34 on human serum albumin, forming a
   covalent bioconjugate in circulation. Albumin's long residence time is what converts a
   minutes-scale peptide into a days-scale one. This is **true CJC-1295**
   (Jetté 2005, Endocrinology, PMID 15817669).

Jetté 2005 showed the conjugates were stable to DPP-IV in vitro, bioactive in a GH secretion
assay, and that CJC-1295 was detectable in rat plasma beyond 72 h, with Western blot placing
the peptide on the serum-albumin band from ~15 min onward.

## Pharmacokinetics (DAC form, humans)

- Estimated terminal half-life **~5.8–8.1 days** (Teichman 2006, PMID 16352683).
- After a single subcutaneous dose: GH elevated roughly 2–10 fold for ≥6 days;
  IGF-1 elevated roughly 1.5–3 fold for 9–11 days.
- Implication: the pharmacology cannot be titrated quickly. Any supraphysiological IGF-1
  excursion persists for over a week after a single exposure. See [[cjc-1295/risks]].

For the no-DAC / Mod GRF (1-29) form there is **no published human pharmacokinetic study**.
Short-duration figures circulating online (tens of minutes) are not traceable to peer-reviewed
primary data and should be treated as unverified.
