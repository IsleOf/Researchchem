# Targeted therapeutic peptides (L0 family hub)

Peptides that went through **real, registered clinical development programmes** with defined disease targets — unlike most "research peptides". Their existence in regulated trials gives unusually good data on what they do *not* do, as well as what they do. Evidence tier scale used here: 1 = phase 3 RCT/full approval; 2 = small or mixed RCTs / surrogate-based approval; 3 = preclinical/anecdotal.

| Compound | Target | Best-supported use | Status (2026-11) | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[ss-31/index\|SS-31 / elamipretide]] | Cardiolipin (inner mitochondrial membrane) | Barth syndrome muscle strength (surrogate) | **FDA accelerated approval 19 Sep 2025 (Forzinity)**; confirmatory RCT required. Failed phase 3 in mitochondrial myopathy, phase 2 in STEMI and GA | 2 |
| [[ara-290/index\|ARA-290 / cibinetide]] | Innate repair receptor (EPOR + CD131) | Sarcoidosis / diabetic small-fibre neuropathy (surrogate nerve endpoints) | Investigational; EU orphan designation (2013), US orphan reported; no phase 3 | 2 |
| [[pt-141/index\|PT-141 / bremelanotide]] | MC4R / MC3R (MC1R → pigmentation) | Acquired generalised HSDD in premenopausal women | **FDA approved 21 Jun 2019 (Vyleesi)**; modest effect | 1 |

## Cross-cutting lessons
- **"FDA-approved" ≠ approved for what it is marketed for.** Each approval is narrow (Barth syndrome ≥30 kg; premenopausal HSDD). Grey-market vials are not the approved product.
- **Negative trials matter:** SS-31's large MMPOWER-3 (n=218) trial found no fatigue/endurance benefit — directly contradicting "energy" marketing.
- **Surrogate endpoints dominate** for SS-31 (knee strength) and ARA-290 (corneal nerve fibre area).
- **WADA:** investigational ARA-290 falls under S0 (non-approved substances); SS-31 and bremelanotide are not named on the List (not verified against the official current List).
- None involves HPG-axis suppression; the main risks are injection-site reactions (SS-31), unknown long-term safety (ARA-290), and nausea / transient BP rise / hyperpigmentation / naltrexone interaction (PT-141).
