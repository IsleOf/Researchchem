# Etomidate (and analogues)

**Category:** [[depressants]]
**Legal / approval status:** Licensed **anaesthetic induction agent** in many countries, including the US, where it is **not CSA-controlled**. Not listed in any international drug convention as of Jan 2026 — but **under WHO/ECDD review** following the Aug 2026 Federal Register notice. Nationally controlled in Singapore (Class C, Sep 2025), Hong Kong (etomidate, metomidate, propoxate, isopropoxate controlled before the vaping problem emerged), New Zealand (Temporary Class Drug Order, briefing Jun 2026); UK ACMD **recommended** control under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 (Feb 2026).

## Why this one breaks the pattern
Every other story in this repo is clandestine chemistry racing regulators. Etomidate is the opposite: **an existing licensed medicine in a novel format**. No analogue design, no clandestine synthesis, no precursor chain. Early-warning infrastructure is built to detect *novel structures*; it is poorly pointed at *familiar medicines in new delivery formats*. If this route generalises, that is a structural blind spot. See [[market-structure/monitoring-systems]].

## Mechanism and the distinctive hazard
GABA-A positive modulator → sedation/anaesthesia, with **respiratory depression** as the proximate fatal mechanism. Not reversed by naloxone.

The hazard that sets it apart from other sedatives: etomidate **inhibits 11β-hydroxylase and suppresses adrenal cortisol synthesis**. This is well established in anaesthesia (a single induction dose measurably suppresses cortisol), which is why it is avoided in critically ill patients. Repeated recreational exposure therefore carries an **adrenal suppression / adrenal crisis** risk that has no parallel in the benzodiazepine or opioid sedatives — and which would not be suspected in an undifferentiated collapse. ⚠️ The clinical significance of repeated low-dose vaped exposure is **not characterised**; this is mechanism-based concern, not demonstrated epidemiology.

## Formats observed
- **Vapes / e-liquids** — sold as **"space oil"** or **"K-pods"**, the dominant format in East and South-East Asia. Concentrations unknown to the user.
- **Tablets and crystalline material** — UK drug checking, **sometimes sold as oxycodone**.
- **Co-detected with potent opioids** — UK drug checking found etomidate in at least **nine samples**, often alongside **protonitazene** and **diazepam**. That combination is the [[polydrug-risk]] pattern with a sedative that no strip tests for.

## Geography (as of Nov 2026)
| Region | Status |
|---|---|
| **Hong Kong / mainland China / Taiwan** | Origin of the trend; 5 case series covering **111 acute toxicity cases**; etomidate and 3 analogues already controlled |
| **Singapore** | Class C from 1 Sep 2025; **256 people referred to NAMS** for etomidate-vape misuse as of 4 May 2026; deaths officially confirmed; Feb 2026 operation seized 1,200+ pods |
| **UK** | Drug-checking detections (≥9 samples, tablets/crystal, some sold as oxycodone); ACMD review Feb 2026 recommends control; prevalence "currently limited" |
| **New Zealand** | Temporary Class Drug Order process, Jun 2026 |
| **Europe (wider)** | Reported spread since ~2023; detections sparse in the published record. **Solid forms, not a documented vape trend.** Weakest-evidence region — treat as emerging, not established |
| **North America** | Reported spread; Canada detections in solid forms |

**Deaths:** seven etomidate-related deaths in published reports (ACMD). Counts vary by source and region — check primary government notices.

## Analogues
**Metomidate, propoxate, isopropoxate, butomidate**, plus halogenated variants and a cyclopropyl derivative (listed in the ACMD review). UNODC maintains an **"etomidate analogues" substance group**. The analogue space is the substitution route if etomidate itself is controlled — expect it to follow the pattern in [[market-structure/scheduling-churn]].

## Key risks
- Respiratory depression; **not naloxone-reversible**
- **Adrenal suppression** — unique to this agent among street sedatives; would not be suspected clinically
- Unknown concentration in vape pods
- Co-formulation with nitazenes and benzodiazepines
- **Absent from routine toxicology and all strip tests**
- Mis-sold as oxycodone → reaches people not seeking a sedative
- Vape format lowers the barrier to use and reaches younger users
