# Market structure

**Scope:** how the NPS and grey-market-peptide markets behave, as documented by monitoring bodies, forensic labs and regulators. This area covers the *system* — scheduling, surveillance, testing, compounding policy, interdiction — not individual compounds.

**Audience:** harm reduction and monitoring. The question throughout is: *what do regulators and labs actually observe, and what does that mean for risk?*

## Summary
Four findings recur across every independent dataset in 2024–2026:

1. **Controls define, markets re-define.** Generic (class-wide) controls specify core structures; substances outside the written scope appear within months. The cleanest 2025–26 example is the EUDA attributing the rise of **orphines** to China's July 2025 nitazene class ban.
2. **The dominant product defect is mislabelling, not exotic adulteration.** WEDINOS found **36.6%** of purchase-intent community samples were not the expected substance in 2025/26. For grey-market peptides, **41.6–71.1%** failed basic purity/dose criteria depending on the standard applied, with **dose accuracy** the main failure mode.
3. **Surveillance is blind in exactly the places that matter.** Nitazenes are not in routine toxicology, degrade post-mortem, are absent from wastewater panels, and are detected by test strips in only ~78% of analogues. Peptides sit almost entirely outside all early-warning infrastructure.
4. **Licensure reduces but does not remove product risk.** Three US endotoxin recalls of compounded glutathione in 2026, traced partly to a shared dietary-supplement-grade raw-material source, plus a tirzepatide sterility recall.

## What changed in 2025–26
- **China placed the nitazene class under generic control (June/July 2025)**, and the EUDA has published the substitution response: **nine new orphines since 2024**, two under EU risk assessment. This is the best-documented analogue-substitution event in the current literature.
- **The first large dataset on grey-market peptide quality appeared** (April 2026 preprint, not peer-reviewed): **41.6% / 71.1%** failure depending on standard, **~15%** of endotoxin-tested samples endotoxin-positive, and critically **purity did not predict endotoxin (R²<0.01, P=0.75)**.
- **US compounding policy moved in two directions at once.** The GLP-1 shortage-driven compounding channel closed through 2025 and FDA proposed permanent 503B exclusion in April 2026; meanwhile **12 peptides were removed from Category 2** (April 2026, because nominations were withdrawn — **not** a safety clearance) and **PCAC recommended six of seven peptides** in July 2026, now contested by a citizen petition.
- **FDA rejected the "research use only" defence** in warning letters, judging intended use from the whole seller presentation including co-sold reconstitution diluent and dose calculators.
- **Research peptides became a freight-scale interdiction category**: CBP reported ~5,000 shipments in >300 master cartons at one port (announced March 2026).
- **Surveillance output grew while coverage gaps were formally acknowledged**: UNODC EWA passed 1,500+ substances; EUDA reached 1,050 monitored with 50 new in 2025; *Lancet Psychiatry* published the clearest statement yet that routine toxicology misses nitazenes.
