# US peptide compounding policy, 2024–2026

Up: [[market-structure/index]]

**One-line:** Between 2024 and 2026 the legal compounded-peptide channel narrowed sharply (GLP-1s off the shortage list) while a separate, slower process reopened a crack for research-style peptides (Category 2 removals and PCAC votes) — and the procedural nature of those removals has been widely misread as a safety clearance.

## The statutory machinery, briefly
- **503A** = traditional pharmacy compounding for an identified patient. **503B** = registered outsourcing facilities compounding at scale.
- A bulk drug substance may be compounded under 503A only if it has a USP/NF monograph, is a component of an approved drug, or appears on the **503A bulk drug substances list**. FDA maintains an **interim** list pending rulemaking, sorted into categories:
  - **Category 1** — nominated, sufficient supporting information submitted; FDA exercises enforcement discretion pending review.
  - **Category 2** — significant safety risks identified; effectively "do not compound."
  - **Category 3** — nomination lacked sufficient information to evaluate.
- The **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC)** gives **non-binding** advice on whether a nominated substance should go on the 503A list. FDA then acts, or does not.
- Separately, 503B facilities may compound from a **503B bulks list**; a drug on the FDA **drug shortage list** or discontinued opens additional compounding pathways, which close when the shortage resolves.

## Thread 1: GLP-1 shortage resolution closed the main compounded channel

|---|---|
| 2 Oct 2024 | FDA first declares tirzepatide shortage resolved; decision remanded after litigation |
| 19 Dec 2024 | FDA re-issues tirzepatide shortage-resolved determination |
| 21 Feb 2025 | FDA declares **semaglutide** injection shortage resolved |
| 5 Mar 2025 | 503A compounders told enforcement discretion for tirzepatide had passed |
| 19 Mar 2025 | 503B deadline to stop compounding tirzepatide |
| 24 Apr 2025 | 503A grace period for semaglutide ends |

Sources: [FDA, "FDA clarifies policies for compounders as national GLP-1 supply begins to stabilize"](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize); [Harris Beach Murtha summary](https://www.harrisbeachmurtha.com/insights/glp-1-weight-loss-drugs-off-shortage-list-deadlines-to-stop-compounding/); [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s); [Holland & Knight on telehealth advertising enforcement](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/fda-hhs-taking-action-against-telehealths-compounded-drug-advertising).

**Date caveat:** secondary legal summaries disagree on several of the 2025 wind-down dates (e.g. 503A tirzepatide given as both "immediately" after 5 Mar 2025 and later). FDA's own compounding and shortage pages are the authority.

### Effect on supply, as published
- Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are **not prohibited outright**: 503A pharmacies may still compound them for an individual patient on a valid patient-specific prescription, including documented clinical need for a variation from the approved product. What ended was the shortage-driven, large-volume, essentially-copies pathway — which was the pathway telehealth platforms used.
- Telehealth was the dominant distribution channel for compounded GLP-1s. A June 2026 clinical-press report states that rapid expansion of telehealth companies prescribing compounded GLP-1s produced a **surge of medication errors and adverse events**, with concerns about screening and counselling ([Rheumatology Advisor](https://www.rheumatologyadvisor.com/news/telehealth-booms-as-demand-for-glp-1s-surges-and-questions-mount-about-safety-oversight/)).
- Market scale context (industry, not peer-reviewed): GLP-1 sales ~$132B in 2025, +33.5% YoY, obesity indication growing fastest, volume-driven ([AMCP 2026 summary](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/glp-1-drugs-to-drive-growth-in-obesity-and-diabetes-treatment-amcp-annual-2026)); J.P. Morgan projects ~25M Americans on GLP-1s by 2030 vs ~10M in 2025 ([JPM](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/current-events/obesity-drugs)).
- **No peer-reviewed measurement of telehealth prescriber counts or of compounded-vs-branded share was located.** Published telehealth-market sizings are commercial forecasts and conflict with each other. Treat all quantitative telehealth claims as **unverified**.
- **The harm-reduction consequence is a channel shift, and it is not directly measured.** The closing of the compounded channel coincides with the grey-market "research peptide" expansion documented in [[market-structure/drug-checking-data]] and [[market-structure/enforcement-trends]], but no monitoring body has published a causal analysis linking the two. Mark the causal claim **unverified**.

## Thread 2: Category 2 removals (April 2026) — procedural, not exculpatory

- In April 2026 FDA announced the removal of **12 peptides from Category 2** of the interim 503A bulks list ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FDA-Announces-Removal-of-12-Peptides-from-Category-2-and-Schedules-PCAC-Meetings); [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/what-fdas-latest-actions-mean-for-peptide-compounding); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)).
- **The stated basis was that the nominators withdrew their nominations.** With no live nomination, there is nothing for FDA to categorise. This is an administrative tidy-up of a docket.
- **What removal does NOT mean:**
  - It does not place a substance on the 503A bulks list.
  - It does not move it to Category 1.
  - It does not authorise compounding.
  - **It is not a finding that FDA's earlier safety concerns were wrong or withdrawn.**
- Industry and vendor-adjacent commentary has widely presented the removals as a safety clearance or a green light. That reading is **not supported** by the stated basis of the action. This is the most consequential misreading in the 2026 peptide-policy story.
- Source discrepancies: counts given as 11 or 12; notice dates given as 15 or 22 April 2026. **GHK-Cu moved in the opposite direction** — removed from Category 1, with PCAC consultation expected before end of February 2027.

## Thread 3: PCAC 2024–2026

- **PCAC meeting 23–24 July 2026** considered seven peptides: **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, emideltide (DSIP), Semax, Epitalon**.
- Reported outcome: the committee **recommended six** and **rejected DSIP (emideltide)** ([ProfPeptide summary](https://profpeptide.com/news/fda-pcac-peptide-vote-july-2026); corroborated in outline by [SafeMedicines, Oct 2026](https://www.safemedicines.org/2026/10/october-26-2026.html)). The vote split and per-substance detail are **single/secondary-sourced — verify against the FDA PCAC meeting materials and transcript before relying on it.**
- **A further five peptides are scheduled for a PCAC meeting before end of February 2027.**
- **None of the six has been placed on the 503A compoundable list.** PCAC advice is advisory only; until FDA acts by notice or rulemaking, compounding these substances carries regulatory risk.
- **Pushback:** on 16 October 2026 the Partnership for Safe Medicines filed a **citizen petition asking FDA not to permit compounding of the six recommended peptides** ([SafeMedicines](https://www.safemedicines.org/2026/10/october-26-2026.html)). The petition cites the 2026 endotoxin recalls as evidence that compounded sterile peptide manufacturing is not reliably controlled — see [[market-structure/drug-checking-data]].

## Thread 4: FDA enforcement against "research use only" sellers

FDA's 2026 position, as expressed in warning letters, is that an **RUO or "not for human use" disclaimer is not a legal shield**; intended use is determined from the whole of a seller's presentation.

- **~31 Mar 2026** (published 7 Apr): a batch of CDER warning letters, reported as seven, to sellers of GLP-1 analogues.
- **24 Aug 2026** (posted 1 Sep): **five warning letters** to online peptide sellers, each following a July 2026 website review. Products named across the letters included **retatrutide, semaglutide, tirzepatide, tesamorelin, SS-31 and PT-141**, all carrying "research use only"/"not for human or veterinary use" labelling. FDA concluded the products were **unapproved new drugs** and, in some letters, **misbranded**.
- The reasoning is the load-bearing part: FDA judged intended use from the **whole website** — product descriptions, the co-sale of bacteriostatic water for reconstitution, and in two cases an on-site dose calculator. Disclaimer text was outweighed by the surrounding presentation.
- One tracker counts ~18 FDA peptide-related warning letters 2024 – Oct 2026, on its own inclusion criteria.
- **Seller names are deliberately omitted from this note** per the knowledge base's no-vendor rule. The letters are retrievable from FDA's Warning Letters database by date.
- **All of the above is from secondary legal/industry reporting; none was verified against fda.gov directly in this round. Treat counts and dates as unverified pending a primary check.** Also note: these are regulatory positions, not court rulings, and genuine laboratory RUO reagents are not the target.
- Related enforcement: a Utah physician was indicted in April 2026 in a case involving 200+ patients supplied with unapproved imported peptides ([SafeMedicines](https://www.safemedicines.org/2026/04/april-6-2026.html)).

## Net picture for a monitoring audience
1. The **legal** compounded-GLP-1 channel contracted substantially in 2025 and FDA moved in 2026 to close it permanently at 503B level.
2. The **research-peptide** channel is under active FDA enforcement on the intended-use theory, with the disclaimer defence explicitly rejected.
3. A **narrow, slow, conditional** legal path for several research-style peptides is being built through PCAC, but as of Nov 2026 **nothing has been placed on the 503A list**, and the path is contested by a pending citizen petition.
4. Meanwhile licensed compounders themselves produced three endotoxin recalls and one sterility recall in 2026, so "move to a licensed compounder" is a risk reduction, not a risk elimination.
