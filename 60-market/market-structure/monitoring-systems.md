# Monitoring and early-warning infrastructure

Up: [[market-structure/index]]

**One-line:** The NPS early-warning system is a set of loosely coupled national and international reporting layers, each with a different denominator, a different lag, and a different blind spot. Reading its output requires knowing which layer a number came from.

## The layers

### UNODC Early Warning Advisory (EWA) — global, voluntary reporting
- Aggregates NPS reports from member states, labs and partners into a cumulative substance register plus periodic *Current NPS Threats* toxicology reports.
- **Cumulative register:** 1,444 unique NPS as of March 2026; **more than 1,500** as of August 2026 ([UNODC, Aug 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/c08e6fbf-3f79-4654-8fe9-409e4b24bd59)).
- **Latest complete year (2024 data, published Dec 2025):** 688 unique NPS detected in drug samples, 101 reported for the first time; **38% of post-mortem cases involved two or more NPS** ([UNODC Dec 2025](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/c0e64537-e486-49ec-8b87-2c23a68f51b5); [Current NPS Threats VII](https://www.unodc.org/documents/scientific/Current_NPS_threats_VII.pdf)).
- UNODC states 2025 and 2026 data remain **preliminary**; it reports continued diversification across structural and effect groups rather than firm counts.
- Toxicology highlights: bromazolam most frequently detected designer benzodiazepine, then desalkylgidazepam. Nitazenes now reported across all regions.
- **Reading trap:** the cumulative register (1,500+) and the annual detection count (688) are different measures. They are routinely conflated in secondary reporting. Counts also are not comparable across publication dates.
- **Access gap:** the full EWA trend dataset is restricted to registered users, so independent re-analysis of the underlying data is limited.

### EUDA Early Warning System (EWS) — EU, legally mandated reporting
- Reporting obligation under the EU early-warning/risk-assessment framework; feeds the annual European Drug Report and formal risk assessments.
- **2025 reporting year:** 50 NPS detected in Europe for the first time, taking the EUDA's monitored total to **1,050**; **seven new synthetic opioids** notified ([European Drug Report 2026, 9 June 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en); [full PDF](https://www.euda.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2026-06/edr2026-09.06.2026-full-pdf-web-optimised_en.pdf)).
- ~400 NPS were detected in seizures in 2024 — i.e. the *active* market is a few hundred substances, not 1,000+.
- Growing EWS signal on **counterfeit medicines containing nitazenes**; orphines rising (nine new since 2024; two under EU risk assessment).
- At least **21 EU Member States** have reported nitazenes since 2019; deaths and acute toxicity in 2024 in Germany, Sweden and Norway.
- Concentration: in 2024, **five substances accounted for ~90% of the NPS quantity** seized by EU law enforcement, four of them cathinones. Quantity and diversity trend in opposite directions.

### NDEWS (US) — multi-stream national early warning
- NDEWS combines primary venue-based data collection, web/online monitoring, and EMS/911 data with secondary seizure and poisoning data ([NDEWS methods](https://ndews.org/novel-surveillance/web-surveillance/); [surveillance overview, PMC13025665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13025665/)).
- Publishes weekly briefings; a nitazene-focused briefing ran in Nov 2025 ([issue 229](https://ndews.org/newsletter/ndews-weekly-briefing-issue-229-this-weeks-focus-nitazenes/?frame=0)).
- Its own briefings state the central limitation plainly: analogues are missed because of low concentrations or novelty, which evades routine toxicological screening.

### RADARS / poison-centre and surveillance networks
- RADARS System is a subscription-based US surveillance programme drawing on poison-centre, treatment-centre and survey streams. **No 2025–26 RADARS output on NPS or peptides was located in this round of searching — treat its current coverage of these markets as unverified.**
- Do **not** conflate RADARS (US) with Public Health Scotland's **RADAR** (Rapid Action Drug Alerts and Response), a separate national alert system that has issued nitazene alerts ([PHS RADAR nitazene alert v1.5](https://publichealthscotland.scot/publications/rapid-action-drug-alerts-and-response-radar-alert-nitazenes/rapid-action-drug-alerts-and-response-radar-alert-nitazenes-version-15/summary/)).

### National forensic early warning and drug-checking networks
- **WEDINOS** (Public Health Wales) is the most transparent published national postal drug-checking dataset: 8,209 samples analysed in 2025/26 ([annual report](https://phw.nhs.wales/app/uploads/sites/2/2026/08/WEDINOS-annual-report-2025-to-2026.pdf)). Detail in [[market-structure/drug-checking-data]].
- Festival/city drug-checking services and forensic centres (e.g. CFSRE in the US) supply the fast analytical signal that national systems then aggregate. Coverage is uneven by country and by season.
- **Structural issue:** drug-checking submissions are a *self-selected* denominator. A rise in detections can reflect a change in who submits, not a change in the market.

### Wastewater-based epidemiology
- The EUDA/SCORE multi-city study is the main European instrument: newest round covers **130 cities in 28 countries**, one-week sampling between January and May 2025 ([EUDA wastewater topic](https://www.euda.europa.eu/topics/wastewater_en); [EUDA news 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/news/2026/%C3%A1guas-residuais-da-europa-contam-uma-nova-hist%C3%B3ria_en)).
- 2024→2025 change in cities reporting both years: **ketamine loads +~41%**, **MDMA loads −16%**; cocaine still rising in many cities.
- **Hard coverage limit:** the study tracks five established stimulants plus cannabis. **Cathinones, nitazenes and peptides are not in the panel.** Wastewater is therefore blind to exactly the substances this knowledge base is about. A Houston HIDTA report makes the same point for the US: nitazenes are not on the wastewater surveillance list ([Houston HIDTA report](https://com-phhp-epi-ndews.sites.medinfo.ufl.edu/wordpress/files/2025/11/Houston_HIDTA_Report.pdf)).

## Coverage gaps — the part that matters for risk interpretation

1. **Nitazenes are not in routine toxicology.** A 2026 *Lancet Psychiatry* commentary states that routine toxicology screens do not include nitazenes, leading to widespread undercounting, and that newer detection methods (immunoassays, test strips) have low specificity and sensitivity — **test strips detected only 78% of analogues and gave false positives with caffeine** ([Lancet Psychiatry 2026](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00120-3/fulltext)). CDC's Tennessee field report made the same observation: tests are often not included in standard toxicology panels ([PMC9484803](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9484803/)).
2. **Post-mortem instability.** The EUDA's 2026 report notes nitazenes degrade in post-mortem blood, so nitazene-involved deaths are probably undercounted even where tested for.
3. **US trend under-ascertainment.** A 2019–2024 US analysis found nitazene detections rising ~45% per year and called for prioritising validation of detection methods ([PMC13019326](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13019326/)).
4. **Non-fatal overdose is an explicit intelligence gap** for nitazenes (Houston HIDTA).
5. **Reference standards lag scheduling.** A substance with no certified reference standard cannot be confirmed quantitatively, so it is absent from data rather than absent from the market.
6. **Reporting-category migration.** Scheduled cathinones moved out of the NPS count into the amphetamine-type stimulant count (UNODC), producing an artefactual "decline."
7. **Peptides sit almost entirely outside all of the above.** No early-warning system routinely assays grey-market peptide products. Data come from ad-hoc academic studies, FDA recall notices and customs actions — see [[market-structure/drug-checking-data]] and [[market-structure/peptide-compounding-policy]].

## Practical reading rules
- Always ask: which system, which year, which denominator, and does the panel include the substance in question?
- A zero in any of these systems is usually "not looked for," not "not present."
- Cumulative registers only ever go up; they are not prevalence.
