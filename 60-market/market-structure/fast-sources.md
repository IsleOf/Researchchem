# Fast sources: reading the market at weeks, not years

**The lag problem.** Annual reports (UNODC WDR, EUDA EDR) publish 18–24 months behind the market. Drug-checking services publish weekly to monthly. If the question is "what is coming", the annual reports are the wrong instrument.

| Source | Cadence | Coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NIST RaDAR** newsletter | Monthly | US | Names first-time detections; **9 new compounds in Aug 2026 alone** (incl. 25B-NBOH, one West Coast sample; 2C-C, several) |
| **Drogart** (Slovenia) | Weekly | EU | Often first on new cannabinoids — reported **THC butanenitrile oximes (THO)**, a new semi-synthetic cannabinoid class, week 32 2026 |
| **Toronto Drug Checking** / Ontario | Weekly–monthly | Canada | Fastest on opioid adulterants; found ethylbromazolam >1 yr before regulators |
| **WEDINOS** (Wales) | Rolling + annual | UK | **36.6%** of purchase-intent samples not the expected substance (Apr 2025–Mar 2026); flagged orphines as emerging |
| **CFSRE public alerts** | Ad hoc | Global | Publishes when something significant lands |
| **Federal Register** ECDD review notices | Ad hoc | Global forward | Names substances about to receive international control — see [[market-structure/global-regional]] |

## The irreducible floor
Even fast services lag **~4–8 weeks**, because naming a compound requires a reference standard: unidentified peak → standard synthesised/obtained → structure confirmed → published. So "what is circulating right now" is genuinely unknowable. The closest available proxy is the **count of unidentified peaks**, which several services report.

## Novel adulterant classes found by fast services, 2026
- **Nefopam** (Toronto, Mar 2026) — **non-opioid** analgesic in 12/102 fentanyl samples; also Peterborough and Kingston. Not naloxone-reversible; lowers seizure threshold. A different hazard class entering the opioid supply.
- **Etodezitramide** — first Canadian detection.
- **Cychlorphine in counterfeit pharmaceutical tablets** (fake Percocet) — the route that reaches people who do not believe they are using street opioids.
- **Phenacetin** in cocaine, **bromazolam** in tablets sold as Xanax (Australia, Sep 2026: >1 in 5 samples contained unexpected substances).

## Signal to watch now
**Orphines are being independently flagged by both Welsh and Canadian services.** Independent multi-jurisdiction flagging is the pattern that preceded nitazenes becoming mainstream.
