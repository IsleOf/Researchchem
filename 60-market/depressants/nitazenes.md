# Nitazenes and the emerging "orphine" class (2025–26)

**Area:** [[depressants/index]] · **Compound node:** [[nitazenes]]

## Summary
Nitazenes (2-benzylbenzimidazole opioids) continued to diversify through 2025 and into 2026, but the defining development of this period is not more nitazenes — it is the arrival of a **second ultra-potent opioid class, the benzimidazol-2-ones or "orphines"**, which appear to be filling the space nitazenes occupied. Both classes share the same practical problem: they are not in routine toxicology panels, so deaths and non-fatal overdoses are systematically undercounted.

## 1. How many analogues, and which ones

- Secondary reporting of the UNODC Early Warning Advisory puts the count at **34 nitazene analogues reported by February 2026**, across at least 37 countries, up from ~26 analogues in 30 countries a year earlier. *Figures taken from trade/secondary coverage of UNODC EWA; the primary UNODC listing was not reachable from this session, so treat the exact numbers as **unverified**.*
- The EUDA reports **seven new synthetic opioids notified to the EU Early Warning System in 2025 — three nitazenes and three orphines** ([EUDA European Drug Report 2026, NPS chapter](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- Named analogues prominent in 2025–26 forensic literature:
  - **N-pyrrolidino protonitazene** (also called **protonitazepyne**) — identified in suspect tablets at an international mail facility ([J Forensic Sci 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1556-4029.70185)); first fatal **mono**-intoxication reported in Belgium in a 22-year-old ([case report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2665910726000459)). Commonly cited at roughly **25× fentanyl** on in vitro μ-opioid data — *that multiplier is widely repeated but should be treated as an in vitro figure, not a human equipotency ratio*.
  - **Protodesnitazene** — first UNODC EWA notification March 2025 (Canada), then Australia and the UK; tissue distribution described in [J Forensic Sci](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1556-4029.70478). In vitro it is **>13-fold less potent than protonitazene**.
  - **5-cyano isotodesnitazene** — first detection in street drugs came via a harm-reduction/drug-checking collaboration ([Sci Rep 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-35256-4)), a good illustration of [[drug-checking]] outrunning routine forensic labs.
  - **N-desethyl etonitazene** and **N-pyrrolidino isotonitazene** were placed in **Schedule I of the 1961 Single Convention** at CND's 69th session (March 2026), effective **29 May 2026** ([UNODC LSS announcement, March 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/475042d4-534c-4a06-b956-4531d4a72432)).

### Potency: what is actually published
- A structure–activity series across emerging 2-benzylbenzimidazoles found **most newly studied nitazenes had in vitro μ-opioid potency comparable to or exceeding fentanyl**, with only a subset (e.g. metonitazene, α'-methyl etonitazene in one dataset) clearly above it ([Arch Toxicol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00204-024-03774-7); [Pharmacol Res](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1043661824004481)).
- **"Desnitazenes" (5-nitro group removed) are generally 10–100× *less* potent** than their 5-nitro parents ([PMC13299982](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13299982/)). This matters for risk communication: not every new nitazene name is a step up in danger.
- The 2025 Clinical Toxicology review is explicit that **in vivo potency appears lower than in vitro assays suggest** ([Stangeland et al., Clin Toxicol 2025, PMID 40422647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40422647/)).

## 2. The orphines (benzimidazol-2-ones)
- **Brorphine**, detected in Europe ~2019 and the US in 2020, was the first potent opioid of this class. Control of brorphine is credited with pushing chemists toward newer analogues.
- Current members reported: **cychlorphine** (N-propionitrile chlorphine), **chlorphine**, **spirochlorphine**, plus related compounds such as SR-17018. CFSRE/Colombo Plan alerts describe the class and advise considering it when opioid toxicity is clinically evident but toxicology is negative ([CFSRE alert, "the orphines"](https://www.cfsre.org/nps-discovery/public-alerts/emerging-global-synthetic-opioid-threats-benzimidazol-2-ones-the-orphines); [Colombo Plan health alert, Feb 2026](https://www.cfsre.org/images/content/reports/public_alerts/Colombo_Plan_Orphine_Alert_Feb_2026_v3.pdf)).
- **Cychlorphine** is the most frequently reported orphine in UN monitoring. UNODC (May 2026) states early pharmacology shows **μ-opioid potency exceeding fentanyl**, with respiratory and cardiovascular depression in mice at lower doses than fentanyl, and reports **78 cychlorphine-related post-mortem cases** from the USA and UK ([UNODC LSS, May 2026](https://www.unodc.org/LSS/Announcement/Details/9403c3d1-12b9-49ac-8604-7da583d38d3b)). A 2025 Clinical Toxicology case report describes a non-fatal overdose predominantly attributable to cychlorphine and cites the related **chlorphine** at Ki 1.92 nM vs fentanyl Ki 6.29 nM ([Clin Toxicol 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15563650.2025.2594070)).
- A "**~10× fentanyl**" figure for cychlorphine circulates in press and treatment-centre coverage. **Unverified** — I found no primary study giving that ratio.
- Spread: Medscape (Oct 2026) reports orphine analogues detected across **22 US states** ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/potent-orphine-opioids-surge-us-illicit-drug-supply-2026a10011ys)). The EUDA began **reviews of cychlorphine and spirochlorphine in spring 2026** to inform whether formal EU risk assessments are needed.
- **Why now:** CFSRE and multiple commentators link the late-2025 nitazene decline and orphine rise to **China's generic (class-wide) scheduling of nitazene analogues in mid-2025**. This is a plausible and widely stated causal account but remains an **inference**, not a demonstrated causal finding.

## 3. Mortality data
- CDC data reported via STAT (June 2026): **confirmed nitazene-involved overdose deaths rose from 27 (2020) to 409 (2024)**; **>1,100 confirmed through SUDORS**, with experts estimating the true total since 2019 could approach **2,000** ([STAT, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/18/nitazenes-deadly-synthetic-opioids-rapid-spread-united-states/)). *These are secondary reporting of CDC figures; the primary SUDORS release was not verified here.*
- Earlier baseline: [MMWR, Nitazene-Related Deaths — Tennessee, 2019–2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9484803/); [MMWR, para-fluorofentanyl/metonitazene — Knox County TN](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9351528/).
- A 2025 UK study combining rat in vivo/ex vivo work with pharmacoepidemiological analysis of coroner-reported deaths argues **nitazene mortality is underestimated**, because post-mortem nitazene concentrations can fall **below laboratory detection thresholds**, producing false negatives ([Clin Toxicol 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15563650.2025.2601141)).

## 4. Naloxone response
Published guidance and reviews converge on: **naloxone works, but expect to need more of it and to watch longer.**
- The Clinical Toxicology systematic review (35 included studies) concludes naloxone **remains an effective antidote**, while cases of **prolonged toxicity** support **extended monitoring and repeated dosing**, and raise interest in longer-acting antagonists ([PMID 40422647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40422647/)).
- Published case series describe **cumulative titrated naloxone in the ~1–6 mg range** (e.g. ~1 mg protonitazene to ~6 mg metonitazene) versus the 0.4–0.8 mg often sufficient for heroin. This is reported as **observed case data, not a dosing protocol** — titration is a clinical decision.
- CDC's Tennessee work noted naloxone was effective but **multiple doses might be needed**, and that naloxone was administered in only **12/52 (23%)** fatal nitazene cases — later Tennessee SUDORS summaries report roughly **1 in 3**. The dominant failure mode in the mortality data is **naloxone not being given at all**, not naloxone not working.
- Australian clinical guidance on responding to nitazene toxicity: [PMC11910947](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11910947/).
- Clinical cohort experience: [Annals of Emergency Medicine, "Clinical Experiences With the Nitazene Class"](https://www.annemergmed.com/article/S0196-0644(25)01040-6/fulltext).

## 5. Detection gaps
- **Routine clinical immunoassays do not detect nitazenes or orphines.** Fentanyl immunoassays and fentanyl test strips do not reliably cross-react.
- **Nitazene test strips exist but are narrow.** UNODC has warned that nitazene immunoassay strips are developed around a **limited subset** of compounds — a negative strip does not exclude the class, and almost certainly does not cover orphines.
- Forensic capacity is the rate-limiting step: only a limited number of labs test for nitazenes, so SUDORS-type surveillance misses cases where the lab never looked. The same is now true, more acutely, for orphines.
- Reference-standard lag: new analogues cannot be quantified until standards are available, which systematically delays recognition of each wave.
