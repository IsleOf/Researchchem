# Other depressants: tianeptine, gabapentinoids, GABAergic NPS (2025–26)

**Area:** [[depressants/index]]

## Summary
Three loose groups sit outside the nitazene / designer-benzodiazepine / veterinary-adulterant story but contribute materially to depressant harm: **tianeptine** sold openly in retail as "gas-station heroin", **gabapentinoids** as overdose risk multipliers rather than standalone killers, and the **GABAergic NPS** space, which in 2025–26 was dominated by new benzodiazepines rather than by new GHB-like compounds.

## 1. Tianeptine ("gas-station heroin")
**What it is:** an atypical antidepressant licensed in some European, Asian and Latin American countries. It is **not approved for any use in the US**. At licensed doses its mechanism is unusual (glutamatergic/AMPA modulation); at the much higher doses associated with misuse it behaves as a **mu-opioid agonist**, which is where the opioid-like dependence, withdrawal and overdose risk comes from.

**FDA action:**
- FDA issued a consumer notice on **8 May 2025** describing tianeptine as **frequently available at convenience stores, gas stations, vape shops and online retailers** ([FDA, new "gas station heroin" tianeptine product trend](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/health-fraud-scams/new-gas-station-heroin-tianeptine-product-trend)).
- FDA guidance to clinicians: advise patients to avoid these products, use the Poison Help Line (1-800-222-1222) for adverse events, and report adverse events to FDA.
- Enforcement has used **warning letters and import alerts** to stop shipments at the border.
- Clinical coverage: [Medscape, 2025](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-issues-warning-gas-station-heroin-tianeptine-2025a1000bhh); [WAFP clinician summary](https://www.wafp.org/assets/files/WAFP%209-25%20Tianeptine%20Warning.pdf).
- State action: New Jersey health alert notes FDA's warning that **tianeptine use can lead to unintentional overdose when combined with other substances** ([NJ DOH health alert](https://www.nj.gov/health/populationhealth/documents/health_alert_tianeptine.pdf)).

**Poison-centre trend:** a secondary source citing FDA data reports poison-centre cases rising from **4 in 2013 to roughly 350 in 2024**. *The primary FDA/America's Poison Centers tabulation was not located in this session — **unverified**, though the direction is consistent across sources.*

**Why it matters for this area:** tianeptine is bought **legally, in retail, by people who are not seeking an opioid**. The resulting dependence is opioid-type, the withdrawal is opioid-type, and most clinicians and toxicology panels do not look for it. It is also a recurring co-exposure with concentrated 7-OH products — the same retail channel. See [[depressants/kratom-derivatives]].

## 2. Gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin)
The 2025–26 literature is consistent and fairly decisive on one point: **gabapentinoids rarely kill on their own, but they measurably increase the lethality of opioids and benzodiazepines.**

- **Systematic review update (2026, Drugs):** recent studies **consistently show concomitant gabapentinoid use increases opioid and polysubstance overdose risk and opioid-related mortality**; gabapentinoid-only overdoses are **generally only fatal in combination** with other substances ([Drugs 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40265-026-02342-w); [PMC13375660](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13375660/)).
- **PLOS Medicine self-controlled case series (2026):** co-use with **opioids raised drug-poisoning risk ~30%**, co-use with **benzodiazepines ~2-fold**; **no significant difference between gabapentin and pregabalin** ([PLOS Med 10.1371/journal.pmed.1005035](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1005035)).
- **Death-certificate data (older):** across 23 states and DC, 2019–2020, **nearly 90% of overdose deaths with gabapentin detected also involved an opioid**, increasingly illicitly manufactured fentanyls ([MMWR, PMC9098248](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9098248/)).
- **Poison-centre volume:** **124,161 exposures** with gabapentin or pregabalin as the primary substance reported to US poison centres, 2012–2022, with **suicide/self-harm a flagged concern** ([PMC11529557](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11529557/)).
- **Earlier cohort:** Ontario nested case–control found concomitant gabapentin + opioid exposure associated with **49% higher risk of opioid overdose death** ([PLOS Med 10.1371/journal.pmed.1002396](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1002396)).
- Inpatient case-control: [PMC10913887](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10913887/).
- **Gap:** I found **no national 2025–26 surveillance count of gabapentinoid-involved overdose deaths**. The most recent death-level US data located runs to 2019–2020; 2025–26 evidence is risk-association and review work. Treat any current national death figure for gabapentinoids as unavailable rather than low.

**Mechanistic note:** pregabalin and gabapentin bind the **α2δ auxiliary subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels**; they are **not** GABA-A agonists despite the name. That is why they are not reversed by flumazenil and not by naloxone. Their contribution to depressant death is **additive respiratory depression and sedation**, plus a dependence/withdrawal syndrome of their own.

## 3. GHB-like compounds and other GABAergic NPS
- **Searching 2025–26 monitoring sources did not surface a new GHB-analogue NPS class, nor any newly notified GHB-like or GABA-B-targeting sedative.** The GHB-analogue literature that exists is older medicinal-chemistry work (biaromatic 4-substituted GHB analogues; cyclic GHB analogues) rather than market surveillance. **This is a negative finding from a limited search, not a confirmed absence** — see [[depressants/open-questions]].
- GHB itself and its precursors **GBL** and **1,4-butanediol** remain the practically relevant compounds in this space; the harm profile (steep dose–response, abrupt loss of consciousness, severe withdrawal with delirium) is long-established and unchanged, and GHB withdrawal is one of the few depressant withdrawals comparable in severity to alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal.
- EUDA's "Other drugs" chapter is the right primary source to re-check for GHB trends ([EUDA EDR 2026, Other drugs](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/other-drugs_en)).

## 4. Newly notified sedative classes, 2025–26
- **EUDA: 50 new psychoactive substances notified for the first time in 2025** overall. Within that, the depressant-relevant notifications were **seven new synthetic opioids (three nitazenes, three orphines)** — see [[depressants/nitazenes]] — and continued benzodiazepine notifications (**40 total 2007–2025; 28 present on the market in 2024**) — see [[depressants/designer-benzodiazepines]] ([EUDA EDR 2026, NPS](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/new-psychoactive-substances_en)).
- **The headline is that 2026's genuinely new sedative class is the orphines, and the genuinely new adulterant is medetomidine.** No new GHB-like, barbiturate-like or alpha-2-agonist *class* beyond medetomidine/dexmedetomidine was identified in this review period.
- **Clobromazolam (phenazolam)** entered WHO ECDD **pre-review** — a benzodiazepine, so not a new class, but worth tracking because it is distributed specifically as counterfeit alprazolam.
- **BTMPS** is sometimes described as a "new sedative" in press coverage. It is not: it is an industrial additive with no established psychoactive effect. See [[depressants/veterinary-sedative-adulterants]].
