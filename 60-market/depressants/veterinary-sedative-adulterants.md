# Veterinary sedatives and industrial additives in the opioid supply (2025–26)

**Area:** [[depressants/index]] · **Compound nodes:** [[medetomidine]] · [[xylazine]] · [[btmps]]

## Summary
The most consequential change in the US opioid supply in 2025–26 is the **substitution of medetomidine for xylazine** as the dominant alpha-2 agonist adulterant, and the recognition of a **distinct, severe medetomidine withdrawal syndrome** that is refractory to standard opioid withdrawal treatment and is driving ICU admissions. Alongside this, **BTMPS**, an industrial UV stabiliser with no pharmacological rationale, is present in a large fraction of the fentanyl supply with essentially unknown human toxicity.

## 1. Medetomidine / dexmedetomidine as a fentanyl adulterant

### What it is
Medetomidine is a potent **alpha-2 adrenergic agonist** veterinary sedative; **dexmedetomidine** is its pharmacologically active (dex-) enantiomer and a licensed human ICU sedative. Street samples have reported both the racemate and the dex- form. It is substantially more potent as an alpha-2 agonist than [[xylazine]] and is **not an opioid** — naloxone does not reverse it.

### Prevalence 2025–26
- **Philadelphia** (highest-prevalence city): medetomidine detected in **90% of Q1 2026 drug samples where fentanyl was the primary drug** ([PDPH health advisory, 22 Jun 2026](https://hip.phila.gov/document/6386/PDPH-HAN-MWD-06.22.2026.pdf/)). This followed **72% of illegal opioid samples in the last four months of 2024**, during which **xylazine fell from 98% to 31%** ([MMWR Notes from the Field, PMC12045483](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12045483/)) — a near-complete substitution, not an addition.
- **National scale:** CDC reports **NFLIS medetomidine reports rose from 2,616 (2024) to 8,233 (2025)**, while still comprising **under 1% of all NFLIS drug reports** in 2025 ([CDC medetomidine situation summary](https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-prevention/situation-summary/medetomidine.html); [CDC HAN 00527](https://www.cdc.gov/han/php/notices/han00527.html)).
- **Geography:** CDC describes spread **expanding from the Northeast into the Midwest and South, with only low-level detections in the West**.
  - **Pittsburgh** — severe withdrawal cluster Oct 2024–Mar 2025 ([MMWR, PMC12045482](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12045482/)).
  - **Chicago** — early sporadic detections 2023–24 plus a **May 2024 overdose cluster**; fentanyl was detected in all medetomidine-positive samples in that cluster.
  - **Maryland** — a CDC sentinel site; medetomidine-related overdoses **July–August 2025** frequently accompanied by withdrawal signs.
  - **California** — only **small amounts** detected in San Francisco, San Diego, the Inland Empire and Los Angeles as of the CDPH brief, April 2026 ([CDPH emerging issue brief](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/sapb/CDPH%20Document%20Library/CDPHEmergingIssueBrief_Medetomidine.pdf)). LA County explicitly notes the severe withdrawal pattern **has not yet been observed locally** ([LAHAN advisory, Apr 2026](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/eprd/lahan/alerts/CDCMedetomidine040326.pdf)).
  - **Scotland** — RADAR alerts describe medetomidine most commonly in drugs sold as **heroin and as benzodiazepines**, and likely consumed unknowingly.
- **Caveat:** much of the clinical literature comes from overlapping Philadelphia/Pittsburgh cohorts. There is no good city-by-city national prevalence table; NFLIS counts are seizure-based and drug-checking coverage is uneven.
- **Dexmedetomidine specifically:** I found **no supply-side surveillance data distinguishing dexmedetomidine from racemic medetomidine as an adulterant**. Treat the dex-/racemate split in the illicit supply as **unknown**.

### Acute presentation
Bradycardia, hypotension (sometimes preceded by transient hypertension), profound sedation, and — the clinically important point — **sedation that does not respond to naloxone** when the alpha-2 agonist is the driver. Review: [Medetomidine as a Fentanyl Adulterant: Pharmacology, Detection, Clinical Presentation, and Management](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40429-026-00786-4).

### The distinct withdrawal syndrome
This is the novel clinical entity of 2025–26 and the reason medetomidine matters more than xylazine did.
- **Multicentre Philadelphia study, n=209** ([PMID 40747932](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40747932/)): **ICU admission in 77.5%**, **intubation in 20.1%**, and **73.7% received dexmedetomidine infusion**. Symptoms were frequently **refractory to standard opioid withdrawal treatment**.
- Penn Medicine reporting on the same work: in the final quarter of the study period, **a third of patients required ICU management** ([Penn Medicine news](https://www.pennmedicine.org/news/severe-withdrawal-cases-point-to-growing-medetomidine-use)).
- Phenotype: severe hypertension, tachycardia, agitation, vomiting and diaphoresis — i.e. an **adrenergic storm** on top of (not instead of) opioid withdrawal, which is what makes opioid-only management fail.
- MMWR case series: [Philadelphia, Sep 2024–Jan 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12045483/) and [Pittsburgh, Oct 2024–Mar 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12045482/).

### Published management principles (general level only)
- **Treat the opioid withdrawal too** — published guidance is explicit that successful management of medetomidine withdrawal **requires** concurrent management of opioid withdrawal.
- **Early alpha-2 agonist therapy is the mechanistic backbone** (replacing the alpha-2 agonism that was abruptly removed); in hospital this has predominantly meant **dexmedetomidine infusion**, with oral clonidine used in less severe and outpatient settings. Philadelphia outpatient guidance sets a **ceiling** on clonidine and requires **close blood-pressure follow-up after discharge**. No regimens are reproduced here.
- **Anticipate a higher level of care.** CDC advises considering higher levels of care for opioid withdrawal where medetomidine is involved. Benzodiazepines and antipsychotics are described as adjuncts, not the primary answer.
- **Diagnosis is clinical.** Medetomidine is **not in standard hospital toxicology panels**, which are immunoassay-based. Confirmation requires send-out LC-MS/MS, which arrives too late to guide care.
- Practitioner overview: [IRETA, Medetomidine: an emerging adulterant reshaping toxicity and withdrawal management](https://ireta.org/resources/medetomidine-an-emerging-adulterant-reshaping-toxicity-and-withdrawal-management/).

## 2. Xylazine's trajectory
- In the highest-prevalence city, xylazine has been **displaced**: Philadelphia opioid samples fell from **98% to 31% xylazine-positive** across late 2024 as medetomidine rose.
- **Nationally, there is no published evidence of a decline.** CDC's National Vital Statistics Reports (March 2026) covers 2017–2023 and lists xylazine among the top drugs in overdose deaths, with **6,033 xylazine-involved deaths nearly all also involving fentanyl** ([NVSR 75(1)](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nvsr/nvsr75/nvsr75-01.pdf); see also [VSRR 030](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/vsrr/vsrr030.pdf)). A CDC WONDER-based analysis found estimated xylazine prevalence in overdose deaths **doubled between 2021 and Q1 2024**.
- **Surveillance limitation:** xylazine has **no dedicated ICD-10 code**. Tracking relies on proxy codes (T42.7, T46.5), death-certificate free text, or SUDORS ([PMC13166611](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13166611/)). A 2026 NY Attorney General letter to Congress describes national statistics as widely underestimated due to limited testing.
- **Honest reading:** xylazine is clearly being *regionally* displaced by medetomidine in Northeast corridor cities, while national death data (which lags to 2023/early 2024) still shows growth. The two statements are compatible. Whether national xylazine prevalence has peaked is **genuinely unresolved** as of late 2026.
- Xylazine's signature harm — **severe necrotic soft-tissue wounds** — remains a major burden regardless of the adulterant shift, and it is **not yet clear** whether medetomidine carries comparable wound risk. See [[depressants/open-questions]].
- Background: [CDC, What you should know about xylazine](https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-prevention/about/what-you-should-know-about-xylazine.html); [PMC11622113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11622113/).

## 3. BTMPS
**BTMPS** = bis(2,2,6,6-tetramethyl-4-piperidyl) sebacate, an industrial **hindered-amine light stabiliser** sold as Tinuvin 770. It is not a drug and has no known recreational effect.
- **Emergence:** first identified in the illicit supply in **June 2024**; seen in **at least 22 states** ([DHS fact sheet CSAC-25-017, Jul 2025](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/25_0814_st_btmps_an_industrial_additive_in_the_illicit_drug_supply_fact_sheet.pdf)).
- **Quantities:** Shover et al. tested samples from Los Angeles and Philadelphia and found a **mean BTMPS-to-fentanyl ratio of 7.4** — most positive samples contained **more BTMPS than fentanyl by mass**. BTMPS-positive samples also had **lower fentanyl content (3.1% vs 8.7%)** ([JAMA 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2829939)).
- **Why it is there is unknown.** Published hypotheses: a change in synthesis or workup, deliberate bulking/dilution, or a shelf-life stabiliser effect (least evidenced). The authors explicitly decline to pick one.
- **Toxicity in humans is essentially unknown.** None of the sources located report clinical toxicity data; the JAMA authors list human health effects as an **open research question**. Concerns raised in the literature derive from non-clinical pharmacology of hindered-amine piperidines — including **nicotinic acetylcholine receptor blockade and calcium-channel effects**, and animal data suggesting potential **cardiac and ocular** effects — but these are **extrapolations, not human findings**. Anything stating a confirmed human BTMPS toxidrome should be treated as **unverified**.
- **Licit-supply signal:** a 2026 MATT conference recap reported **3.5% of 311 non-fentanyl prescription opioid samples** positive for BTMPS, attributed by the presenter to **leaching from plastic containers** ([MATT 2026 recap](https://axisfortox.com/matt-2026-recap-btmps/)). Conference summary, not peer-reviewed — **unverified**, but it matters because it means a BTMPS detection is not automatically evidence of illicit-supply contamination.
- Further: [Psychiatric News, Sept 2025](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2025.09.9.17); [Legislative Analysis and Public Policy Association fact sheet](https://legislativeanalysis.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/BTMPS-Fact-Sheet-FINAL.pdf).

## 4. The common thread
All three are **non-opioid substances arriving in an opioid-sold product**. Consequences:
1. **Naloxone cannot address them.** Sedation persisting after naloxone is now an expected finding, not an anomaly.
2. **They are invisible to routine toxicology** and to fentanyl test strips. Medetomidine test strips exist for some products but coverage is partial. See [[drug-checking]].
3. **Nobody chose to take them.** Unlike classic polydrug use, exposure is involuntary, so there is no self-titration and no awareness to inform clinical history.
