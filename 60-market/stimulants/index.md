# Stimulants: market and health overview (2025–26)

**Area summary.** Synthetic cathinones are now the bulk-volume synthetic stimulant of the European market — **48.5 tonnes seized or imported in the EU in 2024**, up from 4.5 tonnes in 2021 — and both the European and US markets are governed by the same dynamic: **each control decision is followed within months by a structurally adjacent analogue sold under the old street name**. The result is a market where the compound in hand is routinely not the compound named, arrives with no human pharmacology, and reaches clinicians before any monitoring body has reported it. Alongside this sits a four-year prescription stimulant shortage pushing demand into a counterfeit pill supply where "Adderall" has repeatedly been found to contain methamphetamine, and a modafinil-analogue grey market whose first real human data arrived in 2026 — as a doping-control study.

## What changed in 2025–26

1. **Volume kept climbing.** EU cathinone seizures and imports reached **48.5 t in 2024** (+11.5 t on 2023), with **63 production sites dismantled** (54 in 2023) and **2.6 t of precursor chemicals seized**. Most volume came from a small number of bulk imports from India routed through the Netherlands, with EU processing concentrated in Poland. Amphetamine seizures were 11.4 t; methamphetamine jumped from 1.8 t to 6.1 t. ([EDR 2026](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2026/synthetic-stimulants_en))
2. **The EU controlled three more cathinones.** **2-MMC, 4-BMC and NEP** came under union-wide control on **12 January 2026**, following May 2025 risk assessments. 2-MMC was the main 3-MMC substitute — so this control decision is itself the trigger for the next substitution.
3. **The substitution cascade was measured properly.** Dutch drug-checking data 2012–2024 show samples **sold as** 3-MMC rising to 1 853 in 2024 while **only ~10% actually contained 3-MMC, down from 85% in 2021**. The ban changed the molecule, not the market or the name. ([PMID 42452938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452938/))
4. **The US saw a clean, documented replacement event.** DEA controlled **N,N-dimethylpentylone in August 2025**; **N-isopropylbutylone** took its place, reaching 170 Virginia controlled-substance cases by July 2025 and 45% of one commercial laboratory's synthetic stimulant detections in 2025. Eutylone is receding, not rising.
5. **The eugeroics entered formal anti-doping.** **Flmodafinil and fladrafinil were added to WADA S6.A (non-specified stimulants) for 2026**, and the accompanying metabolism study found metabolites detectable for **up to ~2 weeks after a single 20 mg dose** — now the best human data that exists for either compound.

## The three things to take away

**1. Identity is the primary risk, not potency.** A street name no longer identifies a molecule. Past tolerance to "3-MMC" or "Adderall" does not transfer to this week's supply, and routine immunoassay panels do not detect these compounds while isomer discrimination requires methods most laboratories do not run.

**2. Severity concentrates in the DAT-selective pyrovalerones; volume concentrates in the serotonergic ones.** α-PVP, α-PHP, α-PiHP and α-PiHpP dominate the cardiac-arrest and fatality literature. 3-MMC dominates the poisoning counts — **965 poisonings, 22% of all Dutch NPS poisonings 2012–2025** — because it is the most used. No head-to-head per-exposure toxicity comparison exists.

**3. Session length is a risk variable in its own right.** High DAT potency plus short duration of action produces compulsive redosing; the resulting cumulative sympathomimetic load, sleep deprivation and dehydration are the plausible proximate cause of most hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, delirium and arrhythmia presentations — rather than any single exposure. Caveat held openly: **no measured human half-life exists for any currently dominant cathinone**, so the kinetic half of that explanation is inferred, not demonstrated.
