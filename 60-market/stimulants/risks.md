# Health risks specific to the stimulant NPS area

No dosing guidance appears here by design. The risks below are ordered by acuity.

## Acute, potentially fatal

| Risk | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `risk:cardiac-arrest` — sudden cardiac arrest and life-threatening arrhythmia | **Strong for case occurrence, weak for incidence.** 28 cardiac arrests and 4 ventricular tachycardias among 40 published cathinone cardiotoxicity cases ([PMID 38411851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411851/)) | The dominant severe cardiac outcome. 14/42 cases had α-PVP as the only substance identified, which is unusually clean attribution for this literature |
| `risk:hyperthermia` — hyperthermia progressing to **rhabdomyolysis and acute kidney injury** | **Moderate.** Documented α-PiHP case with rhabdomyolysis + AKI (DOI [10.7759/cureus.70708](https://www.cureus.com/articles/298195-management-of-suspected--pyrrolidinoisohexanophenone--pihp-related-hyperthermia-in-a-young-adult-a-case-report)); recurrent across the cathinone death literature | Strongly associated with prolonged redosing sessions, exertion and hot environments rather than a single large exposure. **Medical emergency; cooling is time-critical** |
| `risk:fentanyl-contamination` — unintended opioid exposure from counterfeit stimulant tablets or adulterated stimulant powder | **Strong.** DEA: 2 in 5 (2021) rising to 6 in 10 (2022) fentanyl-containing counterfeit pills carried a potentially lethal dose; US State Dept 2025 report; UNODC advisories describe opioids mixed with cocaine | The single highest-lethality risk in this area, and it is **not intrinsic to the stimulant** — it is a supply-contamination risk. Naloxone is relevant to anyone using non-pharmacy pills or powders |
| `risk:multi-organ-failure` — acute kidney failure, multi-organ failure, serotonin toxicity as reported causes of death | **Moderate.** Recurrent in cathinone review literature | Terminal common pathways of the above |

## Acute, serious

| Risk | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `risk:agitated-delirium` — severe agitation, delirium, violence toward self or others | **Moderate–strong.** Characteristic of the DAT-selective pyrovalerones in clinical reporting | The presentation most likely to delay recognition of concurrent hyperthermia |
| `risk:psychosis` — hallucinations and/or delusions | **Moderate for association, weak for causation.** 32-study meta-analysis found association with psychotic symptoms but **could not conclude a distinct cathinone-induced psychosis** for want of symptom-duration data ([PMID 38446172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38446172/)) | Published cases resemble amphetamine/cocaine psychosis. The claim that **25% transition to a schizophrenia-spectrum disorder** circulates widely but **the primary source was not located — unverified** |
| `risk:myocardial-injury` — STEMI, NSTEMI, myocarditis, cardiomyopathy | **Weak.** 2 STEMI, 2 NSTEMI, 2 myocarditis, **1 cardiomyopathy** across 40 published cases ([PMID 38411851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411851/)); in vitro oxidative-stress mechanism for methylone and 3,4-DMMC ([PMC12656588](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12656588/)) | **Explicitly contested here:** the evidence does **not** support a common chronic cathinone cardiomyopathy. One case cannot carry that claim. A reported 2025 echocardiography finding (reduced EF in 31.1% of cathinone users) **could not be verified — do not cite** |
| `risk:cardiovascular-strain` — tachycardia, hypertension, palpitations, chest pain | **Strong (common).** The modal presentation in 222 DPIC mono-intoxications with cardiotoxicity, 63% involving 3-MMC | Mostly self-reported without toxicological confirmation, so substance attribution is uncertain |

## Pattern-of-use and identity risks

| Risk | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `risk:compulsive-redosing` — binge sessions driven by high DAT potency plus short duration of action | **Moderate mechanistic, strong descriptive.** DAT/SERT ratio associated with abuse liability ([PMC11406692](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11406692/)); redosing-to-exhaustion described for 4-MEC and others | **The amplifier for every acute risk above.** Session duration, sleep deprivation and dehydration are the proximate drivers. Caveat: a rhesus monkey study found reinforcing *strength* unrelated to DAT/SERT selectivity, so the mechanism is not linear. **No reliable human half-life data exists for α-PiHP, α-PHP, 3-MMC or the newer "-ylones" — the "short half-life" premise rests on observation, not measurement** |
| `risk:substance-misidentification` — the compound in hand is not the compound named | **Strong, and quantified.** Only ~10% of Dutch samples sold as 3-MMC contained 3-MMC in 2024, vs 85% in 2021 ([PMID 42452938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452938/)) | **The defining risk of this area.** It also corrupts users' own accumulated experience: past tolerance to a street name does not transfer to this week's molecule |
| `risk:potency-escalation` — each successor analogue is often more potent per unit mass | **Moderate.** Isomer/analogue ladders documented in [[stimulants/cathinone-churn]]; α-PiHP is an exception (less potent than α-PVP in mice) | A habitual quantity carries a different pharmacological load after each substitution |
| `risk:no-human-pharmacology` — newly notified analogues arrive with no human efficacy, safety or kinetic data | **Definitional.** 4 new cathinones notified to the EU EWS in 2025; α-PiHpP, Me-α-PiHP, N-cyclohexyl analogues all characterised only in vitro, in animals or in forensic casework | **Absence of reported harm for a new analogue is a surveillance artefact, not a safety finding** |
| `risk:surveillance-lag` — a new compound typically appears in one forensic laboratory months before any monitoring body reports it | **Strong.** α-PiHpP: China late 2024 → CFSRE Jan 2025 → monograph Jun 2025. N-isopropylbutylone: Aug 2024 → alert Nov 2025 | Clinicians treating a presentation may have no reference data for the actual substance |
| `risk:analytical-invisibility` — positional isomers are indistinguishable without isomer-resolving methods; routine immunoassay panels miss these compounds entirely | **Strong.** Isomer-resolving LC-MS/MS required to separate α-PHP from α-PiHP; EUDA published naming guidance because inconsistent isomer naming makes data non-comparable | A negative screen does not exclude a cathinone; a "positive amphetamines" screen does not identify one |

## Treatment-access and market risks

| Risk | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `risk:counterfeit-stimulant-tablet` — tablets sold as Adderall or methylphenidate containing **methamphetamine** at unknown dose | **Moderate–strong.** DEA laboratory analysis; 9/45 samples sold as Adderall contained methamphetamine in a Mexican pharmacy drug-checking study; 74 000 methamphetamine-containing counterfeit "Adderall" pills in a single 2026 seizure | **Appearance, imprint and packaging do not authenticate a tablet** — the one point every source agrees on |
| `risk:treatment-interruption` — four years of discontinuous Schedule II stimulant supply (shortage listed Oct 2022, still active Oct 2026) | **Moderate**, mostly secondary sourcing | A shortage gap is a substitution event, and the available substitutes are riskier than the medicine |
| `risk:doping-sanction` — eugeroic metabolites detectable for up to ~2 weeks after a single small dose | **Strong.** Flmodafinil and fladrafinil added to **WADA S6.A non-specified stimulants** for 2026; metabolites detectable to ~2 weeks after a single 20 mg dose (DOI [10.1002/dta.70100](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/dta.70100)) | Non-specified status carries the stricter sanction tier. USADA notes these appear in supplements and "research only" products — exposure may be unintentional |
| `risk:unknown-long-term-eugeroic-safety` | **Absence of data.** No human efficacy or safety study found for flmodafinil, fladrafinil, modafiendz or hydrafinil | Modafinil-class concerns (CYP3A4 induction and oral contraceptive failure, rare serious rash, psychiatric effects) are assumed **by analogy only** |

## Population-level context

Dutch Poisons Information Centre, 2012–2025 ([DOI 10.1111/add.70511](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/add.70511)): all NPS poisonings rose **32 → 829**; cathinone poisonings **6 → 326** (1 573 total); cathinones, benzodiazepines and phenethylamines together were **83%** of cases; **3-MMC alone accounted for 965 poisonings (22%)**.

Read that last figure correctly: **3-MMC tops the poisoning count because it is the most used compound, not because it is the most dangerous per exposure.** The DAT-selective pyrovalerones dominate the fatality and cardiac-arrest literature at far lower population exposure. No head-to-head study of relative per-exposure toxicity was found.
