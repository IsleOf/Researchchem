# Eugeroics in 2026: flmodafinil, fladrafinil, modafiendz, adrafinil, hydrafinil

**One-line summary:** The grey-market modafinil analogues got their first proper *analytical* characterisation in 2026 — and that work, plus their addition to the WADA list, is now the single largest body of human data on them. Their **efficacy and safety pharmacology in humans remains essentially unstudied**.

## What changed in 2025–26

**1. WADA named them.** On the **2026 WADA Prohibited List**, **flmodafinil and fladrafinil were added to S6.A — non-specified stimulants**, the stricter of the two stimulant sub-classes (everything in S6 is a Specified Substance *except* S6.A). [[modafinil]] and [[adrafinil]] were already listed there. See [NADA Germany summary of 2026 changes](https://www.nada.de/en/service/news/news-detail/wada-publishes-prohibited-list-2026) and the [USADA 2026 athlete advisory](https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/2026-wada-prohibited-list/), which describes them as "potent analogs of modafinil and adrafinil" found "in dietary supplements or in products marketed as 'research only'". *Unverified:* one third-party reproduction of the 2026 list also shows **hydrafinil** under S6.A; this was not confirmed against WADA's official text and should be checked before relying on it.

**2. The 2026 detection study.** Krug et al., *Drug Testing and Analysis* 2026, DOI [10.1002/dta.70100](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/dta.70100) — "Investigations Into the Metabolism and Elimination of Flmodafinil and Fladrafinil for Sports Drug Testing Purposes":

| Design element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Subjects | **6 volunteers**, each a single **20 mg** dose of one compound |
| Matrices | urine + dried blood spots (DBS) |
| Platform | LC-HRMS; **LOD 0.2–4 ng/mL**, below WADA's MRPL |
| Main metabolites | **flmodafinil acid** and **flmodafinil sulfone** |
| Parent detection window | up to **12 h** |
| Metabolite windows | flmodafinil up to **8 days**; flmodafinil acid and sulfone up to **~2 weeks** |
| Key structural point | **Fladrafinil is a prodrug** — it yields the same flmodafinil metabolites, so the two compounds are not readily distinguished downstream |

Two things matter here beyond sport. First, this is a **very long elimination tail from a single small dose** — markers persist roughly 20–40× longer than the parent. Second, the authors themselves flag that these windows "call for careful result interpretation" of an adverse analytical finding, i.e. a positive does not date the exposure.

**3. This is now the best human kinetic data that exists for either compound.** That is the uncomfortable headline: a 6-person, single-20-mg-dose analytical study designed to catch athletes is the most informative human study of flmodafinil and fladrafinil in the literature.

## What human pharmacology exists vs is absent

| Compound | Human efficacy data | Human safety data | Human kinetics |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[modafinil]] | Yes — licensed, large trial base (narcolepsy, OSA, shift-work disorder) | Yes, incl. serious cutaneous reactions, psychiatric effects, CYP3A4 induction (oral contraceptive interaction) | Yes |
| [[adrafinil]] | Historical: approved in France then withdrawn; prodrug of modafinil | Limited; hepatic concern (transaminase elevation) is the stated reason for preferring modafinil — **primary trial evidence not retrieved here, treat as secondary** | Older doping-control work only ([PMID 19399790](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19399790/)) |
| [[flmodafinil]] (CRL-40,940, bisfluoromodafinil) | **None found.** No controlled efficacy trial in any indication | **None found.** No safety or tolerability study | **Yes, n=6, single 20 mg** — Krug 2026 only |
| [[fladrafinil]] (CRL-40,941) | **None found** | **None found** | **Yes, n=6, single 20 mg** — Krug 2026 only (as prodrug) |
| **Modafiendz** (bis-fluoro N-methyl analogue of modafinil) | **None found** | **None found** | **None found** |
| [[hydrafinil]] (9-fluorenol) | **None found** | **None found** | **One pilot, n=3, 50 mg** — Knoop 2021 |

**Claims that circulate without support:** that flmodafinil is "3–4× more potent than modafinil", that it has "fewer side effects", that fladrafinil is "more anxiogenic" or "more potent" than flmodafinil, and that modafiendz is a "cleaner" analogue. **No human comparative study supporting any of these was found.** The potency claims trace to preclinical and vendor-adjacent material, not to published human work. Mark all as unsupported.

## Hydrafinil: the strongest case of "no evidence"

[[hydrafinil]] (9-fluorenol) is widely sold as a modafinil analogue. Two corrections:

1. **It is probably not a modafinil analogue at all.** It is a fluorenol — structurally unrelated to modafinil's diphenylmethylsulfinylacetamide scaffold. The framing is contested and the "analogue" label appears to be marketing lineage rather than chemistry.
2. **There is no human efficacy or safety literature.** The only human data found is Knoop et al., *Drug Testing and Analysis* 2021, DOI [10.1002/dta.3137](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dta.3137): a **pilot of 3 healthy male volunteers given a single 50 mg oral dose**, run purely to characterise urinary metabolites for doping control (enzymatic glucuronide hydrolysis, liquid–liquid extraction, trimethylsilylation, GC-MS). After implementation into routine testing, **two routine doping control samples returned findings suggesting hydrafinil in early 2021** — evidence that it was in real-world use. The authors noted that environmental/occupational factors may need consideration at low concentrations, and recommended it "might require consideration in sports drug testing programs… if classified as prohibited by WADA".

The frequently repeated claim that fluorenol "induces wakefulness ~39% more than modafinil over 4 hours" derives from **animal work attributed to Cephalon**, relayed through secondary sources; the primary publication was not located. Its reported in vitro profile — a weak dopamine reuptake inhibitor, IC50 ≈ 9 µM, described as ~59% weaker than modafinil at DAT, with no CYP2C19 affinity — comes from the same secondary chain. **Mark the whole hydrafinil pharmacology picture as unverified secondary.** The inference people draw from it ("wakefulness without DAT engagement, therefore safe") is unsupported: a weaker DAT signal with greater behavioural effect implies an *uncharacterised* mechanism, not a benign one.

## Why this group is still lower-acuity than cathinones

No eugeroic in this set has a documented acute fatality pattern comparable to the pyrovalerone cathinones in [[stimulants/cardiac-and-psychiatric-harms]]. The realistic risk profile is: unknown long-term safety, no product-identity assurance in grey-market supply, modafinil-class concerns (CYP3A4 induction and contraceptive failure; rare but serious rash; psychiatric effects) assumed by analogy rather than demonstrated, and — newly concrete in 2026 — **a sanction risk for tested athletes that persists for up to two weeks after a single small dose**.
