# Clinical pipeline (L0)

The legitimate, trial-evidenced drug pipeline that the grey market tracks, reads selectively, and sells ahead of. This area exists so that claims encountered in catalogues and forums can be checked against what was actually measured, in whom, for how long, and with what dropout rate.

**Scope rule for this area:** efficacy is reported by **trial arm label and duration only** — no mg values, no titration schedules. Sponsor press releases are flagged `[sponsor]`; peer-reviewed papers and regulator documents are not. Unverified claims are marked. No vendor, marketplace or sourcing information is recorded anywhere in this area.

## What changed in 2025–26

Five things, in order of how much they reorder the field:

1. **GLP-1 became a pill.** FDA approved **orforglipron (Foundayo)** on **1 April 2026** — the first oral small-molecule GLP-1 for weight management, no food or water restriction, under the National Priority Voucher pilot and the fastest new-molecular-entity approval since 2002. This is an access and manufacturing change, not an efficacy change (−11.2% at 72 weeks), and it undercuts a major rationale for grey-market injectables.
2. **The efficacy ceiling moved to ~25%.** **Retatrutide TRIUMPH-1** (NEJM, n=2335, 80 weeks) reported **−25.0%** in the highest-dose arm vs −3.9% placebo — with the highest discontinuation burden in the class (up to **18.2%** in TRIUMPH-4). Still investigational everywhere.
3. **The indication map expanded onto surrogates.** Semaglutide gained **accelerated approval for non-cirrhotic MASH** (ESSENCE, histology, August 2025) on top of proven CV (SELECT) and kidney (FLOW) outcomes; tirzepatide holds the first-ever OSA approval. Meanwhile **EVOKE/EVOKE+ failed outright** in early Alzheimer's — the flagship neuroprotection hypothesis, falsified.
4. **Muscle-sparing co-therapy became a real category** — four mechanisms (bimagrumab, apitegromab, trevogrumab, enobosarm) all positive on lean mass in phase 2, and **none** with a strength or function result.
5. **Psychedelics got their phase 3 wins — and still no approval.** Two positive phase 3 trials for psilocybin (COMP005, COMP006), three for lysergide (Voyage, Panorama, Emerge), a JAMA phase 2b publication for MM120; while MDMA remains under a 2024 CRL with a resubmission in August 2026. Esketamine monotherapy (January 2025) is the only full approval in the space.

Also: **mazdutide** approved in China (June 2025, chronic weight management) — the first dual GCG/GLP-1 agonist approved anywhere; **elamipretide/SS-31** approved for Barth syndrome (September 2025) on a surrogate with its confirmatory trial **not yet under way at approval**; **CagriSema failed non-inferiority against tirzepatide** (REDEFINE 4) and is still unapproved; **EMA classified NAION as a very rare side effect of semaglutide** (June 2025), contested by AAO/NANOS.

## How to read efficacy numbers in this area

Three recurring traps, in descending order of how often they mislead:

- **Estimands.** "22.7%" (CagriSema) and "12.4%" (orforglipron) are full-adherence/efficacy estimands; "20.4%" and "11.2%" are the treatment-regimen numbers from the same trials. Grey-market and most secondary coverage quotes the larger figure with no caveat.
- **Population.** Every agent loses roughly a third of its effect in people with type 2 diabetes. A number quoted without the population is uninterpretable.
- **Discontinuation.** Efficacy headlines travel; the 4–18% AE-discontinuation rates do not. Read them together or not at all.

And one that applies to the whole area: every figure here was generated with a characterised product, protocol screening and clinical monitoring. None of that transfers to an unregulated vial.
