# Open questions and contested claims (L3)

Part of [[clinical-pipeline/index]].

## Contested or unverified in this area's notes

Flagged here so nothing is silently treated as settled:

1. **NAION and semaglutide.** EMA PRAC says "very rare" side effect, label updated, stop if confirmed; WHO concurred on adding it to the risk management plan. **AAO and NANOS jointly declined to endorse the EMA recommendation.** No equivalent FDA label action confirmed here. The epidemiology is consistent (roughly doubled risk in T2D cohorts) but confounding by indication is hard to exclude. **Unresolved.**
2. **MDMA resubmission content. CORRECTED.** FDA's Complete Response Letter of 9 August 2024 requested an additional phase 3 study (confirmed in search results: CBS News, Clinical Trials Arena). The earlier note that an **August 2026 resubmission was made without a new phase 3** is **not supported**: the August date matches the 2024 CRL, and a search found no 2026 reporting of a resubmission. Lykos said it intends to resubmit but had not confirmed whether it would run the additional trial. **Unverified; treat the 2026-resubmission claim as a likely date confusion.**
3. **BPC-157 regulatory status.** PCAC voted **8–6 with one abstention on 23 July 2026** to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulks list (confirmed across RAPS and law-firm summaries; FDA staff had recommended against). The vote is advisory; no FDA decision or rulemaking was found as of 2026-11-09, so BPC-157 is **not** on the list. Claims of compounding "under enforcement discretion" since October 2026 remain **unverified**.
4. **Whether any controlled BPC-157 trial is actually running.** Sources conflict directly: one vendor-run blog names a recruiting phase 2; other 2026 sources state none are recruiting. The NCT number was not confirmed against the registry and is deliberately not reproduced. **Unverified.**
5. **Retatrutide 104-week ~30.3% figure** in a BMI ≥35 subgroup — secondary coverage only, no primary publication located. **Unverified.**
6. **Amycretin ~22–25% weight-loss figures** — inconsistent with sponsor-reported phase 2 numbers; likely early-phase or aggregator error. **Unverified.** Phase 3 trial names (AMAZE, AMBITION, AMBIENCE) also unverified.
7. **Elamipretide confirmatory-trial enrolment start.** Required by March 2026; sponsor said H1 2026; NCT07531251 registered with a September 2026 update. Actual enrolment start and any FDA action **not confirmed**.
8. **Publication citations stated by sponsors but not confirmed here:** SYNCHRONIZE-1 in NEJM, BELIEVE in Nature Medicine, ZUPREME-1 in Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology (DOI not captured).
9. **Estimand confusion as a systematic problem.** CagriSema's "22.7%" (full-adherence) vs "20.4%" (treatment policy), and orforglipron's "12.4%" vs "11.2%", are the same trials read two ways. Most secondary and all grey-market coverage quotes the larger number without the caveat. This is the most common single error in this literature.

## Genuine scientific open questions

**Obesity pharmacology**
- Is there a real efficacy ceiling, or does it keep rising? MariTide had **not plateaued at 52 weeks**; a retatrutide extension suggests continued loss past 80 weeks. Nobody knows where this stops, or whether 30%+ loss is desirable rather than merely achievable.
- **Why does every agent lose roughly a third of its effect in type 2 diabetes?** (CagriSema 20.4% → 13.7%; survodutide 16.6% → 13.1%; MariTide 12–16% → 8–12%.) Mechanism unexplained.
- Is the **amylin tolerability advantage** real? Petrelintide's lower-than-placebo dropout rate is the most interesting tolerability result in years, and is phase 2 only.
- Is **GIP agonism or GIP antagonism** correct? Tirzepatide and retatrutide agonise; MariTide antagonises. Both produce weight loss. Mechanistically incoherent and unresolved.
- Why did **CagriSema fail non-inferiority vs tirzepatide** (REDEFINE 4) when its placebo-controlled numbers looked competitive? Head-to-head trials keep deflating cross-trial comparisons.
- **Oral vs injectable**: orforglipron's approval changes access economics more than efficacy. Does lower efficacy with far higher uptake produce more population benefit? Unstudied.

**Beyond weight**
- Is any non-weight benefit **weight-independent**? Unresolved for OSA, explicitly. If all benefits are weight-mediated, the class is one drug with many labels.
- Does **MASH histology improvement translate to decompensation, transplant or death**? ESSENCE is an accelerated approval on a surrogate; LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis will not report until ~2029.
- Is there **primary** cardiovascular prevention benefit? Never tested. SELECT was secondary prevention.
- **Why did EVOKE fail so completely** despite biomarker movement? Limited CNS penetration is a hypothesis, not a finding. This also raises whether peripheral metabolic improvement can move CNS endpoints at all.
- **Addiction**: do GLP-1 agonists reduce consumption, or reduce *reward* generally? The AUD trials reduced heavy drinking days and cannabis-use days while missing craving and drinks/day — a confusing pattern that may indicate a consumption-volume effect rather than an anti-craving effect. No phase 3 exists; opioid use disorder has **no** human efficacy data.

**Muscle**
- **Does preserving mass preserve function?** The whole field's validity rests on this and **no trial has tested it**. Myostatin/activin blockade has repeatedly increased muscle mass and failed on functional endpoints in sarcopenia and cachexia.
- Is co-therapy better than **protein plus resistance training**? Never compared head-to-head. The cheap intervention remains untested against the expensive one.
- Who actually needs it? If risk concentrates in older adults and pre-existing sarcopenia, universal co-therapy is unjustified — but no risk-stratification tool exists.
- Post-discontinuation **body-composition ratcheting** (fat returns, lean mass may not) is biologically plausible, barely studied, and would be the strongest argument for muscle-sparing agents if confirmed.

**Psychedelics and neuro**
- **Is the trip necessary?** The non-hallucinogenic programmes assume no; clinical dose–response data and a 2026 DMT programme suggest yes. No non-hallucinogenic analogue has published controlled efficacy data. This is the field's single highest-stakes question.
- **Can psychedelic trials be blinded at all?** Functional unblinding is unavoidable and was part of FDA's MDMA critique. Whether a regulator will ever accept the design is unresolved.
- How much of the effect is the **psychological support and monitored session** rather than the drug? Both arms get support; the drug effect is on top of it — but the whole package is what was approved-for-review, and none of it exists in unsupervised use.
- Why have COMP005/COMP006 **remission rates** not been disclosed?

**Mitochondrial and repair**
- Will the elamipretide confirmatory trial succeed, given the original randomised phase missed its primaries and larger trials in other indications failed? Accelerated approval could be withdrawn.
- Does the recurring **biomarker-improves/function-doesn't** pattern in mitochondrial therapeutics reflect wrong targets, wrong endpoints, or wrong patients?
- For the entire "repair peptide" category: why, after a decade of grey-market use, is there still **no completed published RCT** for BPC-157? Absence of trials despite enormous commercial incentive is itself informative.
