# Mitochondria-targeted and tissue-repair agents (L1)

Part of [[clinical-pipeline/index]]. Related: [[ss-31/index]], [[clinical-pipeline/open-questions]], [[clinical-pipeline/risks]].

This is the part of the pipeline the "peptide longevity" market tracks most closely — and where the gap between a real approval and the claims attached to it is widest.

---

## Elamipretide / SS-31 — the post-approval trajectory

See [[ss-31/index]] for the compound-level note. What matters here is what happened *after* approval.

- **FDA accelerated approval 19 September 2025** as **Forzinity** (elamipretide HCl), to improve muscle strength in adults and children with **Barth syndrome** weighing ≥30 kg. First approved therapy for Barth syndrome; first approved mitochondria-targeted drug. Followed a Complete Response Letter in May 2025. FDA integrated review: application **215244Orig1s000**.
- **The approval rests on an intermediate/surrogate endpoint** (knee-extensor strength) in a **12-patient** crossover trial plus open-label extension. The randomised portion **did not beat placebo on its primary endpoints**. Continued approval is **conditional on confirming clinical benefit**.
- **Confirmatory trial requirement — the notable regulatory fact.** FDA approved the drug **before the required confirmatory study was under way**, giving the sponsor until **March 2026** to begin enrolling. This was reported as an example of FDA flexibility on confirmatory-trial status under accelerated approval, and it is a meaningful precedent: the evidentiary gap is held open rather than closed at approval.
- The postmarketing trial is registered as a **phase 3b/4 randomised double-blind placebo-controlled** study (**SPIBA-401 / "4TAZPower"**, **NCT07531251**), sponsored by Stealth BioTherapeutics, with the explicit aim of confirming the efficacy behind the accelerated approval. Registry record last updated September 2026. The sponsor stated it was on track to start in H1 2026 `[sponsor]`; **I could not confirm the actual enrolment start date or any FDA action since.** Marked **unverified**.
- **What this does and does not license.** Forzinity is approved for **one ultra-rare monogenic disease**, on a **strength surrogate**, in a tiny cohort. Prior larger elamipretide trials in primary mitochondrial myopathy (MMPOWER-3), STEMI (EMBRACE) and geographic atrophy (ReCLAIM-2) **missed their primary endpoints** — see [[ss-31/human-evidence]]. There is **no evidence** supporting use in healthy people for "energy", fatigue, longevity or athletic recovery, and the one approved indication provides none.

## Other mitochondria-targeted agents in registered trials

- **Sonlicromanol** (Khondrion) — most advanced non-elamipretide programme. **KHENERFIN**, pivotal **phase 3** in adults with genetically confirmed **m.3243A>G primary mitochondrial disease**, **NCT06451757**, recruiting from **February 2026** after FDA IND clearance; 52 weeks. Enrolment targets differ between sources (~150 vs ~220) — **unverified**. Earlier phase 2 work registered as NCT04378075 (identity of that record **not confirmed** here). **Not approved for commercial use.**
- **Vatiquinone** (PTC Therapeutics) — mixed. The **MIT-E** trial in refractory epilepsy of mitochondrial origin is **terminated** (sponsor decision). For **Friedreich ataxia**, an **open-label** phase 3 (**PROVE-FA**) is planned to support a future NDA resubmission, enrolment from Q3 2026 `[sponsor]` — an open-label design to support resubmission is itself a signal about the strength of the prior data.
- **SBT-272** and **MA-5** — no clinical trial information located. Status unknown; **do not treat as "in trials"** on the basis of preclinical literature alone.
- The broader m.3243A>G / MELAS therapeutic landscape is reviewed in PMC13504546.

**Pattern worth recording:** across the whole mitochondrial-therapeutics field, the recurring failure mode is that bioenergetic/biomarker improvements do not translate into functional endpoints (6-minute walk, fatigue scales). That is the same failure mode EVOKE showed for GLP-1 in Alzheimer's (see [[clinical-pipeline/glp1-beyond-weight]]). It is the single most useful prior to apply to any "mitochondrial support" claim.

## Tissue-repair peptides: where BPC-157-type claims actually stand

BPC-157 ("pentadecapeptide BPC 157", a partial sequence attributed to human gastric juice protein) is the archetypal grey-market "healing peptide". Against registered trials:

- **BPC-157 is not approved by FDA or EMA for any indication.**
- **No randomised controlled trial of BPC-157 with published results was located.** A phase 1 trial in healthy volunteers is described in the 2025 review literature as having **never had results submitted**.
- Total published human exposure is very small — one count puts it at **five human studies, ~116 participants in total**, attributed to an FDA July 2026 briefing document; another notes FDA identified **one clinical trial** across the entire orthopaedic literature. These figures come from secondary/commentary sources and are **unverified** against the FDA document itself.
- A **phase 2 randomised trial** said to be recruiting from February 2026 (NCT number circulating in vendor-run blogs) **could not be confirmed against the registry**, and other 2026 sources state **no BPC-157 trials were actively recruiting**. **Treat the existence of an active controlled trial as unverified.** I am deliberately not reproducing the unconfirmed NCT number.
- **Compounding status:** BPC-157 was placed in **Category 2** of FDA's 503A bulk drug substances list in **2023**, which restricted pharmacy compounding. FDA staff subsequently **recommended against** adding it to the 503A bulks list; the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee is reported to have voted **8–6 in favour** of adding it on **23 July 2026** — advisory only and **not binding on FDA**. Claims circulating since October 2026 that it "can now be compounded under enforcement discretion" are **unverified**; changing the list requires rulemaking with notice and comment. Enforcement discretion, if it exists, is **not an approval**.
- Essentially the entire BPC-157 literature available to a web search is **vendor- or advocacy-published**. During this research, searches for BPC-157 trial evidence returned almost exclusively consumer peptide-retail and affiliate pages. Recorded as a market signal: the volume of "evidence review" content vastly exceeds the volume of evidence. No vendor or sourcing details are recorded here.

**The asymmetry to note:** elamipretide — one approved mitochondria-targeted peptide — needed a registered rare-disease programme, a CRL, an accelerated approval on a surrogate and a mandated confirmatory trial. BPC-157, marketed far more widely, has **not completed a single published RCT**. The grey market does not distinguish between the two, and the SS-31 approval is now routinely used to lend credibility to the whole "repair peptide" category. It does not transfer.
