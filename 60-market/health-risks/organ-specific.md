# Organ-specific harms

Up: [[health-risks/index]]

Liver, kidney, brain, eye and skin. Grouped by organ because that is how presentations arrive, and because for several of these the key fact is that **the published evidence is thinner than the online consensus assumes** in either direction.

## Liver

### Prodrug eugeroics and transaminase elevation

**The mechanism.** [[adrafinil]] is a prodrug hydrolysed in the liver to modafinil plus a diphenylmethyl-sulphinyl-acetic acid by-product. The concern is that achieving a given modafinil exposure via adrafinil requires the liver to process substantially more material, and the historical French product information for adrafinil carried liver-enzyme monitoring advice. [[fladrafinil]] and [[flmodafinil]] are bis-fluorinated analogues with **no human hepatic safety data at all**.

**What the primary evidence actually says — and does not say.**
- **Modafinil/armodafinil: reassuring in large prospective cohorts.** In the US DILIN prospective drug-induced liver injury study, among **899 enrolled DILI cases (2004–2013), none were attributed to modafinil or armodafinil** (one to methylphenidate). In the US Acute Liver Failure Study Group cohort, among **1,198 acute liver failure patients (1998–2007), 133 were attributed to DILI — none to modafinil**. ([LiverTox, NCBI Bookshelf NBK548702](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548702/))
- LiverTox-derived summaries state modafinil can cause minor liver-enzyme increases but is **not implicated in clinically significant acute liver injury when used as prescribed**.
- **Adrafinil: no primary quantification located.** Claims that adrafinil "elevates liver enzymes and requires periodic liver function monitoring with prolonged use" appear **only on supplement, nootropic and consumer sites** in this research. **Mark as unverified.** No LiverTox adrafinil entry, case report or cohort quantifying transaminase elevation rates for adrafinil was retrieved. The mechanistic argument (prodrug hydrolysis load) is reasonable; the clinical magnitude is unmeasured.
- **Honest conclusion:** the hepatic concern about adrafinil and its fluorinated analogues is **mechanistic and precautionary**, resting on prodrug pharmacology and the absence of data, **not** on a documented case series. It should be stated that way rather than inflated.

### Herbal and NPS hepatotoxicity

- Regulators have begun flagging liver injury as an emergent signal in this market. A joint **TGA and Chief Medical Officer statement** on unapproved peptides advises clinicians to watch for **abnormal liver function tests or signs of acute liver injury** ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/node/575382) · [Medical Republic summary](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/emerging-side-effects-of-unapproved-peptides/126627)). That is a regulator-level signal from spontaneous reports, not a characterised syndrome with an incidence.
- The broader NPS/herbal hepatotoxicity literature (DILI from botanical and multi-ingredient products, where the implicated ingredient is frequently undeclared) is the right comparison class: the recurring finding there is that **the hepatotoxin is often not the labelled ingredient**. Given the identity-failure rate documented in [[health-risks/injection-and-contamination]], the same logic applies here.
- **Unverified:** no systematic review of hepatotoxicity specific to grey-market research chemicals or peptides was located.

**Practical consequence.** A person with new fatigue, nausea, right-upper-quadrant pain, dark urine or jaundice after starting an unregulated product needs liver function tests and a clinician. Jaundice is a reason to seek care now, not to wait.

## Kidney

### Rhabdomyolysis from stimulants

**Mechanism.** Sympathomimetic stimulants produce rhabdomyolysis by several converging routes: direct myocyte toxicity, sustained muscle hyperactivity (agitation, seizures, dystonia), hyperthermia, and muscle ischaemia from vasoconstriction. Myoglobin released from lysed myocytes causes acute kidney injury through tubular obstruction by myoglobin casts, direct tubular toxicity of ferrihaemate, and renal vasoconstriction — compounded by the volume depletion that accompanies hyperthermia and sympathomimetic use.

**The triad to recognise:** hyperthermia + agitation + muscle rigidity, followed by dark/tea-coloured urine and falling urine output. Severe hyperkalaemia from muscle breakdown is the proximate arrhythmic threat, and hyperthermia itself is independently lethal. This is an emergency presentation — see [[health-risks/emergency-recognition]].

**Evidence state.** The synthetic-cathinone toxicology literature reports hyperthermia and myocardial injury prominently (see [[health-risks/cardiometabolic]]), and the bromazolam cluster below documented hyperthermia with myocardial injury. **However: no cathinone-specific rhabdomyolysis case series was located in this research.** The rhabdomyolysis-AKI mechanism is very well established for sympathomimetics as a class (the classic literature is on amphetamines, MDMA and cocaine); its attribution to specific cathinones here is **by class inference**, and should be marked as such.

### Other nephrotoxicity

- **Volume depletion.** The 2024 FDA compounded-semaglutide alert reported **dehydration** among adverse events after 5–20× overdoses, alongside protracted vomiting ([FDA alert via Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20240909/fda-warns-of-adverse-events-due-to-overdosing-of-compounded-semaglutide)). Severe GI losses from incretin overdose produce pre-renal AKI by an entirely ordinary mechanism; the long half-life means the losses are prolonged.
- **Nephrotoxic contaminants.** Elemental impurities and residual process impurities are not tested for in grey-market material ([[health-risks/injection-and-contamination]]). **This is a testing gap, not a documented nephrotoxicity finding** — no case of grey-market-peptide heavy-metal nephropathy was located.
- **Synthetic cannabinoid AKI** is the one well-documented NPS-specific nephrotoxicity in the public-health literature (clusters of unexplained AKI in young users), included here as a class precedent. It is outside this repo's compound set.

## Neurological

### Seizures from designer benzodiazepine withdrawal

**Mechanism.** Benzodiazepine-site agonists produce GABA-A receptor adaptation with chronic exposure — reduced receptor sensitivity and subunit reconfiguration. Removing the agonist leaves a hypo-GABAergic, net-excitatory state: tremor, insomnia, autonomic arousal, psychosis and, at the severe end, **generalised seizures and status epilepticus**, which can be fatal. This is the same syndrome as classical benzodiazepine withdrawal, and it is **one of only two withdrawal syndromes that routinely kill** (the other being alcohol, by the same GABA mechanism).

**Two features make the designer-benzodiazepine version worse:**
1. **Unknown potency and unknown half-life.** [[bromazolam]], [[clonazolam]] and relatives are frequently sold as, or mistaken for, alprazolam. Someone may have been taking a far higher benzodiazepine-equivalent exposure than they believe, for longer, producing a more severe dependence than they expect.
2. **Unpredictable supply interruption.** Dependence formed on a product with no reliable supply means withdrawal is typically **abrupt and unplanned** rather than managed.

**Documented case evidence.** CDC MMWR, Chicago, February 2023 — a cluster of **three young adults** treated at emergency departments for **hyperthermia, seizures and myocardial injury** after taking **bromazolam sold as alprazolam**. One progressed to **status epilepticus despite lorazepam, propofol, levetiracetam and valproic acid, and to persistent coma**. ([MMWR 72(52–53)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm725253a5.htm) · [PDF](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/pdfs/mm725253a5-H.pdf))
- **Read the authors' own caveat carefully.** They state this constellation was **unexpected for a benzodiazepine overdose** and could reflect anoxic brain injury, additional bromazolam features in overdose **or withdrawal**, or an unmeasured intoxicant. They also note animal models suggest bromazolam acts predominantly as a sedative, and that **to date no signal for hyperthermia, myocardial injury or seizures attributable to bromazolam intoxication exists**. So: this cluster **does not establish** that bromazolam intoxication causes seizures, and it does not establish withdrawal as the cause either.
- The report notes none of the patients received **flumazenil**, and that flumazenil can precipitate benzodiazepine withdrawal and cause seizures or tachyarrhythmias — relevant because it means a reversal agent is *not* a safe default here.
- **Gap:** no etizolam-specific case reports and no withdrawal-specific seizure series for designer benzodiazepines were located. The withdrawal-seizure risk is established for the benzodiazepine class and inferred for these analogues. Mark as such.

### Seizures from stimulants

Stimulant-induced seizures arise from direct lowering of seizure threshold by monoaminergic excess, compounded by hyperthermia, hyponatraemia (where excess water intake is involved) and intracranial haemorrhage from acute hypertension. In the cathinone poison-centre and literature data, seizures appear alongside the cardiac events described in [[health-risks/cardiometabolic]]. Polydrug exposure raises risk further — see [[polydrug-risk]].

### Other neurological

- **PRES (posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome)** was a reported complication of medetomidine withdrawal in the Philadelphia MMWR series — a hypertensive-emergency mechanism producing headache, visual disturbance, seizures and altered consciousness ([MMWR 74(15):266–268](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7415a2.htm); **note CDC erratum**).
- **Anoxic brain injury** is the common final pathway for all the opioid and sedative presentations — the reason time-to-ventilation matters more than time-to-antidote ([[health-risks/emergency-recognition]]).

## Ocular: the semaglutide NAION safety signal

This is the best worked example in the whole area of a safety signal moving from case series → regulatory review → label change, with the manufacturer disagreeing. Worth understanding as a template.

**NAION** (non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy) is sudden, usually painless, usually unilateral optic nerve infarction causing permanent visual field loss. There is no established effective treatment.

**Timeline:**
- **Jul 2024** — Mass Eye and Ear researchers published in *JAMA Ophthalmology*: among **194 patients with diabetes treated with semaglutide, 17 developed NAION within 36 months — an incidence of 8.9% vs 1.8%** in patients on other glucose-lowering drugs. A retrospective single-centre study in a neuro-ophthalmology referral population, so **severe selection bias toward the outcome**; the absolute risk in a general population is certainly far lower. ([summary, Deutsches Ärzteblatt](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/nachrichten/156981/EMA-prueft-Risiko-von-Semaglutid-auf-seltene-Augenerkrankung))
- **Jan 2025** — EMA's **PRAC began a formal review**, triggered by two observational studies ([Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/eye-disorder-is-a-very-rare-side-effect-of-weight-loss-drugs-concludes-ema-review)).
- **Jun 2025** — **PRAC concluded NAION is a "very rare" side effect of semaglutide, affecting up to 1 in 10,000 treated people.** The review covered non-clinical studies, clinical trials, post-marketing surveillance and published literature. The epidemiological finding: adults with type 2 diabetes on semaglutide had approximately **two-fold increased risk** of NAION vs those not on the drug. PRAC recommended **label updates**, advised patients with sudden vision loss or rapidly worsening eyesight to contact a doctor **without delay**, and advised that **if NAION is confirmed, semaglutide should be stopped**. ([Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/eye-disorder-is-a-very-rare-side-effect-of-weight-loss-drugs-concludes-ema-review) · [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/eye-condition-very-rare-side-effect-semaglutide-ema-says))
- **Manufacturer position:** Novo Nordisk stated that on the totality of evidence the data did not suggest a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship, and that the benefit-risk profile remains favourable. **This is an open disagreement with the regulator's conclusion and should be reported as such.**

**Regulatory status, stated precisely:** a **label change in the EU, classified "very rare" (≤1 in 10,000)**. It is **not** a withdrawal, not a contraindication, and not a boxed warning. The two-fold relative risk sits on a very low baseline incidence.

**A frequently confused separate finding:** a University of Toronto study in *JAMA Ophthalmology* linked semaglutide to **neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD)** in diabetic users — a **different condition** from NAION ([Benzinga summary](https://benzinga.com/z/45814068)). Do not merge the two signals.

**Why this matters for the grey market.** The PRAC conclusion is about **approved semaglutide at approved doses with prescriber oversight and a label instructing people what to do about sudden vision loss**. Grey-market copies ([[semaglutide]], and by extension unstudied multi-agonists [[retatrutide]], [[tirzepatide]] copies) deliver the same pharmacology with no label, no dose standardisation (see the 5–20× overdose finding), and nobody to tell the user that sudden vision change means stop and seek care today. Whether the class effect extends to other incretin agonists is **not established**.

## Dermatological: melanocortin agonists

### Hyperpigmentation

`risk:hyperpigmentation`. MC1R agonism on melanocytes drives eumelanin synthesis — this is the intended effect of Melanotan II and an unavoidable off-target effect of any non-selective melanocortin agonist. The repo's existing finding records a **38% hyperpigmentation rate** for [[pt-141]] ([[pt-141/risks]]). The approved bremelanotide label notes **focal hyperpigmentation was not confirmed to resolve in all patients after discontinuation** — i.e. it may be permanent ([label](https://medlibrary.org/lib/rx/meds/vyleesi/page/4/)). Case reports describe hyperpigmentation around the injection site with no other localising findings.

### Naevi and the melanoma concern

**What is documented:**
- **Eruptive dysplastic naevi.** A case of a 25-year-old man with multiple new naevi and rapid change in existing naevi within weeks of a 4-week course of subcutaneous Melanotan II; histopathology showed dysplastic naevi, with **severe dysplasia in three** ([Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas](https://actasdermo.org/en-eruptive-dysplastic-nevi-following-melanotan-articulo-S1578219012001357)). A further case of a 42-year-old man with eruptive naevi who obtained the drug through a gym contact.
- **Melanoma.** A report of transformation of a pre-existing abdominal naevus in a 42-year-old woman **3 months after** Melanotan injections. A review notes **four case reports** of melanomas arising in pre-existing moles during or shortly after melanotan use.
- **Mechanistic plausibility:** MC1R agonism stimulates melanocytes, and melanocyte proliferation is the relevant cell lineage. That makes the concern biologically coherent.

**What is not documented — state this plainly:**
- **Causality is unproven.** The authors of the case series explicitly **could not prove and did not suggest** a causal relationship with melanoma.
- **Major confounding:** most Melanotan II users also use UV tanning beds, which are a *proven* melanoma risk factor. This confounds every case report.
- A Dutch dermatology review concludes there is **no conclusive evidence** of a link between Melanotan II and dysplastic naevi, while the concern remains ([NVDV](https://nvdv.nl/storage/app/media/dermatologische-risicogcos-bij-ongereguleerd-gebruik-van-alfa-melanocyt-stimulerend-hormoon-m-mertens-ntvdv-2020-09.pdf)).
- A 2026 secondary summary cites a melanoma in situ diagnosed four weeks after beginning MT-II from a regulated compounding pharmacy; **not verified against a primary source.**
- Commercial peptide and supplement sites making safety claims in either direction are **not reliable** for this question.

**The defensible statement:** case reports associate Melanotan II with eruptive dysplastic naevi and, less often, with melanoma arising in existing naevi; causality is unestablished and heavily confounded by concurrent UV exposure; the mechanism is plausible; the practical consequence is that **new or changing pigmented lesions warrant dermatological assessment**, which is standard advice regardless of cause.
