# Health risks (cross-cutting)

The health harms that cut across every compound class in this knowledge base — the ones that come from the **market and the route** rather than from any single molecule's pharmacology.

## Summary

Reading the clinical, toxicology and public-health literature on grey-market research chemicals and peptides together produces one dominant finding: **the largest documented acute harms are not pharmacological.** They come from what is in the vial, what it says on the label, how much is in each millilitre, and the absence of anyone monitoring. The compounds with the most alarming theoretical profiles (telomerase activators, angiogenic peptides) have no demonstrated human harm; the compounds with mundane pharmacology have put people in intensive care because the concentration was non-standard or the vial was pyrogenic.

The second finding is structural: **no system exists that could detect a delayed harm in this population.** There is no denominator, no exposure registry and no outcome ascertainment. So "no reported harm" carries no evidential weight — a conclusion that cuts against both marketers and alarmists.

The third finding is that **withdrawal can be the emergency**. Both severe designer-benzodiazepine withdrawal and medetomidine withdrawal are potentially fatal syndromes caused by stopping, and the second was being missed by clinicians until 2025 because nobody knew to look for it.

## What changed in 2024–26

- **Apr 2026** — first substantial dataset on grey-market peptide quality: **41.6–71.1% quality failure, ~15% measurable endotoxin, purity uncorrelated with endotoxin**. Preprint-grade, but it is the first number anyone has.
- **2026** — regulators moved from silence to explicit warnings. The **TGA** named products and reported SIRS, severe allergic reactions and a liver-injury signal, and documented a product sold as retatrutide that contained undeclared semaglutide and no retatrutide. **FDA** issued warning letters (24 Aug 2026) holding that "research use only" disclaimers do not protect sellers whose sites carry dosing instructions and dose calculators.
- **2026** — simultaneously, **PCAC recommended six of seven peptides for the 503A compounding list** (23–24 Jul), narrowly and against FDA staff advice. Non-binding, no rulemaking started, and **no new safety data**. The regulatory direction of travel is genuinely two-way.
- **May 2025** — **medetomidine** became a characterised clinical entity: three MMWR reports in one issue describing naloxone-resistant bradycardic overdose (Chicago, 12 confirmed/26 probable/140 suspected) and a life-threatening **withdrawal** syndrome (Philadelphia, 165 hospitalised, with PRES and NSTEMI; Pittsburgh, 23 patients).
- **Jun 2025** — **EMA PRAC concluded NAION is a "very rare" side effect of semaglutide** (up to 1 in 10,000, ~2-fold risk), changed the label, and was publicly contradicted by the manufacturer. The clearest worked example in this area of how a real safety signal gets detected — and of the machinery grey-market use lacks entirely.
- **Jul 2024** — **FDA alert on compounded semaglutide**: patients drawing **5–20× the intended dose**, with hospitalisations, pancreatitis and gallstones. Harm by arithmetic, not toxicology.

## Scope and rules observed

- No dosing protocols, no self-treatment or self-management regimens, no vendors or sourcing, no synthesis.
- Clinical, toxicology, forensic, regulatory and public-health literature only.
- Every claim carries its evidence grade. Preprints are labelled as preprints, animal and in vitro findings as such, and mechanistic arguments as theoretical.
- **Unverified claims are marked in place and collected in [[health-risks/open-questions]] §A.** This includes several widely circulated figures that could not be confirmed against a primary source.
- **Access limitation:** `cdc.gov` and `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` did not resolve from the session that compiled this area. MMWR and PMC sources were characterised from search results and cross-checked secondary summaries, not read in full. Citation details (volume, pages, doi) were corroborated across independent sources, but direct full-text verification has not been done.
