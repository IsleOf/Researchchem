# Cancer and long-term risk

Up: [[health-risks/index]]

> **Reading instruction.** This note separates three distinct epistemic categories and labels every claim with one of them. Conflating them is the single most common error in writing about this area — in both directions, by marketers and by critics.
>
> - **[DEMONSTRATED]** — observed in humans, with the study design stated.
> - **[OBSERVED-NONHUMAN]** — observed in animals or in cell culture.
> - **[THEORETICAL]** — a mechanistic argument with no supporting outcome data. Biologically coherent ≠ shown to happen.

## IGF-1 elevation and cancer epidemiology

This is the most substantive item here, and it needs its inferential chain spelled out, because each link is weaker than the last.

### Link 1: endogenous IGF-1 concentration is associated with cancer risk — **[DEMONSTRATED]**

| Cancer | Study | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Breast | Pooled analysis of **17 prospective studies**, *Lancet Oncology* 2010 ([PMID 20472501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20472501/)) | Top vs bottom fifth of circulating IGF-1: **OR 1.28 (95% CI 1.14–1.44, p<0.0001)**. Confined to **oestrogen-receptor-positive** tumours: OR **1.38** ER-positive vs **0.80** ER-negative (p heterogeneity = 0.007). Not altered by adjusting for IGFBP-3. |
| Prostate | Pooled analysis of **17 prospective + 2 cross-sectional studies**, *Cancer Research* 2016 ([PMID 26921328](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26921328/)) | Highest vs lowest fifth of IGF-I among prospective studies: **OR 1.29 (95% CI 1.16–1.43)**. Authors describe this as "strong evidence that IGF-I is highly likely to be involved in prostate cancer development". |
| Breast (earlier) | Nested case-control, Nurses' Health Study, 1998 ([PMID 9593409](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9593409/)) | **No overall association**; positive association in premenopausal but not postmenopausal women. Small; superseded by the pooled analysis. |

**What this establishes.** A real, replicated, modest association between **naturally varying endogenous** IGF-1 and incident breast and prostate cancer. Effect size: roughly a **28–29% relative increase** comparing the top to the bottom quintile of the normal range.

**What it does not establish.** Causality from the observational data alone (although the prostate authors' language is strong and Mendelian-randomisation work in this field generally supports a causal direction — **not verified in this research**); any effect of *pharmacologically* raising IGF-1; and any threshold or dose-response above the physiological range.

**No pooled analysis of IGF-1 across multiple cancer types was located.** The *Lancet Oncology* paper is breast-specific. Do not cite it as a general cancer finding.

### Link 2: GH secretagogues raise IGF-1 — **[DEMONSTRATED]**

Raising IGF-1 is the stated purpose of the GH-secretagogue class ([[ipamorelin]], [[cjc-1295]], MK-677). The 2-year MK-677 trial confirms axis activation with measurable downstream effects ([PMID 18981485](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18981485/)). This link is not in dispute.

### Link 3: therefore secretagogue use increases cancer risk — **[THEORETICAL]**

This is the load-bearing gap. Why it does not follow automatically:
- The epidemiology measures **variation within the physiological range** in untreated people. Pharmacological elevation is a different exposure.
- Observational IGF-1 may be a **marker** of something else causal (nutritional status, growth history, insulin/IGFBP milieu) as much as a cause.
- A 28% relative increase over a quintile of normal variation, applied to the absolute lifetime incidence of a specific cancer, is a small absolute effect even if causal.
- **No cohort, trial or registry study of cancer incidence in people using grey-market GH secretagogues exists.** The exposure is unrecorded by design.
- Counter-direction consideration: acromegaly — sustained pathological GH/IGF-1 excess — is associated with increased colorectal neoplasia in the clinical literature, which is the strongest argument that *sufficiently large and sustained* elevation matters. Whether grey-market exposure reaches that magnitude or duration is **unknown**.

**Honest formulation for `risk:igf1-cancer-theoretical`:** *endogenous IGF-1 concentration is robustly associated with breast (ER-positive) and prostate cancer risk in large pooled prospective analyses; agents that deliberately raise IGF-1 therefore carry a mechanistically coherent but entirely unquantified cancer concern, with no human outcome data in this population.* That sentence is defensible. Anything stronger in either direction is not.

**Population most exposed to the unknown:** anyone with a current or past hormone-sensitive malignancy, a known predisposition syndrome, or an undiagnosed occult tumour — because the concern, if real, is **promotion of existing neoplasia** rather than initiation.

## Angiogenic peptides and tumour promotion

**[THEORETICAL]** — and that label is doing real work here.

**The mechanism is well characterised.** A peer-reviewed narrative review describes BPC-157 activating both a **VEGF-dependent pathway (VEGFR2–PI3K–Akt–eNOS)** and a **VEGF-independent pathway (Src–caveolin-1–eNOS)** converging on nitric oxide production and supporting angiogenesis ([PMC12446177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12446177/)). The evidence for this is **[OBSERVED-NONHUMAN]** — largely animal models. Related repair/angiogenic peptides in this space include TB-500/thymosin β4 and GHK-Cu.

**The concern.** Solid tumours beyond ~1–2 mm require neovascularisation to grow and metastasise. The existence of an entire licensed anti-VEGF oncology drug class is direct proof that this pathway is causally important in **human** tumour biology. A systemically administered pro-angiogenic agent therefore raises the question of whether it could support growth of an existing, possibly subclinical, tumour.

**What the evidence does and does not show:**
- **[THEORETICAL]** Tumour promotion. The reviewed literature states that **no current evidence shows BPC-157 causing cancer in humans or initiating tumours in animals**. The concern is explicitly framed in the sources as speculative and not adequately studied in humans.
- **[DEMONSTRATED]** That the pathway matters in human cancer — via the anti-VEGF drug class.
- The distinction that matters: the concern is about **promotion of existing disease**, not initiation. Initiation and promotion have different evidentiary requirements, and a negative carcinogenicity finding in healthy animals would not address promotion.
- **No carcinogenicity study, long-term animal study or human cohort of BPC-157 or other angiogenic grey-market peptides was located.** FDA's placement of BPC-157 in Category 2 of the 503A bulks review (Sep 2023 per the PMC review; secondary sources variously say Sep or Nov 2023) cited **safety, impurities and insufficient human data** — i.e. absence of evidence, which is exactly the problem.
- See the regulatory movement in [[health-risks/timeline]]: the July 2026 PCAC vote recommended several of these peptides for the 503A list, which changes their *legal* status pathway without adding any long-term safety data.

## Telomerase and ALT findings

**[OBSERVED-NONHUMAN]**, with one specific finding that cuts against the marketing.

- [[epitalon]] is marketed as a "telomerase activator". Telomere and telomerase findings are **in vitro** ([[epitalon/mechanism]]).
- **The key finding:** Al-Dulaimi 2025 — an independent (non-Khavinson-group) replication — found Epitalon **lengthened telomeres in breast-cancer cell lines via the ALT (alternative lengthening of telomeres) pathway** ([doi:10.1007/s10522-025-10315-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10522-025-10315-x)). Lengthening telomeres in cancer cells is the opposite of a desirable property.
- **Why this is a coherent concern:** telomerase reactivation is a hallmark of most human cancers — unlimited replicative potential requires telomere maintenance. An agent that provides it is, in principle, supplying a capability that transformed cells need.
- **Why it is not a demonstrated harm:** it is cell culture. Some rodent data show **fewer** tumours with Epitalon/Epithalamin (Anisimov 2003), so the in vivo direction is genuinely unknown ([[epitalon/risks]]). Two opposing bodies of non-human evidence is where this sits.
- Related existing node: `risk:theoretical-oncogenic`. Also relevant: [[prostamax]] raised **sister-chromatid exchange**, a classic genotoxic-stress marker, which the developers present as rejuvenation ([[prostamax/risks]]) — **[OBSERVED-NONHUMAN]**, and a finding whose interpretation is contested at the level of what it means, not just how big it is.

## The structural problem: no long-term human safety data

`risk:no-human-safety-data` · `risk:no-long-term-safety-data` · `risk:unknown-long-term`

**[DEMONSTRATED]** — this one is simply a fact about the evidence base, and it is the most important thing in this note.

For essentially every compound in this repo that is not an approved medicine, **all** of the following are absent:
- Long-term (multi-year) human safety follow-up.
- Carcinogenicity studies (2-year rodent bioassays or equivalent).
- Reproductive and developmental toxicology.
- Human pharmacokinetics, in many cases — including the no-DAC CJC-1295 form that is most commonly sold, which has **no published human PK at all** ([[cjc-1295/mechanism]]).
- Drug-interaction studies.
- Pregnancy and lactation data.
- Paediatric data.
- Any pharmacovigilance system capable of detecting a delayed signal.

**Why the last point is decisive.** The semaglutide/NAION example in [[health-risks/organ-specific]] shows what detecting a rare delayed harm actually requires: an approved product, a defined exposed population, a prescription database, a case series, a regulator with the power to compel a review, and a label to change. **Grey-market use has none of these.** A latency-10-year harm affecting 1 in 2,000 users would be undetectable — not unlikely, *undetectable*. There is no denominator, no exposure registry, and no outcome ascertainment.

So the correct inference from "no reported cancers" is **nothing at all**. Absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety when no system exists that could report it. The repo's existing framing in [[epitalon/risks]] — "absence of reported harm ≠ evidence of safety" — is the right one and generalises to the whole area.

**Two further structural points:**
1. **Duration mismatch.** Even the best evidence here is short. The longest controlled trial cited anywhere in this area is the 2-year MK-677 study, whose own authors said it was underpowered for functional endpoints. Cancer latency is measured in decades.
2. **Regulatory status is not safety status.** The repo already records that many 2024 FDA Category-2 removals happened because the **nominations were withdrawn** — they are not safety clearances. The July 2026 PCAC recommendations are likewise **non-binding recommendations requiring notice-and-comment rulemaking**, not safety determinations; FDA staff had recommended against several of them. A compound moving onto a compounding list has not acquired long-term safety data by doing so.
