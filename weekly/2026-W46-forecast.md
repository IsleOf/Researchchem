# 30-day forecast — what scales next

**Scope:** forecasts the spread of **substances already detected**. It does not predict or propose novel structures; that would be NPS design, not monitoring. See [[ecdd-watchlist]] for the regulatory-attention companion to this note.

## Method — the five leading indicators
1. **Recent class-wide ban** → substitution pressure, historically visible within months.
2. **Independent multi-jurisdiction flagging** → the pattern that preceded the nitazene wave going mainstream.
3. **Detected but uncontrolled in a major market** → no legal friction on expansion.
4. **Format innovation** (vapes, counterfeit pharma tablets) → reaches new populations *faster than a new molecule can*, because it bypasses the "who seeks this drug" filter entirely.
5. **Regulatory limbo** → a defined window where a known-harmful substance stays legal.

Plus a sixth, seasonal: **southern-hemisphere festival season (Nov–Feb)** raises AU/NZ drug-checking volume, so expect more *reports* from those services regardless of whether the market changed.

## The detective's caution
Prediction 7 is the important one and the least satisfying. **The strongest signal in this whole picture is an absence.** China banned the orphine class on 1 October. Every prior class ban produced a successor. The successor is therefore very likely already in the supply *right now* — and sitting in some lab's unidentified-peak queue, unnamed, for another two to eight months.

So any forecast claiming to name next month's new molecule is either reporting something already detected, or making it up. The honest output is: **the pressure is measurable, the product is not yet nameable.**
